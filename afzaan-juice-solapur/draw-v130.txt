## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`big-links`):** Large links over the hero: the wordmark top-left and the section names as links at the h3 step in the text face at weight 500, spread across the top of the first screen on the grid columns, the action as a button at the right margin; 96px tall in all. Past the hero the links fold into a Menu text button beside the wordmark, the two fixed top-left on one solid tab of the ground (12px padding, the dealt corner), and the action stays fixed top-right on a tab of its own; Menu opens the same large links full-screen. On a phone: the wordmark and Menu on one tab, the action on the other. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The section names as links at the h3 step spread across the top of the first screen; past the hero they fold into a Menu tab beside the wordmark.
- **Footer (`contact-block`):** A big contact block on the ink: the heading (Visit, Write, Book) at the h2 step on the left half; on the right half the email at the h3 step with a Copy button and a mailto link, and the city with a Directions link (the full visit block stands in the section above); under both, a row of the section links, the legal links and Image credits. About 60-70svh. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** On the ink: the heading at the h2 step on the left half, the email at the h3 step with a Copy button on the right half.
- **Cards (`sticky-stack`):** Cards that stack as you scroll: three to five cards across the content width (a corner of 16px or more), in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so the edge of the card sliding up reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone; each about 70svh tall with its heading at the h2 step, its picture or figure and two lines of text; each sticks 96px below the top as the next slides up over it, the one beneath scaling to 0.96. Native scroll (position: sticky), no pin; reduced motion and phones show them one after another. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Cards about 70svh tall, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each sticking 96px below the top as the next slides over it.
- **Buttons (`wipe-fill`):** A fill that wipes in: fully round, 52px tall, 28px padding, the text face at 16px weight 500; at rest the surface tone with an ink label, on hover the accent wipes in from the left (a scaleX layer behind the label, 300ms) and the label turns the accent's ink. The second action is a text link. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). - **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. ## 1. The idea
For a place, the photograph is the argument. One full-bleed image of where you would be standing fills the first screen, and the design steps back: the name sits whole on the bottom margin like a film title, or small in a corner, and nothing else interrupts the view. The page that follows keeps that restraint: few words, large pictures, long pauses. The designer's work here is editing: choosing the one frame that makes someone want to be there, and then not covering it.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: a single photograph at 100vw by 100svh, focal point kept clear of text. The name is set either very large (14–20vw) whole on the bottom margin, no letter cut by any edge, or small (20–28px) top-left with a one-line description bottom-left. Navigation is two to four words in the top corners in the same small size. If text sits on the image, a flat dark scrim under the text area only. Below, sections alternate between a short centred or left-set paragraph in a narrow column (max 52ch) with wide empty margins, and photographs shown large: one full-bleed, then a pair at different sizes, then one portrait offset to the right. Practical information (hours, address, rates, booking) is gathered once near the end in a plain two-column block.

## 3. Type — Gantari, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.333 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5.8vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Gantari does every job, told apart by size and weight (500 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Gantari:wght@400;500;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Gantari** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.026em, leading 0.97
- Text: **Gantari** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.420rem, 0.852rem + 0.89vw, 1.775rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.775rem, 1.065rem + 1.32vw, 2.369rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.192rem, 1.315rem + 3.60vw, 5.219rem);
}
```
A face with some grace for the name; small, calm, well-spaced text everywhere else. Sentence case. Body 17–19px with leading around 1.55.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-bold-tight-grotesk-large-scale`, Bold tight grotesque at large scale): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one grotesque or neo-grotesque at 400 and 700; a second plain sans at 500 for small text is optional. Display in 700 at 9.7-11.7vw (140-168px at 1440), tracking -0.03 to -0.05em, leading 0.78-0.85, on a ladder of about 128 / 94 / 57 / 33px; body 400 at 17px, tracking -0.02em, leading 1.4; small text 14-15px at 500 in sentence case. Ratio 8-9x. The leading is so tight that lines nearly touch: check that descenders (g, y, p) clear the ascenders of the next line and open the leading to 0.85 where they collide. Keep headlines to one or two lines of four words or fewer. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at the strongest contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card fill. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; hover on links changes colour only. House rules for it: No condensed accent word and no second small voice: small text is the body sans at 14-15px, sentence case; the id's 'mono-labels' no longer applies. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

Off-white ground (#faf6ec), dark ink, one rich blue accent. **Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

Without the client's photographs, a dish or drink the menu names stands in type, its name and its price, never by a stock picture of another's dish or cup; the trade's photographs (the cup, the pour, the beans, the street) only set the mood, and its room, its counter and its people are never another café's or restaurant's: never a whole room.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**The visit: this is a place people go to, so the page says how to get there** (in the contact section): the street, a real street in the right district of the town the brief names, with no house number (the brief gives none), or for a remote place or a brief that names only a region, the village it stands in (a real one in that place, chosen and marked as sample copy when the brief names none) and the nearest town with the road from it; the district or area; the nearest stop and its line (read from a map in this session or one you are sure of, otherwise the district's main station), or for a remote place the drive from that town with its time, and from the airport or city most visitors come from (from the road distance on a map, or at about 60 km an hour on country roads when no map can be read, rounded up; never a time that makes the place nearer than it is: a Westfjords house once claimed forty minutes from Reykjavik); the opening hours; a "Directions" link to a maps service searching that street, village or road and town; and one contact (the email on the brand's own domain, on `.example` where the name is coined, since the brief gives no phone). Set it as one group, in the card style section 0 deals or as one panel, and only here: the footer gives the city and Directions, not the block again. Never a placeholder street, never a zeroed phone, never the city's name alone (`visit-without-directions`). The report marks the street or the village, the district, the way there and its time, the hours and the email as sample copy for the client to confirm.

**Structures, in order:** intro column, list split, gallery gallery, contact the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

## 8. Motion — Pushed line
One line of very large type, the name or the claim, is pushed sideways by the scroll rather than by a clock: it travels from 12% right to 12% left of its place while it crosses the screen, linear and locked to the scrollbar, and reads whole at the middle of its run. ### Entrance — Focus pull (`focus`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** The first screen comes into focus like a lens finding it. The picture starts soft and a touch large and sharpens over a second; as it does, the headline comes into focus from half strength. Nothing travels, and the sentence and the action are sharp from the first frame. On a phone the picture only settles in scale, with no blur; the headline still comes into focus. Settled by 1.0 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="focus">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). A photo treatment's filter (black-and-white, a tint) goes on the frame, never on the img or video: the focus animates the picture's own filter.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `focus` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=focus`.
- **Below the fold: Headings sharpen.** Chapter headings (data-reveal="heading", at most one per section) clear from 4 px of blur at 45% strength in 700 ms, once, with no travel. Body copy, pictures, cards and lists never move. No other block gets `data-reveal`: no fade-up on every block.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Brackets close in** (`mo-text-bracket-close-in`): A single word or two short words set large as the section's statement (display step, display face, ink, centred), flanked by two bracket glyphs from the same face at 1.2x the word's height. Scrubbed by scroll (from 85% to 50% of the viewport height) the brackets start 300px further out on each side and slide inward until they sit 0.2em from the word; the word itself is always fully visible. After landing everything holds still; a paragraph (lead step, 44 characters) sits 56px under it. Ground as dealt, accent none. Phone: word at the head step, brackets travel 120px. House rules for it: Never a small bracketed label above a heading (an eyebrow): the brackets are typographic marks around the large statement word itself; the page's own face, never a monospace; brackets stay ink, not a styled accent; scroll-linked, so the dealt entrance stands. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.

A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The photographs, at full size, uninterrupted. Empty paper around short text makes the next image land harder.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A weak hero photograph cannot be fixed by typography; pick again. Do not stack a headline, a paragraph and two buttons on the image. Do not use a booking bar that covers the bottom of the view; one small action is enough.

Studied sites that work this way. Take the principle, never the specifics: no names, copy, images or signature assets of theirs.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
