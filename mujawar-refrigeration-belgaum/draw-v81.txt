## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`floating-pill`):** A floating capsule: centred at the top, 16px below the edge, as wide as its content (about 520-640px at 1440), 52-56px tall, fully round, filled with the surface tone and opaque (no blur, no border, no shadow at rest); in it the wordmark, three or four links at 15-16px and the action as the accent fill at its right end. It stays fixed on every screen on its solid fill, and every held heading keeps clear of it. On a phone: the wordmark and a Menu button in a 48px capsule, 12px from the edges. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** An opaque capsule as wide as its content floats 16px below the top edge, centred, with the action as its accent end, on every screen.
- **Footer (`two-halves`):** Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** Two halves meeting on the centre line: the closing line and button on the ink at the left, the links and contact on the surface tone at the right.
- **Cards (`card-offset-panel-pair-wide-image`, Caption over wide image panel: a pattern of the study):** Two filled panels side by side across the content width, each about 560px tall, 32px gap, the dealt corner, 28px padding, both on the surface tone, the second one dropped 96px lower. At the top of each: a title at the sub step and one line at the body step of up to ten words at 65% ink, left-aligned, nothing above the title. Below, a wide image (1.7) with the dealt corner fills the panel width: a staged photograph or a rendered object, never drawn art. As the block crosses the viewport each image drifts 24px inside its clip. The pair arrives with the dealt entrance. Phone: stacked, no offset, 14px gap, image keeps 1.7. House rules for it: The image is a staged photograph or a rendered object, never drawn art and never a number; both panels on the one surface tone; the caption is a title and one line only. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** The second panel hangs a fixed 96px below the first, so the two image-topped panels step down like a staircase.
- **Buttons (`circle-arrow`):** A label with a round arrow: the label at 18-20px in the ink, weight 500, then 12px on a 52-56px circle filled with the accent holding an arrow in the accent's ink; label and circle are one link, 56px tall; hover turns the arrow 45° and slides the label 4px. The second action is the label alone, without the circle. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A label followed by a 52-56px circle in the accent holding an arrow that turns 45 degrees on hover; label and circle are one link.
- **Corners:** A 4px radius on cards, panels and fields; pictures square or at the same 4px. (`--radius: 4px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Airy: 22-28vh between sections (200-250px at 1440, 112-140px on a phone), page margins 48-72px at 1440 and 24px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(7rem, 12vh + 7vw, 15.75rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4.5vw, 4.5rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** One grade over every picture (slightly warm or slightly cool, a little lifted shadows), the same on all of them. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).

- **Video:** No video. Motion comes from the page itself.

## 1. The idea
The page is an argument in plain sentences, each with its evidence beside it. The first screen is one short sentence about the subject that a visitor can check against the picture next to it ('Every loaf leaves the oven by six', beside the racks at six): the sentence sets the grid and the measure, and the picture stands beside it or opens full width right after it, never inside it. Write the sentences first, from the inventory, one claim each; then give each the one thing that proves it: a photograph, a figure, a short list, the product doing it. The reader believes the page because every sentence is shown.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the sentence, four to seven words on two lines at the dealt display step, flush to the left margin across columns 1–7, with one supporting sentence and the action under it on the same edge. The picture it names fills columns 8–12 at the full height of the screen and ends on the right margin; or, when the sentence takes the full width, the picture runs across the width directly under it, cropped by the bottom of the screen (never on a software page: its interface stands beside the sentence). Later sections repeat the grammar at a smaller scale: a heading of one short sentence on the left half and its proof on the right half (a photograph, a figure with its unit, a short list, an interface fragment), top-aligned with it, so the page reads as a series of sentences with evidence. Every sentence starts on the same left edge, and no picture sits between words or between lines. The space between sections as section 0 deals it.

## 3. Type — Lexend, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.414 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 6.6vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Lexend does every job, told apart by size and weight (600 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Lexend:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Lexend** — weight 600, sentence case, tracking -0.023em, leading 1.03
- Text: **Lexend** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.600rem, 0.960rem + 1.00vw, 2.000rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.000rem, 1.200rem + 1.57vw, 2.825rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.494rem, 1.496rem + 4.09vw, 5.938rem);
}
```
Display weight with real presence (medium to bold), tracking -0.03 to -0.05em, leading 0.95–1.0 so two lines sit close. The sentences are real sentences: sentence case, with a full stop on every one or on none.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-very-tight-sans-display-ladder`, Very tight sans, graduated ladder): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one medium (400-500) sans with tracking of -0.04 to -0.06em on every display step, even the 33px one; body stays near -0.01 to -0.02em for reading. Six steps from 10-12vw (144-175px at 1440, leading 0.8-0.95) through 107 / 80 / 53 / 33px, then 17px body, all in mixed case; headlines two lines at most. Ratio 7-13x. The signature is the tracking, and the steps are graduated: hero 144-175px, section titles 80-107px, card titles 33px. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card or button fill beyond the dealt button. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; link hover changes colour. House rules for it: Clean after the pass: tracking is the one signature; entrance as dealt; card titles at the sub step, not display size. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

keep them in colour, as shot; a black-and-white stand-in from anywhere else is left out too (references/imagery.md §6). Use no more of them than the photograph total below allows, not all eight. Always set `width`, `height`, `alt` and `loading="lazy"` (not on the hero image).

**The photograph total: at most 5 on the whole page** (this direction takes 5): the hero 1 and the gallery 2. Each blueprint below takes its share, whatever number it names; a part left with none is built from its words, the colour and the dealt card style, never with a photograph shown again at another crop.

**Hard rule: the client's own thing is never shown by another's.** No other maker's product however cropped, no named hotel's or other house's room, no other artist's work, no other house captioned as the client's, whatever the credit says; and no picture shown as the client's carries a credit or file title that names another business (a hotel, a maker, a brand): a picture so credited shows only the world around, captioned with what and where it is, or is left out. Without the client's photographs, its place, its people and its things are never another's: the pictures show the world around it, captioned with what and where they are, and a slot only the client's own picture can fill goes to type.

## 6. Hero — The answer first
The headline says what the service is and for whom; one sentence; the action. Beside or under it one real picture of the place or the people at work. Beside it, the headline is 5vw or more (72px at 1440); a headline that cannot be that large goes above the picture, or over it full-bleed. Under both, three or four facts a visitor would otherwise search for (price from, where, when, how long a stay or a session lasts, never a travel time the brief does not give), each a short line at the lead step that says the whole fact ("From 39 € a night, breakfast included"), never a band of big figures over one-word labels. The facts are true and complete.

On a phone: Headline, sentence, action, picture, then the facts as a short list.
Headline: What it is and for whom, three to eight words; at most about 28 characters when it stands beside the picture.
**Must show:** A headline that says what the service is and for whom, one real picture beside or under it, and three or four complete facts as short lines under both.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**A split hero takes a large headline:** beside a picture that holds half the screen, the `h1` is 72px (5vw) or more at 1440, on two lines at most, so about 26-30 characters. If the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead; never a small headline in a 50/50 split, which reads as a shop template.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (600), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** gallery gallery, features split, intro the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

## 8. Motion — Ink-fill statement
One statement of three to five lines at 32px or more starts at 45% ink and fills to full ink line by line as it rises from three quarters of the way down the screen to just past half way, so the reading pace follows the scroll. Every word is readable before it fills; the fill only sets the rhythm. Once per page, never on body copy, never on a headline the visitor needs at once. Full ink without scroll timelines or under reduced motion. Recipe: 'Ink fill' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Line blocks (`line-blocks`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Each line of the headline starts as a solid block of one palette colour, exactly as long as its words, and the blocks slide off toward the line ends one after another. The words never move. The picture is in place and still. Settled by 1.1 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="line-blocks">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must): a line that wraps gets one block across the whole column, and the inspector fails it. The blocks are the ink: put `--block: var(--ink)` on the body.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `line-blocks` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=line-blocks`.
- **Below the fold: Rows step in.** Lists, indexes, tables and price rows (data-reveal="rows" on the list) go from 40% to full opacity in reading order, once: the first four 60 ms apart, the rest together. No travel, nothing else enters, and no blocks below the fold.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Brackets close in** (`mo-text-bracket-close-in`): A single word or two short words set large as the section's statement (display step, display face, ink, centred), flanked by two bracket glyphs from the same face at 1.2x the word's height. Scrubbed by scroll (from 85% to 50% of the viewport height) the brackets start 300px further out on each side and slide inward until they sit 0.2em from the word; the word itself is always fully visible. After landing everything holds still; a paragraph (lead step, 44 characters) sits 56px under it. Ground as dealt, accent none. Phone: word at the head step, brackets travel 120px. House rules for it: Never a small bracketed label above a heading (an eyebrow): the brackets are typographic marks around the large statement word itself; the page's own face, never a monospace; brackets stay ink, not a styled accent; scroll-linked, so the dealt entrance stands. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, tested and ready to fill. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 0.6 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### The dot becomes the full stop (`dot-becomes-the-full-stop`) — carries the intro section
On the page's own ground one large dot in the ink, the display face's own full stop set very large, drops in from the top edge as the stage arrives and lands in the middle, and the first word of a claim comes out of it before the stage holds; the rest of the claim, four or five words at true display size across nearly the whole width, grows out of it to the left one whole word at a time with a short rise and no blur, the dot stepping right and a step smaller with every word while the phrase stays centred, until it is exactly the size and place of the sentence's own full stop as the last word lands. An optional second beat cuts the ground to ink in one jump, turns the sentence the paper colour and sets one plain supporting line under it, whole. A graphic object promoted into the typography: a middle for pages short of pictures, services above all. Hold: 0.6 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle of the page, after the hero: the intro, the about or the proof, the one claim the page stands on told as one sentence; never the call to action, which needs its button. With the second beat on, the section after starts on the ink ground and carries it on; with it off, on the page ground. Once per page, with a quiet section before and after it.
Content: No pictures. One claim of 4-5 words, 16-30 characters, ending on its full stop (the scene's subject), every word under about 11 characters, in the page's display face; a face whose full stop is a true round dot reads best, since the dot is that glyph. One optional supporting sentence of 8-16 words for the last beat. Nothing else on the stage: no labels, buttons, counters or drawn shapes. The page's ink and ground, 7:1 or better both ways; no accent on the line or the dot. Without a claim that ends on a full stop, use a plain heading.
Options (data-options): hold 0.6 (0.5-1.5); phoneHold 0.7 (0.5-1.5); beat true (the hard cut to ink, the sentence in the paper colour and the supporting line whole) | false (add class dfs--plain to the section; the ground stays, and a supporting line, if any, still appears whole once the full stop lands); dot 0.2 (0.1-0.28) the dropped dot's diameter as a share of the stage height, never more than a third of its width; fill 0.95 (0.5-0.97) share of the content width the finished sentence spans; max 0.17 (0.1-0.24) share of the stage height the type never passes; min 0.07 (0.04-0.1) share of the stage height under which one row takes two; rise 0.24 (0.1-0.4) em each word rises into place; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-ink', the class it gets from the cut on; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --dfs-paper, --dfs-ink, --dfs-size, --dfs-lh, --dfs-gap, --dfs-pad, --dfs-support-size.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/dot-becomes-the-full-stop.js` and `dot-becomes-the-full-stop.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/dot-becomes-the-full-stop.html`. On a phone: The same scene with phone numbers: the claim fitted to the full width on two rows broken where they are most even (one when it fits large, never three); the dot rides the first row, takes a short carriage return down to the last row under the row above, rides it a step smaller with each word and lands on the full stop measured on the last row; hold 0.7. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Sentences a reader can check, each paired with the thing that proves it. The first picture is read as the evidence for the headline, so choose it with care: it shows exactly what the sentence says, large.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
The sentence must be short enough for two lines on a phone (about 32 characters). Do not style one word differently to stress it. Do not put a picture inside the sentence to make it an idea: the idea is the match between what the sentence says and what the picture shows. A sentence with no proof beside it is a slogan; cut it or find its proof.

## Always

no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
