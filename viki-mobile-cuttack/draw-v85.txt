## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`bottom-dock`):** A dock at the bottom: at the top of the first screen only the wordmark, at the left margin, which scrolls away with the hero. The links and the action live in a capsule fixed 20px above the bottom edge and centred, there from the first screen on, 56px tall, as wide as its content, filled with the ink and lettered in the ground colour (on a dark page the surface tone with ink letters), opaque, no border, no shadow: three or four section names at 15-16px, the current one at weight 600, and the action as its one filled item in the accent. On a phone the dock spans the width less the margins, 60px plus the safe area, each target 44px or taller, and the page keeps 96px free under its last line. It slides down out of view while a pinned scene or the replay holds the screen, and comes back after. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The links and the action live in a capsule in the ink fixed 20px above the bottom edge; at the top only the wordmark.
- **Footer (`contact-block`):** A big contact block on the ink: the heading (Visit, Write, Book) at the h2 step on the left half; on the right half the email at the h3 step with a Copy button and a mailto link, and the city; under both, a row of the section links, the legal links and Image credits. About 60-70svh. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** On the ink: the heading at the h2 step on the left half, the email at the h3 step with a Copy button on the right half.
- **Cards (`overlapping`):** Overlapping cards: a row of three or four filled cards with the dealt corner, in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so every edge reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone. Each overlaps the one before by 24-32px and sits 24px lower, so the row steps down from left to right; its padding on the covered side is the overlap plus 24px or more, so no word sits under the card above. Hover or focus brings one to the front with a 6px rise. On a phone they stack with no overlap. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** A row of three or four cards, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each overlapping the one before by 24-32px and sitting 24px lower.
- **Buttons (`big-block`):** Big blocks: square, 72px tall, 280px wide or more, the text face at the h4 step, weight 600, filled with the accent, the label at the left; hover darkens the fill. For the one action of a confident page. The second action is a text link at the same size. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** Square blocks in the accent, 72px tall and 280px wide or more in the hero and the sections, the label at the h4 step at the left.
- **Corners:** A 4px radius on cards, panels and fields; pictures square or at the same 4px. (`--radius: 4px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Airy: 22-28vh between sections (200-250px at 1440, 112-140px on a phone), page margins 48-72px at 1440 and 24px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(7rem, 12vh + 7vw, 15.75rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4.5vw, 4.5rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts

## 1. The idea
Lay the page out like a well-designed printed report or magazine feature: a masthead image, a tight oversized title, text held to a strict column line, and colour used in solid blocks the way a two-colour print job would. It gives weight and authority to subjects that are really about content: findings, a manifesto, a programme, a serious product. The reader should feel that someone edited this.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A fixed vertical axis at about 42% of the viewport width organises everything: headings begin on it, body columns hang from it, and the 0–42% zone holds small side notes, figures or stays empty. Masthead: a wide image band (photographic mosaic or a single crop) across the top 45% of the first screen; below it the title at 7–9vw starting on the axis, with a two-sentence standfirst at 20–24px. Sections are mostly text pages (title on the axis, body in one or two columns of 34–40ch to its right); once or twice a full-bleed colour page, in a band section 4 deals, carries one large figure, quotation or image mosaic. Mosaics are gutterless: photographs and tiles of the surface tone or the accent butted together in a 4- or 6-cell grid. Page numbers, running heads and other print furniture are left out.

## 3. Type — Ranade over Sofia Sans
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.333 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 4.4vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Ranade only for the display and head steps; Sofia Sans for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=ranade@400,500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Sofia+Sans:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Ranade** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking -0.038em, leading 0.89; section headings (the head step) at weight 500
- Text: **Sofia Sans** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.420rem, 0.852rem + 0.89vw, 1.775rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.775rem, 1.065rem + 1.32vw, 2.369rem);
  --step-display: clamp(1.895rem, 1.137rem + 2.73vw, 3.962rem);
}
```
A tight heavy grotesk or a sharp serif for titles; an excellent text face for reading at 18–20px with leading near 1.5. Headings in sentence case, tracked tight.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

## 4. Colour — White and orange
```css
:root {
  --bg: #ffffff;
  --surface: #e9e9e9;
  --ink: #000000;
  --muted: #5f5f5f;
  --accent: #fe7141;
  --accent-ink: #000000;
  --field-1: #000000;  --field-1-ink: #f4f4f4;
  --field-2: #fe7141;  --field-2-ink: #000000;
}
```
A studied palette with its other fields left out: white ground, black ink, one orange accent for the primary action. Two grounds and two bands: the page ground and --surface, and the ink as ground and the orange accent, one section at most each. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product. The accent reads under 3:1 on this ground, so, whatever this note says before, it is a fill with its own text colour (the button, one block), never the colour of a word, a link or a thin mark: those take the ink.
Paper, ink and the accent, like a two-colour print job. A colour page is a whole section in the ink or the accent, as section 4 deals its bands; every other section stands on the paper.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); each of `--field-1`, `--field-2` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 6. Hero — Colour-block collage, crossing headline (`hero-colour-block-collage-crossing-headline`, a pattern of the design-anatomy study)
Described as the study saw it on a great site: build this composition with this page's own words and pictures, in the dealt type, colour and corners, its buttons section 0's; a backdrop it names that this page cannot honestly have (a 3D scene, footage) is a photograph of the subject or the product's own interface at the same size and place, or the ground itself.

Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (400), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro statement, features object, showcase single-picture, cta the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — Stacked headline with the last word rising right (statement) — `sec-statement-stacked-headline-last-word-rising-right`, a pattern of the design-anatomy study
A pattern of the study standing in for this section's blueprint: its description is the spec, built with this page's content, in the dealt type, colour, corners, card style and button, within the word and picture budgets above.
Ground = the palette ground, ink in contrast. A four-word headline at the display step in the dealt display weight on two lines: line one holds words 1-2 flush left at the margin; line two holds word 3 flush left and word 4 right-aligned to the container's right margin. Word 4 rises about 120px into place over about 80svh of scroll (scroll-linked, translateY 120 to 0) so it arrives last. Under word 3 at the left, 32px below the headline, a paragraph at the lead step 360px wide in three or four lines and, 32px under it, the dealt button, so no row is empty at the left. Phone: the headline in two lines at the head step with the last word right-aligned, the paragraph and the full-width dealt button under it. House rules for it: The words stay inside the page margins; the last word is the same style as the rest, never an accent word; the paragraph fills the left of the second row, so no row is empty on the left.

### 2. features — Capabilities as a menu (object)
The capabilities are set as a printed menu or price list. One paper-coloured sheet, 520–600px wide and taller than it is wide, sits on a contrasting flat ground, centred or starting at column 2; its top and bottom edges are cut into shallow scallops or a zigzag with clip-path, its sides straight. On it, centred at the top, the heading: two or three words at 44–56px, then 40px of space. Below, items in two or three groups; each group opens with its name at 20px bold. Each item is a row: the name at 19px on the left, a short value on the right (a price, a time, a quantity, 'included'), and between them a leader of round 4px dots (border-bottom: 4px dotted, sitting on the baseline). A description at 15px in the soft ink sits under the name, at most 34ch. 18px between items, 44px between groups.
On a phone: The sheet fills the width minus 12px margins and keeps its cut top and bottom edges. Names stay left and values right on one line with the dot leader between; descriptions wrap beneath at 15px. Group names drop to 18px. Padding inside the sheet is 20px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets running, list or table text at 15px: use your body step (16px or more, 15px at the least on a phone). The inspector fails it under 14px and warns under 16px (`small-body`).
- It draws lines or borders: keep them only inside a table, a form or a `data-ui` fragment; elsewhere separate with space or a change of ground.
- It puts the content on a panel of another tone: set it on the section's own ground, or as the card style section 0 deals on the surface tone. A panel in a colour the palette does not name is one more ground (`surface-count`).
- It joins a name and its value with a leader of dots: join them with space alone (`justify-content: space-between`), no dots and no line.
- It cuts scalloped, zigzag, notched or torn edges: no drawn or decorative shapes on this page, and a card is never cut into a ticket. Every edge stays straight, and the content stands on the section's own ground.

### 3. showcase — One image across tiles (single-picture)
One photograph is cut across a board of tiles. A grid of six columns and four rows with 8px gaps spans the full width between 12px margins; every tile is square (aspect-ratio: 1). Each tile shows its own piece of the same image: set the photograph as every tile's background at background-size: 600% 400% and step background-position by column and row, so the picture reads whole through the gaps. Five of the twenty-four tiles are swapped for flat colour tiles from the palette, never two adjacent: one holds the heading (two to four words at 2.6vw, top-left, 16px padding), three hold one fact each at 18px, one holds the action. On hover a tile's piece scales to 108% inside its own clip. Choose a photograph with one clear subject, so the board is legible at a glance.
On a phone: Three columns by six rows with 6px gaps, the photograph re-cut at background-size 300% 600% from a portrait crop. Four colour tiles only; the heading tile spans two columns in the top row with the heading at 6vw. Facts drop to 15px.

### 4. cta — the scene `page-lifts-off-closing-picture` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the cta) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
A big contact block on the ink: the heading (Visit, Write, Book) at the h2 step on the left half; on the right half the email at the h3 step with a Copy button and a mailto link, and the city; under both, a row of the section links, the legal links and Image credits. About 60-70svh. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Pushed line
One line of very large type, the name or the claim, is pushed sideways by the scroll rather than by a clock: it travels from 12% right to 12% left of its place while it crosses the screen, linear and locked to the scrollbar, and reads whole at the middle of its run. One per page, never across running text, and the words must never be cropped at the moment the line sits in the middle of the screen. Build with animation-timeline: view() on the line and a translateX keyframe; static where scroll timelines are unsupported and under reduced motion.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Set type (`set-type`), dealt
This is the masked line rise with the clip-path shutter: once every page's default, now one entrance of seven and rarely dealt. Build it as base.css has it.
- **What the visitor sees:** The headline's lines rise out of their own baselines one after another while the picture opens downward like a blind; the sentence and the action rise 12 px after them. Lines, then sentence and action; there is no picture step. Settled by 1.2 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="set-type">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"` and `data-e="action"` (no picture on this first screen). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must).
- **A scene carries the first screen:** mark its headline, sentence and action only; its picture moves with the scene.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `set-type` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=set-type`.
- **Below the fold: Headings by line.** Chapter headings (data-lines, five at most) rise line by line from their masks once, using the "Lines that rise on scroll" script in motion.md. Nothing else enters, and a stray data-reveal does nothing.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Mask roll swap** (`mo-text-mask-roll-swap`): When a state changes (a slide, a tab, a disc, a list item) the text that describes it, a title, a price, a date or a one-to-three-word value at the lead, sub or head step, rolls out and the new text rolls in. The old text moves up 100 percent out of a mask that is exactly one line tall plus 0.1em at the bottom so descenders survive, in 300-420ms on cubic-bezier 0.77, 0, 0.18, 1; the new text enters from below 60ms later, words staggered 30ms. Labels beside it slide 12-23px. If the new title wraps to two lines the container animates its height in the same 300ms. The roll follows the direction of navigation (next goes up, previous goes down). It plays only after the visitor's own action, never on load or on scroll. Phone: the same, 260ms. House rules for it: The mask only clips the text itself, no outlined box; whole words roll, never single letters; it runs only after the visitor's own action, so it is not an entrance. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Rounded tile goes edge** (`mo-scale-rounded-tile-widen`): A picture tile (16:9 or 21:9, 1370px wide at 1440) sits inset by the page margin on each side with the dealt corner as the section arrives. Over the next 650-720px of scroll (while it is sticky, or as its top travels from 90 to 30 percent of the viewport) it widens to the full 1440px: clip-path inset(0 var(--margin) round var(--radius)) to inset(0 0 round 0), the height holding or growing from 80svh to 100svh. The corner eases to 0 so it ends edge to edge. Text for the section sits below the tile and is simply there (the dealt entrance decides whether it arrives); no caption sits on the picture. Phone: inset by the page margin with the dealt corner, widening to the full 390 over 60 percent of the scroll distance. House rules for it: A picture tile in the dealt corner, no outline, no caption box on the photograph; the pin, if any, is under two screens. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, tested and ready to fill. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 3.4 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Shape-shifting window over a fixed world (`shaped-window-over-fixed-world`) — carries the first screen
One full-bleed photograph of a place stays still behind a shutter in the page's ground colour with two tall windows cut in it side by side, level, a narrow bar between them, so the first frame is one picture of the place seen through a mullion, under the title; the scroll widens the windows outward (the title over one and the caption under the other never move), closes the bar, then scales the shutter past the screen edges so the visitor pushes through into the place, and one chapter word rises in once the window has cleared it. It lets one strong, wide picture of a building, a room or a landscape carry the opening title and the name of the first chapter. Hold: 2.4 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: The first screen, as the hero (the page's one action stays in the header bar), opening onto the first chapter, which is the place itself. The page's ground is one flat colour that the shutter matches exactly.
Content: One landscape photograph of the place, 3:2 or 16:10, about 3600px wide, with the place's subject in its middle half (the first frame shows that half, the bar on its centre line), a calm top third for the word and nothing important on the vertical centre line; a 9:16 crop for phones; or a muted 8-12 s loop with little motion and the still as its poster. Two optional props for the bottom corners (foliage, a lamp, a chair), as cut-outs or keyed from a photograph of something dark against a bright sky. A title of 4-7 words on two lines (about 25 characters), a caption of 10-18 words (wide screens only, and left out where it does not fit under the windows, so repeat its key fact in the lede), one chapter word of 6-12 letters, and a lede of 18-32 words with a 2-3 word link, set on a strip of the page's ground at the foot of the stage.
Options (data-options): hold 2.4 (1.8-3); phoneHold 2.0 (1.6-2.6); open 0.38 (0-0.5) how far each window widens outward, a share of its width in the first frame; phoneOpen 0.22 (0-0.4; the window stops 16px from the screen's edge); (stagger and phoneStagger are ignored: the windows always start level); bar 'auto' or 8-24 px of ground between the windows; high 'right' | 'left', the window the caption hangs under (the title stands over the other); overscan 1.08 (1.04-1.15); phoneOverscan 1.16 (1.04-1.2); worldScale 0.8 (0.75-1) the world's start scale; wordTop 80 px (phoneWordTop 72); wordWidth 0.92 (0.6-0.96; phoneWordWidth 0.86); parallax 0.2 (0-0.3); scrub 0.6, true with Lenis; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Under a page's fixed or sticky bar the title and the word stay clear of it, and on wide screens the windows are laid out under it. The dim behind the word is one flat, even fill over the whole world (--sw-scrim-fill); keep it dark: recoloured to a pale ground it is a milky veil over the photograph (`gradient`). The bar's merge, the title's fade, the push past the screen's edges, the word and the lede run on short clocks, so every stop is a finished frame.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/shaped-window-over-fixed-world.js` and `shaped-window-over-fixed-world.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/shaped-window-over-fixed-world.html`. On a phone: The same push with two slim windows across 85% of the width, the title full width above them, no caption and the word fitted to 86% of the width, its letters landing on a clock of 0.6s once the window has cleared the word, never with the scroll (on every screen); hold 2. Portrait tablets get the same.

### Page lifts off a closing picture (`page-lifts-off-closing-picture`) — carries the cta section
The last content section slides up like a sheet, at its full width and in its own ground, and uncovers a picture that was held underneath it all along; while the sheet clears the screen the picture settles from a close 1.35x zoom to its natural framing, landing exactly as the scroll runs out. A solid band across the top third carries the closing line, a headline at display size, and the contact block in the body face, in the picture's own top-edge colour, so band and picture meet with no seam, and the whole picture is the page's one action with a plain label at its foot. The picture is a photograph, or a full-bleed close crop of the product's interface built in HTML (data-ui) whose app bar takes the band's colour, so a page can close on its product rather than a photograph. Hold: 1 screen. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: The end of the page: the contact or the call to action, straight after the last content section, which becomes the sheet that lifts (give it a screen of height or more). The footer after it is a designed section of its own and follows the finished frame: leave `foot` unset (it pulls the element it names over the stage's foot, so it suits only a page that ends on the scene). Once per page, and never right after another scene: the section that lifts is the quiet section between them.
Content: One picture and one line. Photo mode: one photograph 2400px wide or more (3000+ for full sharpness at the 1.35x start), its top edge even (fog, sky, a plain wall, a table top) and its subject in the lower half; a busy or textured top edge shows the seam, so smooth it or redraw. Interface mode: a close crop of the real product in HTML with data-ui, full bleed with no card, frame or rounded edge: an app bar in var(--lift-band-c) (so it meets the band with no seam), a title and two to four rows at true size (labels 14px or more at 1440, any heading in it under 24px), believable figures that add up, one item picked; side padding var(--lift-ui-inset). Its words end in the top ~37% of the stage (about 335px at 1440x900) because it grows 1.35x from its top edge and must never pass under the label; the rest is the product's own empty surface, where the label sits. Its CSS under [data-ui]. Words: a closing line of 3-7 words (h2, display face at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) over seven columns, two lines at most), a contact block of one to three short paragraphs at body size (address, email and phone, hours; never a giant name or email), and a label of 1-3 words for the one action. --lift-band set to the photograph's top-edge sample at 1440x900 for no script (the script samples it live), or to a tone step of --ground in interface mode.
Options (data-options): mode 'photo' | 'ui' (default from data-ui on .lift-media); settle 1.35 (1.1-1.5) the start scale, 1 at the end of the scroll; phoneSettle 1.18 (1.05-1.3); lift 1 (0.9-1) the sheet's scale as it leaves (under 1 pulls its sides in over the picture: old pages only); dim 0 (0-0.3) how much it darkens (over 0 turns the page's ground into a grey slab: old pages only); band 'auto' (the median of the photograph's top edge as cropped on this screen; same-origin pictures, or CORS ones with crossorigin="anonymous") | 'token' (--lift-band; the default in ui mode) | a CSS colour, and a dark band turns its words to --ground; focus the settle's transform-origin ('50% 0%', so the seam row never changes, in both modes); label 'foot' | 'centre'; sheet a selector for the section that lifts (default the element before the scene); foot unset by default (the designed footer follows the scene); a selector pulls that element over the picture's foot so the scroll ends on the settled frame, only for a page that ends on the scene with one plain line; scrub true (locked) or 0.1-1 s; phone 'same' | 'rest'. The hold is not an option: it is the lift, the sheet's height up to one screen. Each word block lands whole on a short clock once the sheet's edge has cleared it.
Leave `foot` unset: the footer dealt in section 0 (`contact-block`) follows the held stage as its own section, after the section gap, and is never pulled over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/page-lifts-off-closing-picture.js` and `page-lifts-off-closing-picture.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/page-lifts-off-closing-picture.html`. On a phone: The same lift: the picture stays held under the page while it slides away at its full width, with a gentler 1.18x settle. The band grows with its words (a 32-40px title over a two-column contact block, about 32-37% of the screen, the words low in it so the title lands by about 0.9 of the lift) instead of squeezing them, the label sits just above the footer line, and the interface crop stays full bleed but drops to the columns that fit, its words inset by --lift-ui-inset so none leaves the screen at the start scale. phone: 'rest' gives the finished composition.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The column line, the mosaics and whole pages of colour give structure and variety without ornaments.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
The axis must be exact and reused everywhere or it reads as random indentation. Keep paragraphs to a real reading measure. On a phone the axis collapses to the left margin and side notes move inline above their paragraph.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
