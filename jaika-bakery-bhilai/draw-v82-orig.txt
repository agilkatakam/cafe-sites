## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`chapter-band`):** A band that names where you are: fixed at the top, 56px tall, a solid band of the page ground on every screen. The wordmark at the left margin at the h4 step; at the right margin a Menu text button beside the one action (40px tall); dead centre the name of the section in view at 16px in the ink: the section's one- or two-word name (its link in Menu), never a sentence or a number. When the next section takes the screen the old name slides up out of a 24px mask and the new one slides up in (280ms, ease-out); over the hero the centre is empty. Menu opens the section names full-screen on the ink, with the action and the email. On a phone: a 56px band with the wordmark, Menu and the action; the live name is dropped. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as the links of the Menu panel, and the one action. **Must show:** The centre of the fixed 56px band shows the name of the section in view and swaps it by sliding the old name up out of a mask.
- **Footer (`two-halves`):** Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** Two halves meeting on the centre line: the closing line and button on the ink at the left, the links and contact on the surface tone at the right.
- **Cards (`overlapping`):** Overlapping cards: a row of three or four filled cards with the dealt corner, in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so every edge reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone. Each overlaps the one before by 24-32px and sits 24px lower, so the row steps down from left to right; its padding on the covered side is the overlap plus 24px or more, so no word sits under the card above. Hover or focus brings one to the front with a 6px rise. On a phone they stack with no overlap. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** A row of three or four cards, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each overlapping the one before by 24-32px and sitting 24px lower.

One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A 48px ink pill with a 32px round chip inset 8px at its right end; on hover the label rolls up and the chip turns the accent.
- **Corners:** A 4px radius on cards, panels and fields; pictures square or at the same 4px. (`--radius: 4px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. ## 1. The idea
The page is an argument in plain sentences, each with its evidence beside it. The first screen is one short sentence about the subject that a visitor can check against the picture next to it ('Every loaf leaves the oven by six', beside the racks at six): the sentence sets the grid and the measure, and the picture stands beside it or opens full width right after it, never inside it. Write the sentences first, from the inventory, one claim each; then give each the one thing that proves it: a photograph, a figure, a short list, the product doing it. The reader believes the page because every sentence is shown.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the sentence, four to seven words on two lines at the dealt display step, flush to the left margin across columns 1–7, with one supporting sentence and the action under it on the same edge. The picture it names fills columns 8–12 at the full height of the screen and ends on the right margin; or, when the sentence takes the full width, the picture runs across the width directly under it, cropped by the bottom of the screen (never on a software page: its interface stands beside the sentence). Later sections repeat the grammar at a smaller scale: a heading of one short sentence on the left half and its proof on the right half (a photograph, a figure with its unit, a short list, an interface fragment), top-aligned with it, so the page reads as a series of sentences with evidence. Every sentence starts on the same left edge, and no picture sits between words or between lines. The space between sections as section 0 deals it.

## 3. Type — Anton over Source Sans 3
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.25 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5.5vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Anton only for the display and head steps; Source Sans 3 for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Anton:wght@400&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Source+Sans+3:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Anton** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking -0.008em, leading 0.9
- Text: **Source Sans 3** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.125rem, 0.675rem + 0.56vw, 1.250rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.340rem, 0.804rem + 0.84vw, 1.675rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.675rem, 1.005rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.079rem, 0.600rem + 4.60vw, 5.500rem);
}
```
`--step-display` is raised above for this page's split hero (section 6): it reaches 76px at 1440, where the type system's own stops at 69px. The other steps stand.
Display weight with real presence (medium to bold), tracking -0.03 to -0.05em, leading 0.95–1.0 so two lines sit close. The sentences are real sentences: sentence case, with a full stop on every one or on none.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names: the place itself, never the village next door under "a white house on the fjord" (with no such picture, the first screen is set in type); and the first screen names the specific place the brief gives (the fjord, the village, the district), or when the brief names only a region, a real place in it chosen with the visit block and marked as sample copy in the report, never the region alone.
**A split hero takes a large headline:** beside a picture that holds half the screen, the `h1` is 72px (5vw) or more at 1440, on two lines at most, so about 26-30 characters. If the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead; never a small headline in a 50/50 split, which reads as a shop template.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (400), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

Adjusted for this page:

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**The visit: this is a place people go to, so the page says how to get there** (in a short visit section before the footer, which a place people go to always has): the street, a real street in the right district of the town the brief names, with no house number (the brief gives none), or for a remote place or a brief that names only a region, the village it stands in (a real one in that place, chosen and marked as sample copy when the brief names none) and the nearest town with the road from it; the district or area; the nearest stop and its line (read from a map in this session or one you are sure of, otherwise the district's main station), or for a remote place the drive from that town with its time, and from the airport or city most visitors come from (from the road distance on a map, or at about 60 km an hour on country roads when no map can be read, rounded up; never a time that makes the place nearer than it is: a Westfjords house once claimed forty minutes from Reykjavik); the opening hours; a "Directions" link to a maps service searching that street, village or road and town; and one contact (the email on the brand's own domain, on `.example` where the name is coined, since the brief gives no phone). Set it as one group, in the card style section 0 deals or as one panel, and only here: the footer gives the city and Directions, not the block again. Never a placeholder street, never a zeroed phone, never the city's name alone (`visit-without-directions`). The report marks the street or the village, the district, the way there and its time, the hours and the email as sample copy for the client to confirm.

**Structures, in order:** gallery the scene, features table, intro the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. gallery — the scene `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the gallery) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 2. features — Comparison set as text (table)
A comparison table with no lines and no tick marks. A CSS grid: capability names in columns 1–5 of 12 at 22–24px medium, then three answer columns of equal width (plans, methods, or this against the usual way). Every cell answers in words at 17px: 'Yes, unlimited', 'Up to five', 'Not yet', never a symbol. Seven to ten rows, each 64–72px tall, with every other row on a ground one tone darker so the eye tracks across; rows touch. The column that matters is one filled block in the accent or ink colour running the full height of the table behind its cells and rising 24px above the first row, with its text reversed. Column names sit in the top row at 17px bold. The heading, two to four words at 40–48px, hangs top-left above column 1 with 48px beneath.
On a phone: The table turns on its side: one block per capability. The name at 20px bold, then three lines beneath, each giving a column name and its answer at 16px; the featured column's line is set on the filled colour at full width. Blocks alternate the two ground tones.

### 3. intro — the scene `lib-0236` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the intro) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 4. cta — carried by the footer's closing line (section 0)
The footer dealt in section 0 opens on the page's closing line and its button: that is this page's call to action. Build no section here; the section before it runs straight into the footer after the section gap.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. ## 8. Motion — Rolling figures
For a page whose case is a handful of numbers (a report, a fund, a practice with a founding year): three or four figures at display size roll each digit up to its value once as they come into view, about 1.1 seconds on a strong ease-out, the columns 70ms apart. Tabular figures, the real number kept in the text for screen readers and search, never in running text and never for a value that changes after load. Set at its value under reduced motion. Recipe: 'Rolling figures' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Already there (`already-there`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Nothing arrives. The first frame is the finished first screen. The only motion is inside the media: a clip that plays, a product that responds to the pointer, a picture that fades in only if it arrived late. The photograph is there. If none of it had painted by the first frame, it waits on --picture-ground and fades in over 320 ms. A dealt clip plays from frame one, with that frame as its poster. Settled by the first frame.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="already-there">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `already-there` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=already-there`.
- **Below the fold: Nothing.** Nothing enters below the fold, and no element on the page gets data-reveal. Section content is simply there, and motion comes from the moves the sheet deals, any moving picture and feedback under the hand. Lazy pictures get the same arrival courtesy as the hero: they wait on their ground colour and fade in when they land. That is loading, not a reveal.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Mask roll swap** (`mo-text-mask-roll-swap`): When a state changes (a slide, a tab, a disc, a list item) the text that describes it, a title, a price, a date or a one-to-three-word value at the lead, sub or head step, rolls out and the new text rolls in. The old text moves up 100 percent out of a mask that is exactly one line tall plus 0.1em at the bottom so descenders survive, in 300-420ms on cubic-bezier 0.77, 0, 0.18, 1; the new text enters from below 60ms later, words staggered 30ms. Labels beside it slide 12-23px. If the new title wraps to two lines the container animates its height in the same 300ms. The roll follows the direction of navigation (next goes up, previous goes down). It plays only after the visitor's own action, never on load or on scroll. Phone: the same, 260ms. House rules for it: The mask only clips the text itself, no outlined box; whole words roll, never single letters; it runs only after the visitor's own action, so it is not an entrance. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Grows from small** (`mo-scale-grow-from-small-on-entry`): A vertical list of 3-5 rows, each one photograph (a frame full width inside the page margins, about 1296x680 at 1440, the dealt corner) with its title at the display step, one line, above it. As a row enters, the frame scales from 0.2 to 1.0 about its centre (scrubbed, 0.4s smoothing) between 100% and 55% of the viewport height, so it opens like a window from a thumbnail to full width; the title is fixed in place and unaffected. Inside the frame the image sits at 112% height and drifts 6% vertically as the row passes. A one-line caption (body step) sits bottom-left under the frame. The dealt section space between rows. Large scale range, no pin. Ground and ink as dealt, accent none. Phone: scale 0.5 to 1.0, drift 4%, frame 4:5 full width. House rules for it: No numbering on rows; no overlay boxes on the photographs; captions sit on the ground, not on the picture; one title per row, two lines at most. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, one tested and ready to fill, and one researched technique to build fresh. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 4 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Thumbnail becomes the stage (`thumbnail-becomes-the-stage`) — carries the gallery section
A statement of one or two lines at display size sits over a row of large thumbnails on the layout's columns, whose middle one is a keyhole onto a full-screen chapter; from the first step of the scroll the keyhole grows over its neighbours as they slide in under it, rises and opens to full bleed, and the statement passes through its edge, ink outside and paper inside, sliding from centred to flush left to become the chapter's heading word for word. It opens a product, a place or a film chapter from one strong muted loop or one strong picture. Hold: 2 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle, as the door from the intro into the showcase or the work; the opened picture carries the chapter's feature notes on a solid band at its foot, and below 600px they follow it in the page's flow.
Content: One chapter picture or muted loop of 8-15 s (16:9, 1920x1080 or more, 8MB at most, with a poster; for software, a screen recording of the product), its subject in the centre: the keyhole opens on about the central half of its width and three quarters of its height on desktop (that crop is the thumbnail); its upper left calm and dark for the white heading; a 4:5 cut for phones. One or two satellite stills a side of neighbouring chapters in the same grade, 960px wide or more with the subject in the centre (the row crops them close to square on desktop, 3:4 on tablets, 3:5 on phones). No text burned into the pictures. A statement of up to two lines of 20 characters or fewer that names the chapter and becomes its heading word for word; an optional note under the heading; three or four feature items of a 2-3 word label and 16 words or fewer.
Options (data-options): hold 2 (1.4-2.6); phoneHold 1.6 (1.4-2.6); phone 'same' | 'rest'; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis (0-0.8); thumb, the layout's columns by default (0.12-0.5; a share under the columns is lifted to them, a larger one runs the neighbours off the edges); radius, the page's --radius or 0 (0-16 px), always 0 at full bleed; shade 0.3 (0-0.6) flat scrim over the picture under the heading; band 1 (0-1) the band under the notes, solid by default; heading 0.15 (0.1-0.25) where the heading settles, the statement starting at the same height; nav '', the selector of a fixed nav that gets is-on-dark over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.js` and `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.html`. On a phone: The same opening with the keyhole starting at 64% of the width, 3:5, its neighbours running off the edges; hold 1.6. From 600px all four notes show two by two on the band; below 600px wide or under 540px tall they leave the stage and follow the picture in the page's flow, every one shown.

### Statements pull into focus under the scroll (`lib-0236`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. On a held stage short statements arrive by focus rather than by movement: each word or line comes in oversized, soft and faint and sharpens into place under one scrubbed value a little behind the reader, then leaves the same way or is pushed out while the next statement, or a paragraph drifting up through it, takes over, so a run of statements plays as a relay of focus pulls.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Animated blur on dozens of split letters is costly on low-end phones, so split by word and drop the blur on touch. A fast scroll swaps statements before they are read unless each rests sharp for a stretch. Text over a busy picture or video loses contrast mid-pull, so keep the ground flat. A paragraph crossing the statement hurts legibility for a moment and needs short text. Screen readers get split letters unless the sentence sits whole in an aria-label. More than two screens of hold feel stuck if nothing else happens on the stage.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a research lab's three findings: three statements of five to eight words in a heavy grotesque, ink on a flat paper ground, left-aligned at one height on a stage held for two screens. Each word, not each letter, comes in from 125 percent scale and a 6 px blur to sharp over its own slice of the scroll, the blur gone in the first third of its travel; once a statement is sharp, its one-sentence explanation in the body face rises up through and past it at 1.4 times the scroll speed, and the statement then leaves by focus in reverse while the next arrives. No accent word in a statement, no picture behind, and on touch the blur is dropped for a plain opacity rise. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 2 screens of scroll: `hold: 2` in `Stage.scene`, its section 3 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** its own version, as the direction above says (`phone: { hold, build }` in `Stage.scene`), or `phone: 'rest'` with a finished composition that reads whole; never the desktop move squeezed. Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 2, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Sentences a reader can check, each paired with the thing that proves it. The first picture is read as the evidence for the headline, so choose it with care: it shows exactly what the sentence says, large.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
The sentence must be short enough for two lines on a phone (about 32 characters). Do not style one word differently to stress it. Do not put a picture inside the sentence to make it an idea: the idea is the match between what the sentence says and what the picture shows. A sentence with no proof beside it is a slogan; cut it or find its proof.
