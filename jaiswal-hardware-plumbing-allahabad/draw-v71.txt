## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`big-links`):** Large links over the hero: the wordmark top-left and the section names as links at the h3 step in the text face at weight 500, spread across the top of the first screen on the grid columns, the action as a button at the right margin; 96px tall in all. Past the hero the links fold into a Menu text button beside the wordmark, the two fixed top-left on one solid tab of the ground (12px padding, the dealt corner), and the action stays fixed top-right on a tab of its own; Menu opens the same large links full-screen. On a phone: the wordmark and Menu on one tab, the action on the other. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The section names as links at the h3 step spread across the top of the first screen; past the hero they fold into a Menu tab beside the wordmark.
- **Footer (`newsletter-card`):** A newsletter block on the page ground: one filled card on the surface tone with the dealt corner, whatever the page's card style, across eight columns, holding one sentence at the h3 step and an email field with its button in one row; beside it, on four columns, the contact and two or three links; the legal line and Image credits under both. It is the page's one email form. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** One filled card across eight columns with a sentence at the h3 step and the email field and its button in one row.
- **Cards (`tall-text`):** Tall cards of words, filled top to foot: narrow cards at 4:5 on the surface tone with the dealt corner and 28px padding, four to a row with 16px gaps, the name at the h3 step on two lines at the top, under it two short facts as lines at the lead step (what it includes, for whom), and the price or the link at the foot; a card with less to say is cut to 1:1, never left hollow. Hover steps the fill one tone and moves the link 4px. For services, plans in short, reasons to choose. On a phone they stack at their natural height. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Narrow 4:5 cards of words, four to a row, filled top to foot: the name at the h3 step on two lines, two facts at the lead step under it, the price or the link at the foot.
- **Buttons (`btn-pill-tonal-trailing-chip`, Tonal pill with arrow chip: a pattern of the study):** A secondary pill with an arrow chip at its end: 52px tall at 1440, fully round, filled with the surface tone, the label at 16px at the type system's button weight in sentence case in the ink with 24px left padding, and a filled arrow chip 32px across inset 10px from the right end (an ink disc with a ground-coloured 14px arrow). Hover (200ms) floods the pill with the ink while the chip inverts to a ground-coloured disc with an ink arrow. It goes beside a solid primary, or ends a list of rows as the "see all" action. Dealt as the page's button design, the primary is the same pill filled with the ink with its chip in the accent, and this tonal pill is the second action. Colour roles: ground = page, surface tone = pill, ink = chip and hover fill. Phone: Full column width at 52px tall with the chip kept inset; hover becomes press. House rules for it: The pattern's faint 1px ring is an outline as the only shape, so the body is a surface-tone fill with no stroke; the flood and invert on hover stay. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A 52px pill in the surface tone with a 32px ink arrow disc inset 10px at its right end; on hover the pill floods ink while the disc turns to the ground colour.
- **Corners:** A 16px radius on cards and panels; pictures at 16px or square inside a card. (`--radius: 16px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(6rem, 9vh + 5vw, 11.25rem); --margin: clamp(1.25rem, 3vw, 3rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** One photograph that moves slowly as the page scrolls (a scale or drift of a few percent), once.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

## 1. The idea
Software is most convincing when it is doing its job in front of you. Instead of describing features, put small, true, working fragments of the product on the page: the actual control, with real content, responding to the pointer. A colour picker's loupe reads the page's own background and prints its value. A list tool lets you tick an item right there. A type-scale plugin sets the page's own headings. Find the one interaction that is the product and make the hero that interaction; then show each further capability as another fragment at real size. No dashboard collage, no browser frame, no screenshots shrunk until unreadable.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: a short plain headline (5–6vw, left-aligned, upper-left third) and one sentence; the rest of the first screen is the main fragment at actual interface size, sitting directly on the page or on one flat stage colour, large enough to read every label. The fragment is built in HTML and works. Below, capabilities are a single-column sequence, each a row of two parts on a 5/7 split: a two-line statement on one side and the relevant fragment on the other, with the fragments varying in size and shape so the column never becomes a zigzag of equal blocks. Fragments are cropped pieces of interface (a menu, a row, a control, a panel edge), not whole windows. One section pins a fragment while three short statements change it.

## 3. Type — Author, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.414 scale from a 17px body; the hero's display step is about 4.4vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Author does every job, told apart by size and weight (400 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=author@400,500,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Author** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking -0.035em, leading 0.99; section headings (the head step) at weight 500
- Text: **Author** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0625rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.277rem, 0.766rem + 0.63vw, 1.419rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.700rem, 1.020rem + 1.06vw, 2.125rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.125rem, 1.275rem + 1.67vw, 3.006rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.405rem, 0.600rem + 4.60vw, 5.500rem);
}
```
`--step-display` is raised above for this page's split hero (section 6): it reaches 76px at 1440, where the type system's own stops at 62px. The other steps stand.
The marketing type matches the product's interface type so the fragments and the page feel like one thing. Headline plain in its words, never small: beside the main fragment it is 5vw or more at 1440 (72px); interface text at its true size (13–15px) inside fragments.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-very-tight-sans-display-ladder`, Very tight sans, graduated ladder): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one medium (400-500) sans with tracking of -0.04 to -0.06em on every display step, even the 33px one; body stays near -0.01 to -0.02em for reading. Six steps from 10-12vw (144-175px at 1440, leading 0.8-0.95) through 107 / 80 / 53 / 33px, then 17px body, all in mixed case; headlines two lines at most. Ratio 7-13x. The signature is the tracking, and the steps are graduated: hero 144-175px, section titles 80-107px, card titles 33px. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card or button fill beyond the dealt button. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; link hover changes colour. House rules for it: Clean after the pass: tracking is the one signature; entrance as dealt; card titles at the sub step, not display size. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

```css
:root {
  --bg: #eef0f3;
  --surface: #d8dbe0;
  --ink: #0b0c0e;
  --muted: #575e6a;
  --accent: #1f2de6;
  --accent-ink: #ffffff;
  --field-1: #1f2de6;  --field-1-ink: #ffffff;

```
Cool pale grey holds the page with near-black type and nothing else tinted. Cobalt starts as the button and one highlighted sentence, takes one whole section as a flat field with white text, then returns as a single button block.
Neutral ground. The product's own interface colours provide the colour. At most one flat stage colour behind fragments.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

Where no honest picture exists, leave the slot to type and space; never a drawn illustration in its place (references/imagery.md §8). List every picture and its source in your report.

**What the pictures show for this brief:** the subject's world: its place, its materials and the details that belong to no one. **Never:** another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

Fewer photographs, chosen better: no more of the subject and its world than the photograph total below allows (the rooms, dishes or products a page pictures are their own set, one photograph each beside its facts), each one modern, well lit and professional-looking (the quality of a good Unsplash or Pexels photograph), or none at all, with the product, the type, the colour and the interface carrying the page. **Never documentary or archive photographs:** no old Commons or museum pictures, no history photographs, postcards, old maps or engravings, no news-like or snapshot-looking shots; they made recent pages look serious, old and cheap, and the owner named them first. ## 6. Hero — Words beside the product
The content width is split about 5 to 7. The headline, one sentence and the action sit in the narrower part, vertically centred or aligned to the top of the picture; the headline is large, 5vw or more (72px at 1440) on two lines at most, since a small headline beside a half-screen picture reads as a shop template. The wider part holds the product, the object or the real place, large, from the split to the right margin at the full height of the first screen. When the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead, with the words on its calm side. Nothing else on the first screen.

On a phone: Words first, then the picture at full width.
Headline: What it is, two to five words, at most about 26 characters.
**Must show:** The content width split about 5 to 7: a headline of 72px or more beside the product, the object or the place at the full height of the screen.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**A split hero takes a large headline:** beside a picture that holds half the screen, the `h1` is 72px (5vw) or more at 1440, on two lines at most, so about 26-30 characters. If the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead; never a small headline in a 50/50 split, which reads as a shop template.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (400), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro statement, features grid, showcase split, cta the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — Stacked headline with the last word rising right (statement) — `sec-statement-stacked-headline-last-word-rising-right`, a pattern of the design-anatomy study
The hand-over scene `line-carries-colour-swap` (section 9) opens this section; its "Where" line says what of the section it carries, and the rest follows in this shape.
A pattern of the study standing in for this section's blueprint: its description is the spec, built with this page's content, in the dealt type, colour, corners, card style and button, within the word and picture budgets above.
Ground = the palette ground, ink in contrast. A four-word headline at the display step in the dealt display weight on two lines: line one holds words 1-2 flush left at the margin; line two holds word 3 flush left and word 4 right-aligned to the container's right margin. Word 4 rises about 120px into place over about 80svh of scroll (scroll-linked, translateY 120 to 0) so it arrives last. Under word 3 at the left, 32px below the headline, a paragraph at the lead step 360px wide in three or four lines and, 32px under it, the dealt button, so no row is empty at the left. Phone: the headline in two lines at the head step with the last word right-aligned, the paragraph and the full-width dealt button under it. House rules for it: The words stay inside the page margins; the last word is the same style as the rest, never an accent word; the paragraph fills the left of the second row, so no row is empty on the left.

### 2. features — One-size name columns (grid)
Pure text at one size. Three columns start on columns 1, 4 and 7 of the 12-column grid, leaving columns 10–12 empty on purpose. Each column opens with a group name in full ink and continues with a tight stack of six to ten capability names in the soft ink, all at 16–17px, regular weight, line-height 1.25, no bullets and no symbols; one blank line separates group name from stack. Hovering a name brings it to full ink and shows a one-line description in a fixed spot: the top of the empty columns 10–12, at the same size. The heading is no larger than 28px; two or three words, alone on column 1, 96px above the columns. Give the section 20vh of empty ground above and below, so the smallness reads as decided. Works on light or deep grounds.
On a phone: Columns stack with 40px between groups; text rises to 18px with line-height 1.4 for touch. Group names stay in full ink. Hover descriptions are dropped; each group ends instead with one sentence in the soft ink that sums it up. The heading is 24px, 48px above.

### 3. showcase — Two objects, two fields (split)
Two panels side by side, each 50vw wide and 92svh tall, in two different flat tones from the palette, meeting on a clean vertical edge with no gap. Each panel is dominated by one cut-out object photograph scaled to 130–150% of the panel's width and positioned so the panel's top edge crops it: the reader sees the lower two-thirds of the object, very close. On light panels use mix-blend-mode: multiply to lose a white studio background. Along the bottom of each panel, 28px in from the edges, one row: the name at 20px medium and a price or fact at 17px on the left; on the right, two small rectangular actions, one filled with ink and one with paper. Nothing sits in the middle of a panel. The heading, two to four words at 32–40px, is set at the top-left of the first panel only, in the empty corner beside the object.
On a phone: Panels stack, each 78svh tall, the object scaled to 150% of the width, centred, and cut by the panel's top, left and right edges alike. The bottom row becomes two lines: name and fact, then the two actions side by side at full width. The heading sits top-left on the first panel at 26px.

Adjusted for this page:
- It asks for a photograph, and the page's photograph total leaves none here (section 5): build it from its words, the colour and the dealt card style, with no picture.

### 4. cta — the scene `page-lifts-off-closing-picture` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the cta) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
A newsletter block on the page ground: one filled card on the surface tone with the dealt corner, whatever the page's card style, across eight columns, holding one sentence at the h3 step and an email field with its button in one row; beside it, on four columns, the contact and two or three links; the legal line and Image credits under both. It is the page's one email form. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Edge-to-edge marquee
One line of very large type travels sideways across the full width at a slow constant speed, as a band between two sections or as the closing statement. Linear timing, duplicated content for a seamless loop, paused on hover and under reduced motion. One marquee per page, carrying real words, never a list of buzzwords. Its words are new information (the services, the formats, the clients the page can name), never the hero's line again.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Line blocks (`line-blocks`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Each line of the headline starts as a solid block of one palette colour, exactly as long as its words, and the blocks slide off toward the line ends one after another. The words never move. The picture is in place and still. Settled by 1.1 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="line-blocks">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must): a line that wraps gets one block across the whole column, and the inspector fails it. The blocks are the ink: put `--block: var(--ink)` on the body.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `line-blocks` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=line-blocks`.
- **Below the fold: Rows step in.** Lists, indexes, tables and price rows (data-reveal="rows" on the list) go from 40% to full opacity in reading order, once: the first four 60 ms apart, the rest together. No travel, nothing else enters, and no blocks below the fold.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Brackets close in** (`mo-text-bracket-close-in`): A single word or two short words set large as the section's statement (display step, display face, ink, centred), flanked by two bracket glyphs from the same face at 1.2x the word's height. Scrubbed by scroll (from 85% to 50% of the viewport height) the brackets start 300px further out on each side and slide inward until they sit 0.2em from the word; the word itself is always fully visible. After landing everything holds still; a paragraph (lead step, 44 characters) sits 56px under it. Ground as dealt, accent none. Phone: word at the head step, brackets travel 120px. House rules for it: Never a small bracketed label above a heading (an eyebrow): the brackets are typographic marks around the large statement word itself; the page's own face, never a monospace; brackets stay ink, not a styled accent; scroll-linked, so the dealt entrance stands. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Late word slides in** (`mo-parallax-word-slides-into-line`): A hero headline of three to five words at the display step over two lines. The last word starts about 96px below its line, fully visible from the first frame, so the sentence already reads whole; across 60svh of scroll, in a hero held that long, it slides up (scrubbed, eased) into its line and closes the gap. Every word shares one face, colour and weight. The hero still fills the first screen alone. On a phone the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. House rules for it: The sliding word has exactly the same face, colour, weight and style as the rest (no accent word); it is a real word of the sentence; the hero still fills the first screen alone and reads whole in the first frame. On a phone: Display step on the phone; the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On the hero's headline it runs on scroll only, once the entrance has set the headline in place. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Rounded tile goes edge** (`mo-scale-rounded-tile-widen`): A picture tile (16:9 or 21:9, 1370px wide at 1440) sits inset by the page margin on each side with the dealt corner as the section arrives. Over the next 650-720px of scroll (while it is sticky, or as its top travels from 90 to 30 percent of the viewport) it widens to the full 1440px: clip-path inset(0 var(--margin) round var(--radius)) to inset(0 0 round 0), the height holding or growing from 80svh to 100svh. The corner eases to 0 so it ends edge to edge. Text for the section sits below the tile and is simply there (the dealt entrance decides whether it arrives); no caption sits on the picture. Phone: inset by the page margin with the dealt corner, widening to the full 390 over 60 percent of the scroll distance. House rules for it: A picture tile in the dealt corner, no outline, no caption box on the photograph; the pin, if any, is under two screens. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, tested and ready to fill. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 2 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### One line carries the colour swap (`line-carries-colour-swap`) — the hand-over into the intro section
A straight-edged photograph that closes one chapter is cropped away from the bottom by a square clip while one very large line of the page's display face climbs under it, already at its final size and the full content width but showing only its first word; the next chapter's picture starts rising under it at once, so the first word is never alone on flat colour; each further word slides in from the right with a short slide that lands it beside the word before, and on the last word the stage's ground and the line's ink swap in one jump (ink to paper, paper to ink, never a blend) while the next chapter's first picture, already rising under the line, settles whole beneath it. It turns a change of chapter or of mood into one precise moment, in any sector. Hold: 1 screen. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: Between two sections, as the hand-over from a dark chapter (the opening, the work, the evening) to a light one (the detail, the about, the order), or the other way with direction 'to-ink'. The section before sits on the first ground and ends on the photograph; the section after continues on the second ground straight under the picture, and the line is its heading. Once per page.
Content: One straight-edged landscape photograph that closes the section before (3:2 or wider, 1920px or more, dark and calm enough to end the ink chapter). One picture that opens the section after: it rests whole under the line, so it must crop well both wide (about 2.2:1 on desktop) and tall (about 2:3 on phones), 2400px or more. One line of 3-5 words, 14-24 characters, in the line's one colour; a full stop only where the page's voice uses them everywhere. Two grounds that contrast 7:1 or better both ways (the page's ink and paper). Nothing else on the stage: no labels, captions or buttons. Without a closing photograph and an opening picture, redraw: the line alone on flat colour reads as an empty screen.
Options (data-options): hold 1 (1-1.5); phoneHold 1 (1-1.5); direction 'to-paper' (ink ground to paper, paper line to ink) | 'to-ink' (the reverse; add class lcs--to-ink to the section too); slide 0.6 (0.3-1) how far a word slides in, as a share of its own width, never more than 1.1 times the type size; min 0.08 (0.05-0.12) share of the stage height under which a one-row line takes two rows; max 0.3 (0.2-0.4) share of the stage height the line's box never passes; drift 0.05 (0-0.1) how far the old photograph keeps rising while it is cropped; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-swapped', the class it gets from the swap on (style it to swap the bar's own ground and ink in the same jump); scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --lcs-top, --lcs-foot, --lcs-gap, --lcs-size (no script), --lcs-photo-pos, --lcs-next-pos, --lcs-photo-h, --lcs-next-h.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/line-carries-colour-swap.js` and `line-carries-colour-swap.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/line-carries-colour-swap.html`. On a phone: The same scene with phone numbers: the line breaks into two rows where they are most even (never three) and is fitted to the full width, the words still landing one by one; the next picture crops tall under it; hold 1. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

### Page lifts off a closing picture (`page-lifts-off-closing-picture`) — carries the cta section
The last content section slides up like a sheet, at its full width and in its own ground, and uncovers a picture that was held underneath it all along; while the sheet clears the screen the picture settles from a close 1.35x zoom to its natural framing, landing exactly as the scroll runs out. A solid band across the top third carries the closing line, a headline at display size, and the contact block in the body face, in the picture's own top-edge colour, so band and picture meet with no seam, and the whole picture is the page's one action with a plain label at its foot. The picture is a photograph, or a full-bleed close crop of the product's interface built in HTML (data-ui) whose app bar takes the band's colour, so a page can close on its product rather than a photograph. Hold: 1 screen. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: The end of the page: the contact or the call to action, straight after the last content section, which becomes the sheet that lifts (give it a screen of height or more). The footer after it is a designed section of its own and follows the finished frame: leave `foot` unset (it pulls the element it names over the stage's foot, so it suits only a page that ends on the scene). Once per page, and never right after another scene: the section that lifts is the quiet section between them.
Content: One picture and one line. Photo mode: one photograph 2400px wide or more (3000+ for full sharpness at the 1.35x start), its top edge even (fog, sky, a plain wall, a table top) and its subject in the lower half; a busy or textured top edge shows the seam, so smooth it or redraw. Interface mode: a close crop of the real product in HTML with data-ui, full bleed with no card, frame or rounded edge: an app bar in var(--lift-band-c) (so it meets the band with no seam), a title and two to four rows at true size (labels 14px or more at 1440, any heading in it under 24px), believable figures that add up, one item picked; side padding var(--lift-ui-inset). Its words end in the top ~37% of the stage (about 335px at 1440x900) because it grows 1.35x from its top edge and must never pass under the label; the rest is the product's own empty surface, where the label sits. Its CSS under [data-ui]. Words: a closing line of 3-7 words (h2, display face at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) over seven columns, two lines at most), a contact block of one to three short paragraphs at body size (address, email and phone, hours; never a giant name or email), and a label of 1-3 words for the one action. --lift-band set to the photograph's top-edge sample at 1440x900 for no script (the script samples it live), or to a tone step of --ground in interface mode.
Options (data-options): mode 'photo' | 'ui' (default from data-ui on .lift-media); settle 1.35 (1.1-1.5) the start scale, 1 at the end of the scroll; phoneSettle 1.18 (1.05-1.3); lift 1 (0.9-1) the sheet's scale as it leaves (under 1 pulls its sides in over the picture: old pages only); dim 0 (0-0.3) how much it darkens (over 0 turns the page's ground into a grey slab: old pages only); band 'auto' (the median of the photograph's top edge as cropped on this screen; same-origin pictures, or CORS ones with crossorigin="anonymous") | 'token' (--lift-band; the default in ui mode) | a CSS colour, and a dark band turns its words to --ground; focus the settle's transform-origin ('50% 0%', so the seam row never changes, in both modes); label 'foot' | 'centre'; sheet a selector for the section that lifts (default the element before the scene); foot unset by default (the designed footer follows the scene); a selector pulls that element over the picture's foot so the scroll ends on the settled frame, only for a page that ends on the scene with one plain line; scrub true (locked) or 0.1-1 s; phone 'same' | 'rest'. The hold is not an option: it is the lift, the sheet's height up to one screen. Each word block lands whole on a short clock once the sheet's edge has cleared it.
Leave `foot` unset: the footer dealt in section 0 (`newsletter-card`) follows the held stage as its own section, after the section gap, and is never pulled over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/page-lifts-off-closing-picture.js` and `page-lifts-off-closing-picture.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/page-lifts-off-closing-picture.html`. On a phone: The same lift: the picture stays held under the page while it slides away at its full width, with a gentler 1.18x settle. The band grows with its words (a 32-40px title over a two-column contact block, about 32-37% of the screen, the words low in it so the title lands by about 0.9 of the lift) instead of squeezing them, the label sits just above the footer line, and the interface crop stays full bleed but drops to the columns that fit, its words inset by --lift-ui-inset so none leaves the screen at the start scale. phone: 'rest' gives the finished composition.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Fidelity. A fragment with believable content and a real hover state is more interesting than any illustration. Spend the effort there.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A fake interface made of grey bars and circles is the worst outcome; if you cannot build a believable fragment, change direction. Mark each fragment's root element with the attribute data-ui: inside it the product's own conventions apply (1px control borders, small radii, a shadow on its open menu) and the inspector leaves them alone; outside it the house rules hold as usual. data-ui is for cropped fragments only, never for a page wrapper. Do not put the fragment in a browser or laptop frame.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
A name the brief does not give (the client's, the product's) is coined, then searched with the trade's word ("<name> notes app") and as the domain it would use ("<name>.com", "<name>.app") before use, and replaced if a product or company of that trade has it or the domain is a live site: two coined names in tests were real apps of the same kind, and one linked a real product's address.
