## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`shrinks`):** A bar that changes on scroll: over the hero it is 104-120px tall with the wordmark at the h2 step and the links at body size; after 80px of scroll it settles into a 56-64px band of the ground (or the surface tone) with the wordmark at the h4 step, by transform alone (scale and translate, 300ms), and stays fixed. Never fades to glass. On a phone: 72px with the wordmark at the h4 step and a Menu button, settling to 56px the same way. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** A 104-120px bar with the wordmark at the h2 step over the hero that settles into a fixed 56-64px band after 80px of scroll.
- **Footer (`two-halves`):** Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** Two halves meeting on the centre line: the closing line and button on the ink at the left, the links and contact on the surface tone at the right.

## 1. The idea
Art-direct the subject as if it belonged to a different, more vivid category. A money app is sold like a streetwear drop. An industrial roaster is presented like a design object. Drilling hardware gets the calm of a watch brand. A parcel-locker service becomes a fairground of stickers. Choose the borrowed category by asking what feeling the audience is missing from this kind of product (swagger, calm, glamour, play) and which category owns that feeling. Then take its manners completely: its type, its pacing, its way of photographing things. The contrast between dull subject and vivid dress is what people remember.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Depends on the borrowed category, so decide it explicitly and write it in your notes. Fashion drop: one shouted condensed headline across the full width, a single cropped image under it, then product facts as a blunt list. Watch or perfume: a centred object on a dark field, a small serif name, very few words, long vertical pauses. Poster or zine: flat colour blocks, heavy type, tilted cut-out images. Museum: one item per screen on a plinth-like band of colour with a short label beside it. Whichever you choose, the page structure stays simple (four to six sections) and every section uses the borrowed manners, including pricing and the footer.

## 3. Type — Varela Round over Onest
A rounded sans. A 1.25 scale from a 18px body; the hero's display step is about 4.4vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Varela Round only for the display and head steps; Onest for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Varela+Round:wght@400&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Onest:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Varela Round** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking -0.02em, leading 1.01
- Text: **Onest** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.9000rem;
  --step-body: 1.1250rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.266rem, 0.759rem + 0.63vw, 1.406rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.510rem, 0.906rem + 0.94vw, 1.887rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.887rem, 1.132rem + 1.41vw, 2.531rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.025rem, 1.215rem + 2.73vw, 3.962rem);
}
```
The borrowed category's type, fully committed: condensed and loud, or refined and small. No halfway.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

## 6. Hero — Counter object, wide wordmark
Borrowed from a watch or perfume counter. One flat charcoal field. Centred slightly above the midline, a single low-key photograph of the object about 31% of the viewport wide (columns 5-8), 4:5, its own dark background matched to the field with filter: brightness(0.9) so it sits without a frame. Under it the name runs on one line as an ultra-wide wordmark spanning 72% of the width yet only about 45px tall: an extended face with letter-spacing near 0.6em, or a regular face stretched with transform: scaleX(2.4). Then one centred serif sentence at clamp(24px, 2.6vw, 38px). Then a single centred row at 15px with 48px between its three parts: one specification, the price inside the one solid light rectangle that is also the action, a second specification. The wordmark repeats small top-left and one text link sits top-right, both on 32px margins. The bottom fifth and both outer quarters of the screen stay empty and dark.

On a phone: The photograph grows to 70% of the width at 4:5, its top at 16% of the height. The wide wordmark spans the full width inside 20px margins at about 28px tall. The serif sentence sets at 6vw on two lines. The row stacks into three centred lines with the action in the middle at full width. The lowest sixth of the screen stays dark and empty.
Headline: The name as a very wide, very low wordmark on one line (up to 12 characters); the serif sentence beneath is the supporting line, up to 40 characters.
**Must show:** One object photograph centred on a dark field, and under it the name as an ultra-wide wordmark across 72% of the width and only about 45px tall.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro grid, features band, showcase single-picture. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — Lead line over four columns (grid)
The hand-over scene `lib-0312` (section 9) opens this section; its direction says what of the section it carries, and the rest follows in this shape.
Two parts on one 12-column grid with 20px margins. First, a lead paragraph of 30–40 words spans all 12 columns at clamp(22px, 2.2vw, 32px), regular weight, leading 1.2: three lines that read as the opening of a text, with no heading before it. 72px below, four equal columns (three grid columns each, 24px gutters) carry small text at 14–15px with leading 1.45. Each column is one paragraph of 40–70 words that opens with a bold run-in phrase of two or three words in full ink, the rest continuing on the same line in the soft ink. Columns top-align and end ragged at different depths. No cards, icons or dividers; the bold run-ins are the only markers. The four columns answer four plain questions: who, what, where, how to reach them. Padding is modest, 96px top and bottom, so the next section can start straight after.
On a phone: The lead paragraph at 20px, full width. The four columns become a two-by-two grid with a 20px gutter and 40px row gap at 14px; if any paragraph exceeds 60 words, switch to one column with 32px between paragraphs. Run-in phrases stay bold.

### 2. features — Statements on colour fields (band)

The benefit is a phrase of two to four words at clamp(56px, 9vw, 150px), heavy, leading 0.9, on two lines at most, hugging the bottom-left corner with 28px margins. ## 8. Motion — Opening window
A type screen hands over to the one great photograph: the picture first shows as a window about 45% of the width in the middle of a held stage and opens to full-bleed over about one screen of scroll, while the image inside settles from 1.15 scale. Scroll-linked and linear, so the reader sets the pace; it is fully open after three quarters of the hold and rests a moment before the page moves on. Use it once, for the picture that carries the subject. Where scroll timelines are unsupported, or under reduced motion, the picture is simply a full-bleed screen. Recipe: 'Opening window' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.
It holds the stage about 1 screen: one of this page's held stretches, counted with the scenes in section 9, and no section beside it pins.

### Entrance — Line blocks (`line-blocks`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Each line of the headline starts as a solid block of one palette colour, exactly as long as its words, and the blocks slide off toward the line ends one after another. The words never move. The picture is in place and still. Settled by 1.1 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="line-blocks">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must): a line that wraps gets one block across the whole column, and the inspector fails it. The blocks are the accent (the default `--block`).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `line-blocks` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=line-blocks`.
- **Below the fold: Rows step in.** Lists, indexes, tables and price rows (data-reveal="rows" on the list) go from 40% to full opacity in reading order, once: the first four 60 ms apart, the rest together. No travel, nothing else enters, and no blocks below the fold.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Char blur scrub** (`mo-text-char-blur-scrub`): A short headline of at most 10 words at the head step on two lines (or a statement of 14 words at most at 36px or less on three lines), ink on the ground, split into characters. It is scroll-linked (0.6 smoothing) over 400-720px of scroll as the block travels from 85 to 40 percent of the viewport: each character comes into focus from blur 4-12px to blur 0, staggered left to right and line by line so the focus runs like a wave of three to five letters. The text is in full ink and in place from the first frame (nothing fades or moves in; arrival is the dealt entrance's): only the focus changes. On the way out the same values reverse once the block passes 20 percent from the top. It is fully crisp before it has to be read. One type step, no colour change per character. Phone: blur peaks at 8px, splitting limited to 80 characters to protect the frame rate; the parent keeps an aria-label with the whole text. House rules for it: No accent word, no coloured letters; letters never move or fade in, only their focus changes, and the real words are present whole; the headline is two lines at most or the statement 36px or less on three lines. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Grows from small** (`mo-scale-grow-from-small-on-entry`): A vertical list of 3-5 rows, each one photograph (a frame full width inside the page margins, about 1296x680 at 1440, the dealt corner) with its title at the display step, one line, above it. As a row enters, the frame scales from 0.2 to 1.0 about its centre (scrubbed, 0.4s smoothing) between 100% and 55% of the viewport height, so it opens like a window from a thumbnail to full width; the title is fixed in place and unaffected. Inside the frame the image sits at 112% height and drifts 6% vertically as the row passes. A one-line caption (body step) sits bottom-left under the frame. The dealt section space between rows. Large scale range, no pin. Ground and ink as dealt, accent none. Phone: scale 0.5 to 1.0, drift 4%, frame 4:5 full width. House rules for it: No numbering on rows; no overlay boxes on the photographs; captions sit on the ground, not on the picture; one title per row, two lines at most. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, a researched technique to build fresh. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 1 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall); the signature move (Opening window) holds about 1 more. A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The example is not the content.** The direction a technique gives is written for another client: its business, its objects and its words are examples. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the example's content is unfinished.

### A full-screen veil hides the swap of the whole palette (`lib-0312`) — a technique to build fresh, the hand-over into the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. A flat full-screen sheet comes up to fully opaque at the exact moment a chapter ends, the page's ground, ink and chrome swap roles underneath it while nothing can be seen, and the sheet then thins away to leave the visitor standing in the new palette, so a change of world is hidden behind one beat instead of blended or wiped, and the colour never passes through an in-between.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** A bright full-screen flash from near-black is hard on the eyes and on photosensitive visitors, so it needs a reduced-motion path and must never loop; an accent sheet and a content-break placement soften it. It depends on two grounds that contrast. Anyone who jumps by keyboard or an anchor link lands past the veil, so the ground must be tied to scroll position, not to time. The line is only seen by people who stop, so it must be dispensable. Sections with their own opaque backgrounds do not follow the swap. Fails if the brief wants a gradual colour ramp: the hand-off is a cut, so slow scrolling never produces an in-between colour. With fewer than four chapters the swap does not read as a pattern. Built in WebGL it is heavy and wants a laptop; the custom-property version is light and works on phones.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** Make the veil the page's single accent colour, not white: a flat sheet that rises from the bottom edge like a tide line (clip-path inset from 100 percent at the top to 0, scrubbed over about 150 px of scroll), covering the fixed header as well. While it is fully opaque, swap ground and ink instead of adding a second ground: an ink-on-paper page for the first half becomes paper-on-ink after it, and the header flips with it. Pick the swap point at a content break (between the studio's case studies and its contact section) rather than after a hero. The sheet then clears over about 400 px, fastest in the middle, and if the visitor stops on it one short plain line about the next section appears in the middle of the sheet. Reverse on scroll up. The chrome re-skins on the same beat (the header ink, the button fill), and the swap is used once or twice per page at the edges that mean something, never on every section. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 1 screen of scroll: `hold: 1` in `Stage.scene`, its section 2 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** the same move at phone size (`phone: 'same'`). Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 1, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).
