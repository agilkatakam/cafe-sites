## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`nav-tall-wordmark-bar-collapses`, Tall wordmark bar that collapses: a pattern of the study):** A fixed bar that starts tall and collapses on scroll. At rest it is 96px high with no fill over the hero: a typographic wordmark at the left (about 212px wide, the display face at the head step in the heaviest dealt weight, type only, never drawn), four one-word links at body size (16px), weight 600, 32px apart, and at the right margin the one action as the dealt button in the accent, 44px tall (on a shop the basket as a word before it). Over the first 120px of scroll the bar eases to 64px and takes a solid band of the page ground; the wordmark scales and cross-fades (200ms) into a 40px monogram chip, and the four links fold into Menu as the dealt button in the ink beside the action, so content never runs under bare letters. Where the hero's headline is the name, the first screen shows the monogram chip alone. On a phone: a 72px bar with the wordmark about 150px wide, a Menu text button and the action, collapsing to 56px with the monogram the same way. House rules for it: No announcement line above the bar; the wordmark is type only (no drawn mascot or illustration); the bar is 96px, about a tenth of the first screen; Menu and the action are the dealt button, not outlined icon buttons or a second shape; the collapsed bar is opaque. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** On the first screen the 96px bar carries a head-size type wordmark and four links; over 120px of scroll it collapses to a 64px solid band where the wordmark has become a 40px monogram chip and the links have folded into Menu.
- **Footer (`closing-columns`):** A closing line over columns: the closing line at the h2 step (the action as a statement, four to seven words, two lines at most) with the one button under it; 96px below, three or four short columns at body size: the sections, the contact (the email, a phone only if the brief gives one), the place or the social links, the legal links with Image credits; the © line at the foot. On the surface tone, as tall as its content, about 60svh at 1440, so the line, the button and the columns share one screen. On a phone the columns stack in pairs. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** A closing line at the h2 step with its one button, then three or four short columns 96px below, on the surface tone, about 60svh, all on one screen.
- **Cards (`dealt-deck`):** Cards laid on the table like a dealt hand: three to five filled cards with the dealt corner in one row, centred, 40px apart, each turned about its centre by an angle fixed for its place (-2.5°, 1.5°, -1°, 2°, -1.5°), never overlapping, their corners 16px apart or more and inside the margins. In turn the ink (lettered in the ground colour) and the surface tone, the first in the ink. Each holds, 28px in, the name at the h2 step in the display face's heaviest weight, one line at body size and the price or the fact at the h4 step at its foot, as tall as its words; where the items have pictures, the picture inset 16px at the top at 4:3. The deal plays once as the row arrives (the cards fly from one squared pile to their places, 90ms apart), and a still shows them dealt. Hover or focus straightens a card and raises it 8px. For the three things to try, the dishes of the day, the hosts, a small range. On a phone one card to a row at the content width less 16px, at a third of the angle. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Three to five cards lying at small fixed angles in one row, in turn the ink and the surface tone, each name at the h2 step in the heaviest weight.
- **Buttons (`soft-accent`):** Soft rectangles: 8px radius, 44-48px tall, 20px padding, the text face at 15-16px weight 600, filled with the accent; hover lightens the fill one step, press darkens it. The second action is the same shape in the surface tone with ink letters. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** 8px-radius buttons 44-48px tall in the accent; the second action the same shape in the surface tone.
- **Corners:** A 16px radius on cards and panels; pictures at 16px or square inside a card. (`--radius: 16px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(6rem, 9vh + 5vw, 11.25rem); --margin: clamp(1.25rem, 3vw, 3rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** One working tool the visitor would really use, built from the subject's content (a price or size calculator, a booking with real dates, a filter over the real list). Not a toy, not a demo of the page's own design. It works on a phone with a thumb and has a plain fallback.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts

## 1. The idea
Take one shape from the subject and let it run the whole system. A drilling tool's octagonal cross-section becomes the buttons, the image masks and the loader. A two-account money app becomes two circles that recur as icon, inline dot and section colour. A café's striped awning becomes every band on the page. Look at the product, the logo, the object or the word in the headline and find the simplest geometry in it (a circle, an arc, a chamfer, a stripe, a notch, a ring). Then use only that shape, at three or four very different scales, everywhere a designer would normally reach for a generic rounded rectangle.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: the motif appears once at enormous scale as a flat filled shape, cropped by the viewport (a circle 120vh across entering from the right, a chamfered block filling the lower half), with a short headline at 6–8vw and one supporting sentence set on the remaining ground, left-aligned on a shared margin. Through the page the motif returns as: the mask for every photograph (via `clip-path` or `border-radius` made from the same geometry), the shape of the primary button, the bullet beside list items at text size, and one full-width band that separates the two halves of the page. Sections use simple two-column or single-column layouts so the shape is the only thing with character.

## 3. Type — Josefin Slab, a slab
A slab. A 1.25 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 7.6vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Josefin Slab does every job, told apart by size and weight (500 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Josefin+Slab:wght@400;500;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Josefin Slab** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.01em, leading 0.98
- Text: **Josefin Slab** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.125rem, 0.675rem + 0.56vw, 1.250rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.340rem, 0.804rem + 0.84vw, 1.675rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.675rem, 1.005rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.872rem, 1.723rem + 4.71vw, 6.838rem);
}
```
A clean geometric or grotesk face that echoes the shape (round bowls for circles, squared terminals for chamfers). Medium weight; headings 6–8vw in the hero and 3–4vw after.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-single-book-serif-low-contrast`, One book serif, flat scale): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one book serif (transitional or old-style) at 400 with a true italic kept for whole quotes. The top step is only 40-56px (2.75-3.9vw at 1440), leading 1.0-1.1, tracking -0.01em, two lines at most; then 44 / 32 / 24px, body 18px at leading 1.5, small 14px. Ratio 1.5-2.8x. Almost no size contrast, so rank comes from space, position and cards on the one surface tone; headings in sentence or title case. A small sans at 14px in sentence case for captions is optional. It reads like a printed book, so the dealt hero's large photograph has to fill the first screen. Colour: the dealt ground and ink, ink one step off full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; hover darkens ink. House rules for it: Italics only for whole quotes, never for a word in a headline; no paper-and-terracotta pairing: ground and accent are the dealt ones. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

## 4. Colour — Graphite and orange
```css
:root {
  --bg: #18191b;
  --surface: #2a2c2f;
  --ink: #eff0f1;
  --muted: #9c9ea3;
  --accent: #ff4a1f;
  --accent-ink: #18191b;
  --field-1: #eff0f1;  --field-1-ink: #18191b;
}
```
Graphite and cool light-grey sections alternate, graphite taking about two thirds, and photographs are chosen calm and low in colour so nothing competes (in full colour where the pictures carry the colour; greyscale only where section 0 deals black-and-white). Safety orange marks the buttons, one thick solid bar across the top of the page and the highlighted item in a list or chart.
The motif carries the accent colour at large scale, flat. Everything else is ground and ink. A second motif colour is allowed only if the subject has a real pair (two accounts, two modes).
**The brief names "ice", "midnight".** Where they describe the client or what it makes (its name, its material, its light) rather than something it replaces or works on, keep the proportions above and take the colours from what they name where the dealt ones do not fit (the material for the ground, a strong hue for the accent), and say so in the report.
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

Where no honest picture exists, leave the slot to type and space; never a drawn illustration in its place (references/imagery.md §8). List every picture and its source in your report.

**What the pictures show for this brief:** the fresh juice's world: its place, its materials and the details that belong to no one; and Gokul Nagar as it is today. **Never:** another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

### Letter bar floods the ground (`letter-bar-floods-the-ground`) — the hand-over into the intro section

The next section's opening statement, 4-8 words in two lines at most at every width, and an optional sentence of 12-24 words under it, both in the ground's colour on the ink. One ground and one ink, 7:1 or better both ways; no accent, no second colour on any word.
Options (data-options): hold 1.1 (1-1.5); phoneHold 1 (0.8-1.2); pivot, the letter's index in the statement (spaces counted) or the letter itself (a span.lbf-pivot in the markup wins; else the straight letter nearest the middle); third 0.3 (0.2-0.4) share of the hold by which the stem is a third of the width; half 0.5 (0.4-0.6) share by which it is half the width (the next statement arrives about here); flood 0.8 (0.6-0.8) share by which the column fills the screen; grow 0.5 (0.3-0.8) the words' scale as a power of the letter's; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-flooded', which it gets while the ink covers it; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --lbf-size, --lbf-gutter, --lbf-next-size, --lbf-next-measure, --lbf-pad.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.js` and `letter-bar-floods-the-ground.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.html`. On a phone: The same zoom with phone numbers: the line spans nearly the whole width round its stem (a 12px gutter); the letter stays a letter, ends in view, until it is taller than the screen (about 0.55, near 30 times its size, repainted sharp rather than stretched from a small layer); the stem is a third of the width by 0.3 with the outgoing words' edges in frame; full bleed by 0.8; hold 1. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
One shape at many sizes. The giant cropped shape gives the hero mass and colour without a single image.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
One motif means one. A circle here, a hexagon there and a blob behind is the generated look this replaces. The motif must come from the subject and you must be able to say from where in one clause.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
