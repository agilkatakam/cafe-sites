## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`ink-band`):** A band in the ink: full width, 60-64px, filled with the page's ink colour (on a dark page the surface tone), the wordmark, links and action in the ground colour, the action a button of the ground colour with ink letters. It stays fixed at the top on every screen, a solid frame for the page. On a phone the band is 56px with the wordmark and a Menu button in the ground colour; Menu opens the links full-screen on the ink. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** A full-width 60-64px band filled with the ink, its wordmark, links and action in the ground colour, fixed on every screen.
- **Footer (`statement-and-row`):** A closing statement: one line of five to eight words (the action as a statement) at the display step held to 6vw at most, two lines at most, with the button 32px under it, on the ground; then 96px of space and one band of the surface tone, 120-160px tall, with the links on the left, the contact in the middle, the © line and Image credits on the right. As tall as its content, about 60svh, so the statement, its button and the band share one screen. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** One line at the display step (6vw at most) with its button on the ground, then 96px of space and one band of the surface tone with the links, contact and legal line, all on one screen.
- **Cards (`sticky-stack`):** Cards that stack as you scroll: three to five cards across the content width (a corner of 16px or more), in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so the edge of the card sliding up reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone; each about 70svh tall with its heading at the h2 step, its picture or figure and two lines of text; each sticks 96px below the top as the next slides up over it, the one beneath scaling to 0.96. Native scroll (position: sticky), no pin; reduced motion and phones show them one after another. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Cards about 70svh tall, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each sticking 96px below the top as the next slides over it.
- **Buttons (`circle-arrow`):** A label with a round arrow: the label at 18-20px in the ink, weight 500, then 12px on a 52-56px circle filled with the accent holding an arrow in the accent's ink; label and circle are one link, 56px tall; hover turns the arrow 45° and slides the label 4px. The second action is the label alone, without the circle. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A label followed by a 52-56px circle in the accent holding an arrow that turns 45 degrees on hover; label and circle are one link.
- **Corners:** A 10px radius on cards, panels and pictures alike. (`--radius: 10px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Airy: 22-28vh between sections (200-250px at 1440, 112-140px on a phone), page margins 48-72px at 1440 and 24px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(7rem, 12vh + 7vw, 15.75rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4.5vw, 4.5rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** One working tool the visitor would really use, built from the subject's content (a price or size calculator, a booking with real dates, a filter over the real list). Not a toy, not a demo of the page's own design. It works on a phone with a thumb and has a plain fallback.
- **Video:** One photograph that moves slowly as the page scrolls (a scale or drift of a few percent), once.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

## 1. The idea
The first screen is made only of words, colour and space, good enough that nobody misses a picture there. Pictures arrive later, large and few, as relief from the type. This is the honest answer when a subject has nothing worth photographing: instead of inventing illustrations, set what you have to say as well as it can be set. The typography carries a point of view through scale (one enormous statement, everything else small), through measure (a narrow, bookish column or a full-width line), and through one committed colour.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the statement, two lines at most, set at 9–14vw and occupying the full width with tight leading, positioned in the upper two-thirds. One supporting sentence at 20–24px sits bottom-left on the same margin and the single action sits bottom-right on the same baseline. No navigation bar; two or three links in a top corner. Sections that follow are typographic set pieces, each different: a list of five to eight capabilities set at 4–6vw one per line; a paragraph at 28–36px in a 28ch column pushed to the right half; a pair of large numerals with a sentence each; a single quotation filling a colour field. Each section uses a different horizontal position (left, right, full) so the page has movement without imagery.

## 3. Type — Saira Condensed over Archivo
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.618 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 8.3vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Saira Condensed only for the display and head steps; Archivo for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Saira+Condensed:wght@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Archivo:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Saira Condensed** — weight 700, set in capitals, tracking 0.02em, leading 0.87
- Text: **Archivo** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(2.095rem, 1.257rem + 1.31vw, 2.619rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.627rem, 1.576rem + 2.35vw, 4.237rem);
  --step-display: clamp(3.390rem, 2.034rem + 5.12vw, 7.425rem);
}
```
This direction lives or dies on the face and its settings. Display at tight tracking and leading; text sizes few and far apart (for example 15, 22, 34 and the display size). Use real typographic detail: proper quotes, balanced line breaks, hung punctuation where it helps.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-condensed-caps-display-neo-text`, Condensed caps as seasoning over neo text): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** A condensed grotesque at 500 in capitals, used sparingly (about 4% of the characters), over a neo-grotesque for everything else. The condensed display is 5.3vw (76px at 1440), leading 1.0, tracking 0, two lines at most, only for the first-screen headline and the section headings. The neo-grotesque's display cut at 500 sets the 20-24px sub-heads, and its text at 400-500 runs at 16px, leading 1.5. Ratio about 4.8x. The condensed face is a seasoning, never the body. The headline does not fill the screen alone, so the dealt hero's photograph carries it. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card fill. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; link hover tints ink. House rules for it: Capitals only in the condensed display; all text in sentence case; accent off cards; entrance as dealt. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

## 4. Colour — Deep, ink only
```css
:root {
  --bg: #132031;
  --surface: #273546;
  --ink: #f2f7ff;
  --muted: #abb4c0;
  --accent: #f2f7ff;
  --accent-ink: #132031;
  --field-1: #f2f7ff;  --field-1-ink: #132031;
}
```
Deep ground (#132031), light ink, and no accent hue: the action is set in the ink, solid. Colour comes only from the pictures. Two grounds and one band: the page ground and --surface, a clear step from it, and the ink as ground for one section at most. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product, never from added tints, badges or chips.
Either one saturated ground for the whole page with a single ink, or paper and ink with one section inverted and one in the accent as a full field. Colour does the job images would have done.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 6. Hero — Void, then statement on axis
A deliberate void: the upper 60% of the viewport holds nothing but the top row. That row, 44px from the top on 48px margins, has the wordmark at the left margin, two text links starting exactly on a vertical axis at 42% of the width, and the single action at the right margin as a text link. Everything else hangs from the same axis in the bottom third. The statement starts on the 42% line: two lines at clamp(56px, 6.6vw, 104px), regular or medium weight, tracking -0.04em, leading 0.95, running towards the right margin, with its last baseline 72px above the bottom edge. Left of the axis, in columns 1-4, one supporting sentence at 18px in the soft ink, max 32ch, is bottom-aligned to the statement's last baseline. Nothing sits in the left zone above it. No image and no shape; the ground is one flat tone and the ink one colour. The emptiness is composed: three elements share the axis, so the blank area reads as intended and not as a page that failed to load.

On a phone: The axis collapses to the 20px left margin. The top 48% of the screen stays empty under the wordmark row. The statement sets at 12.5vw on two lines, flush left, followed by the sentence at 17px and the action as a text link, the whole block ending 28px above the bottom edge. The two links fold behind a menu word.
Headline: A 3-6 word statement, at most 30 characters over two lines, about 15 per line at 6.6vw from the axis.
**Must show:** The upper 60% empty but for the top row, and the statement hanging from a vertical axis at 42% of the width in the bottom third.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. The scene in section 9 sets the first screen's picture and its motion; the words keep their places here. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (700), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

Adjusted for this page:
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.
- Its vw sizes assume a face of normal width: with this condensed display face, size the words to fill the measure the blueprint gives them instead of copying the vw value.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro statement, features split, showcase object. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

## 8. Motion — Rolling figures
For a page whose case is a handful of numbers (a report, a fund, a practice with a founding year): three or four figures at display size roll each digit up to its value once as they come into view, about 1.1 seconds on a strong ease-out, the columns 70ms apart. Tabular figures, the real number kept in the text for screen readers and search, never in running text and never for a value that changes after load. Set at its value under reduced motion. Recipe: 'Rolling figures' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Focus pull (`focus`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** The first screen comes into focus like a lens finding it. The picture starts soft and a touch large and sharpens over a second; as it does, the headline comes into focus from half strength. Nothing travels, and the sentence and the action are sharp from the first frame. Only the headline comes into focus, from half strength; the sentence and the action are there and sharp. Settled by 1.0 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="focus">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"` and `data-e="action"` (no picture on this first screen). A photo treatment's filter (black-and-white, a tint) goes on the frame, never on the img or video: the focus animates the picture's own filter.
- **A scene carries the first screen:** mark its headline, sentence and action only; its picture moves with the scene.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `focus` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=focus`.
- **Below the fold: Headings sharpen.** Chapter headings (data-reveal="heading", at most one per section) clear from 4 px of blur at 45% strength in 700 ms, once, with no travel. Body copy, pictures, cards and lists never move. No other block gets `data-reveal`: no fade-up on every block.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Scroll ink fill** (`mo-text-scrub-ink-fill`): A statement of 18-24 words at the sub step or smaller (36px at most, leading 1.2, three lines in a column of about 880px, left aligned on the page margin) rests in ink at 45% opacity, already readable (3:1 or better). As it crosses the viewport (block top at 80% to block bottom at 40%) each word goes to 100% ink in reading order, smoothed 0.15. It is not pinned: it scrolls with the page, so the fill finishes as the last line reaches about 45% of the screen height. The section holds the dealt gap above and below and nothing else. Colour roles: flat ground, ink, dim ink = ink at 45%. All words use the same fill so none is singled out. House rules for it: No accent word: the whole statement fills in one ink and the dim state stays readable. Designed out: the 36-56px paragraph (a paragraph statement stays at 36px or less on three lines) and the pinned variant with a picture. On a phone: 24px on four lines, same word by word mechanism; with reduced motion show full ink at once. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes

**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 3.1 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Scale contrast and colour mass. A capability list set at 5vw is an image in its own right.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Without pictures, weak copy has nowhere to hide: write real sentences. Vary the set pieces; four sections of big-heading-plus-paragraph is a text document, not a design. Keep all headings within two lines.
