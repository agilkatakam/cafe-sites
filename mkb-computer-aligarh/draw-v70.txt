## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`menu-button`):** Minimal: the wordmark at the left margin, and at the right margin a Menu text button beside the one action, both 44-48px tall, at every width. Menu opens a full-screen panel on the ink: the section names as links at the h2 step stacked on the left, the action, the email and the address or hours on the right, Close where Menu was; it slides down in 320ms. The 72px bar's ground scrolls away with the hero while the wordmark and the two buttons never leave: they stay fixed in their corners, each on a solid tab of the ground. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as the links of the Menu panel, and the one action. **Must show:** At every width only the wordmark and a Menu button beside the action; Menu opens a full-screen panel on the ink with the section names at the h2 step.
- **Footer (`closing-columns`):** A closing line over columns: the closing line at the h2 step (the action as a statement, four to seven words, two lines at most) with the one button under it; 96px below, three or four short columns at body size: the sections, the contact (the email, a phone only if the brief gives one), the place or the social links, the legal links with Image credits; the © line at the foot. On the surface tone, as tall as its content, about 60svh at 1440, so the line, the button and the columns share one screen. On a phone the columns stack in pairs. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** A closing line at the h2 step with its one button, then three or four short columns 96px below, on the surface tone, about 60svh, all on one screen.
- **Cards (`sticky-stack`):** Cards that stack as you scroll: three to five cards across the content width (a corner of 16px or more), in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so the edge of the card sliding up reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone; each about 70svh tall with its heading at the h2 step, its picture or figure and two lines of text; each sticks 96px below the top as the next slides up over it, the one beneath scaling to 0.96. Native scroll (position: sticky), no pin; reduced motion and phones show them one after another. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Cards about 70svh tall, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each sticking 96px below the top as the next slides over it.
- **Buttons (`btn-tile-row-bar-chevron`, Stacked row bars with chevron: a pattern of the study):** A stack of three to five rows, each as wide as its column (up to 880px at 1440), 80px tall, in the dealt corner, filled with the surface tone (rows 8px apart, so the gap does the work a line would do), a 16px sentence-case label in the dealt button weight at the left (32px padding) and a 20px chevron at the right (32px in). Hover: the row's fill goes to the ink with ground-coloured letters over 300ms and the chevron moves 6px right. Used for a short list of destinations or ways in. Its everyday form, for the first screen, the navigation and the cards, is one such bar at button size: 52px tall, as wide as its label plus the chevron and 24px each side, filled with the ink (letters in the ground colour), the chevron moving 6px on hover; the stack of rows is used once, as the list of ways in. Phone: the same rows, 72px tall, full margin width. House rules for it: Rows 8px apart: the gap does the work a line would do, no rules. Separate filled rows are cards in a list, never a box in a box; use it for one list only. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A stack of three to five filled rows 80px tall, 8px apart, each with its label at the left and a chevron at the right that moves 6px on hover, so the destinations read as one set.
- **Corners:** Square corners on pictures, cards, panels and fields. (`--radius: 0`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Open: 30-36vh between sections (270-320px at 1440, 128-160px on a phone), page margins 40-64px at 1440 and 24px on a phone; one section to a screen at most. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(8rem, 14vh + 10vw, 20rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4vw, 4rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** One grade over every picture (slightly warm or slightly cool, a little lifted shadows), the same on all of them. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** One working tool the visitor would really use, built from the subject's content (a price or size calculator, a booking with real dates, a filter over the real list). Not a toy, not a demo of the page's own design. It works on a phone with a thumb and has a plain fallback.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

## 1. The idea
One dark field from top to bottom, and in it, lit things. Photographs and objects appear like exhibits in a dim gallery: each with space around it, each the brightest thing on its screen. Type is light, small and sparse. The darkness is not a theme switch; it is a room, and the page is a slow walk through it. It suits subjects sold on atmosphere and material: an evening restaurant, a metal card, a record, a house at dusk.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A single near-black or deep-toned ground runs the full page with no section bands. First screen: either the name at full width in a light weight whole on the bottom margin with one dim photograph above it, or one object or photograph centred at 40–50% of the viewport with a line of text beneath at 16–18px. Each later screen holds one or two lit items at a generous size (the main one 40–60% of the screen width) with its text set beside it at full reading size; the darkness frames the items, it does not replace content. Images vary in size but all share the same darkness at their edges so they sit in the ground without frames. Practical details come last in a simple two-column text block. The only solid light element on the page is the single primary action.

## 3. Type — Red Hat Display, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.333 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 7.6vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Red Hat Display does every job, told apart by size and weight (600 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Red+Hat+Display:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Red Hat Display** — weight 600, sentence case, tracking -0.037em, leading 1.03
- Text: **Red Hat Display** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.420rem, 0.852rem + 0.89vw, 1.775rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.775rem, 1.065rem + 1.32vw, 2.369rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.872rem, 1.723rem + 4.71vw, 6.838rem);
}
```
Light or regular weights; they glow on dark. Avoid heavy bold, which looks cheap on black. Headings 4–7vw at most; text 16–18px with leading 1.6 and a slightly lifted ink (not pure white).
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-large-body-single-neo`, Large-body single neo-grotesk): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** One neo-grotesque at 400 and everything big: display 97px at 1440 (6.8vw), h3 52px, h4 37px and a large body of 30px, mixed case, tracking -0.02em, headings at lh 1.1 and the body at lh 1.2. The only small tier is 16px sentence case 500 (lh 1.3) for captions; buttons are the dealt button. Ratio about 3.3x, so contrast comes from ink and space, not size: heads in the ink, body in the dealt muted tone. A 30px body means at most 44 characters a line and at most three lines a paragraph, so the page carries few words. Colour: the dealt ground and ink; the accent only on the action fill. Motion: arrives with the dealt entrance; hovering a link row lifts its ink from the muted tone to full ink (160ms). House rules for it: Captions are 16px sentence case, never small capitals. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

A studied palette with its other fields left out: black ground, white ink, one red accent for the primary action and links. Two grounds and one band: the page ground and --surface, and the ink as ground, one section at most. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product.
A dark ground with a tint (blue-black, green-black, brown-black), a soft light ink, a dimmer ink for secondary text, and one warm or bright accent used once per screen at most.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

**What the pictures show for this brief:** the subject's world: its place, its materials and the details that belong to no one. **Never:** another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

Fewer photographs, chosen better: no more of the subject and its world than the photograph total below allows (the rooms, dishes or products a page pictures are their own set, one photograph each beside its facts), each one modern, well lit and professional-looking (the quality of a good Unsplash or Pexels photograph), or none at all, with the product, the type, the colour and the interface carrying the page. **Never documentary or archive photographs:** no old Commons or museum pictures, no history photographs, postcards, old maps or engravings, no news-like or snapshot-looking shots; they made recent pages look serious, old and cheap, and the owner named them first. Without the client's photographs, its place, its people and its things are never another's: the pictures show the world around it, captioned with what and where they are, and a slot only the client's own picture can fill goes to type.

## 6. Hero — Lit strip, four corners
One near-black field with a single lit band. Four small pieces of text at 14px, medium weight, sit in the corners on 72px side margins and 44px top and bottom margins: the name top-left, one link top-right, one sentence saying what this is bottom-left (max 36ch), the single action as a text link bottom-right. Across the vertical middle runs one horizontal strip of photographs, 34vh tall (about 300px), each at its natural width with 10px gaps and square corners: eleven or more frames in a flex row wider than the viewport, so the first and last visible frames are cropped by the left and right edges. The frame nearest the centre is at full brightness; the others sit at filter: brightness(0.55) and brighten on hover or as they reach the centre. Under the centred frame, aligned to its left edge, its title is set in the same 14px text. The top quarter and the bottom quarter of the screen stay empty and dark. The strip scrolls sideways with overflow-x: auto and scroll-snap; never hijack vertical scroll.

On a phone: The strip stays a single sideways-scrolling row but grows to 46vh tall, so one frame and the cropped edges of its neighbours fill the width. The name and the link sit at the top on 20px margins; the sentence and the action stack bottom-left under the strip at 14px. Dark space remains above and below the strip, about 12% of the height each.
Headline: None at display size: the name at 14px in a corner and one sentence of up to 60 characters.
**Must show:** A dark field crossed by one strip of photographs wider than the screen, the frame nearest the centre the brightest, and only small text in the four corners.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (600), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets running, list or table text at 14px: use your body step (16px or more, 15px at the least on a phone). The inspector fails it under 14px and warns under 16px (`small-body`).
- It asks for 11 photographs: on this page it takes 1, the best (section 5 counts the page's photographs), and a plain line of names for the rest where it lists more.
- It names colours (black): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).
- Its action is a text link: the primary action on the first screen is a real button (filled or solid-bordered, 44px or taller), placed where the blueprint puts the link. A second action may stay a link.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro the scene, features table, showcase the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — the scene `dot-becomes-the-full-stop` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the intro) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 2. features — Comparison set as text (table)
A comparison table with no lines and no tick marks. A CSS grid: capability names in columns 1–5 of 12 at 22–24px medium, then three answer columns of equal width (plans, methods, or this against the usual way). Every cell answers in words at 17px: 'Yes, unlimited', 'Up to five', 'Not yet', never a symbol. Seven to ten rows, each 64–72px tall, with every other row on a ground one tone darker so the eye tracks across; rows touch. The column that matters is one filled block in the accent or ink colour running the full height of the table behind its cells and rising 24px above the first row, with its text reversed. Column names sit in the top row at 17px bold. The heading, two to four words at 40–48px, hangs top-left above column 1 with 48px beneath.
On a phone: The table turns on its side: one block per capability. The name at 20px bold, then three lines beneath, each giving a column name and its answer at 16px; the featured column's line is set on the filled colour at full width. Blocks alternate the two ground tones.

### 3. showcase — the scene `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the showcase) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 4. cta — carried by the footer's closing line (section 0)
The footer dealt in section 0 opens on the page's closing line and its button: that is this page's call to action. Build no section here; the section before it runs straight into the footer after the section gap.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
A closing line over columns: the closing line at the h2 step (the action as a statement, four to seven words, two lines at most) with the one button under it; 96px below, three or four short columns at body size: the sections, the contact (the email, a phone only if the brief gives one), the place or the social links, the legal links with Image credits; the © line at the foot. On the surface tone, as tall as its content, about 60svh at 1440, so the line, the button and the columns share one screen. On a phone the columns stack in pairs. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Edge-to-edge marquee
One line of very large type travels sideways across the full width at a slow constant speed, as a band between two sections or as the closing statement. Linear timing, duplicated content for a seamless loop, paused on hover and under reduced motion. One marquee per page, carrying real words, never a list of buzzwords. Its words are new information (the services, the formats, the clients the page can name), never the hero's line again.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Exposure (`exposure`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** The type is in place at once. The photograph starts pale, as if over-exposed (dim on a dark page), and its real light and colour come up in about a second. A photograph or a clip. base.css animates the img's or video's own filter, so a caption in the frame stays at full ink. Settled by 1.22 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="exposure">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Use it on a mid-tone or dark photograph: on a pale one the change barely shows and reads as a glitch. A photo treatment's filter goes on the frame, never on the img or video. This page is dark, so the photograph comes up from dim: put `--exposure-from: brightness(0.35) saturate(0.5)` on the body.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `exposure` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=exposure`.
- **Below the fold: Pictures take their colour.** Large pictures (data-reveal="picture", four at most) go from 20% saturation to full colour once as they enter, over 900 ms with ease. No travel, no scale, and text never moves. No other block gets `data-reveal`: no fade-up on every block.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Slow layer under sheet** (`mo-parallax-under-sheet-slow`): The outgoing section's background (a full-bleed photograph or fixed layers) travels at 0.3-0.5 of scroll speed while the next section, a solid flat-colour sheet (the dealt corner on its top corners, or square), slides up over it at normal speed. The sheet is as tall as its content (at least 60vh) with the dealt gap above its first line. The underlay keeps full brightness, with no dimming. Its content (a headline at the display step, two lines at most) stays readable until covered. Colour roles: underlay brings its own colour, sheet = a flat ground with ink, the accent on the sheet's dealt button. Variant: the underlay holds perfectly still (sticky, factor 0) for the height of the next section, a plain ground of one flat tone (the ink field, or the page ground on a dark page) or one word at the display step, so the contrast of the held layer and the sheet's other tone carries it; when the covering sheet is the footer it is a designed section at content height (at least 40% of a screen) with its own actions and links. House rules for it: No shadow where the sheet overlaps and no gradient; the underlay text sits directly on the picture's calm part with no box; the sheet is content height, never a 100vh floor holding one line. On a phone: Factor 0.6 with the same cover; with reduced motion the sheet simply scrolls. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Colour wave through the headline** (`mo-text-char-colour-flash`): A headline of two lines at most at the display step, ink on the ground. A wave of the accent colour runs through every character from left to right as the section crosses the screen: scrubbed by scroll from 80% to 40% of the viewport height, with a window of about eight characters in the accent at any time (each character turns accent as the wave reaches it and eases back to ink after it passes). The wave covers the whole headline equally, so no word is singled out, and at rest the headline is one plain ink colour. Phone: the head step. Colour roles: ground as dealt, ink one value, accent the page's single accent (used only in the wave). The headline is set in one weight and size so the wave is the only event. House rules for it: Flashing only the emphasised words would make a styled accent word, so the wave passes through every character equally and ends in plain ink, leaving nothing emphasised at rest; it follows scroll, never a timer. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, tested and ready to fill. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 2.6 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### The dot becomes the full stop (`dot-becomes-the-full-stop`) — carries the intro section
On the page's own ground one large dot in the ink, the display face's own full stop set very large, drops in from the top edge as the stage arrives and lands in the middle, and the first word of a claim comes out of it before the stage holds; the rest of the claim, four or five words at true display size across nearly the whole width, grows out of it to the left one whole word at a time with a short rise and no blur, the dot stepping right and a step smaller with every word while the phrase stays centred, until it is exactly the size and place of the sentence's own full stop as the last word lands. An optional second beat cuts the ground to ink in one jump, turns the sentence the paper colour and sets one plain supporting line under it, whole. A graphic object promoted into the typography: a middle for pages short of pictures, services above all. Hold: 0.6 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle of the page, after the hero: the intro, the about or the proof, the one claim the page stands on told as one sentence; never the call to action, which needs its button. With the second beat on, the section after starts on the ink ground and carries it on; with it off, on the page ground. Once per page, with a quiet section before and after it.
Content: No pictures. One claim of 4-5 words, 16-30 characters, ending on its full stop (the scene's subject), every word under about 11 characters, in the page's display face; a face whose full stop is a true round dot reads best, since the dot is that glyph. One optional supporting sentence of 8-16 words for the last beat. Nothing else on the stage: no labels, buttons, counters or drawn shapes. The page's ink and ground, 7:1 or better both ways; no accent on the line or the dot. Without a claim that ends on a full stop, use a plain heading.
Options (data-options): hold 0.6 (0.5-1.5); phoneHold 0.7 (0.5-1.5); beat true (the hard cut to ink, the sentence in the paper colour and the supporting line whole) | false (add class dfs--plain to the section; the ground stays, and a supporting line, if any, still appears whole once the full stop lands); dot 0.2 (0.1-0.28) the dropped dot's diameter as a share of the stage height, never more than a third of its width; fill 0.95 (0.5-0.97) share of the content width the finished sentence spans; max 0.17 (0.1-0.24) share of the stage height the type never passes; min 0.07 (0.04-0.1) share of the stage height under which one row takes two; rise 0.24 (0.1-0.4) em each word rises into place; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-ink', the class it gets from the cut on; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --dfs-paper, --dfs-ink, --dfs-size, --dfs-lh, --dfs-gap, --dfs-pad, --dfs-support-size.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/dot-becomes-the-full-stop.js` and `dot-becomes-the-full-stop.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/dot-becomes-the-full-stop.html`. On a phone: The same scene with phone numbers: the claim fitted to the full width on two rows broken where they are most even (one when it fits large, never three); the dot rides the first row, takes a short carriage return down to the last row under the row above, rides it a step smaller with each word and lands on the full stop measured on the last row; hold 0.7. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

### Thumbnail becomes the stage (`thumbnail-becomes-the-stage`) — carries the showcase section
A statement of one or two lines at display size sits over a row of large thumbnails on the layout's columns, whose middle one is a keyhole onto a full-screen chapter; from the first step of the scroll the keyhole grows over its neighbours as they slide in under it, rises and opens to full bleed, and the statement passes through its edge, ink outside and paper inside, sliding from centred to flush left to become the chapter's heading word for word. It opens a product, a place or a film chapter from one strong muted loop or one strong picture. Hold: 2 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle, as the door from the intro into the showcase or the work; the opened picture carries the chapter's feature notes on a solid band at its foot, and below 600px they follow it in the page's flow.
Content: One chapter picture or muted loop of 8-15 s (16:9, 1920x1080 or more, 8MB at most, with a poster; for software, a screen recording of the product), its subject in the centre: the keyhole opens on about the central half of its width and three quarters of its height on desktop (that crop is the thumbnail); its upper left calm and dark for the white heading; a 4:5 cut for phones. One or two satellite stills a side of neighbouring chapters in the same grade, 960px wide or more with the subject in the centre (the row crops them close to square on desktop, 3:4 on tablets, 3:5 on phones). No text burned into the pictures. A statement of up to two lines of 20 characters or fewer that names the chapter and becomes its heading word for word; an optional note under the heading; three or four feature items of a 2-3 word label and 16 words or fewer.
Options (data-options): hold 2 (1.4-2.6); phoneHold 1.6 (1.4-2.6); phone 'same' | 'rest'; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis (0-0.8); thumb, the layout's columns by default (0.12-0.5; a share under the columns is lifted to them, a larger one runs the neighbours off the edges); radius, the page's --radius or 0 (0-16 px), always 0 at full bleed; shade 0.3 (0-0.6) flat scrim over the picture under the heading; band 1 (0-1) the band under the notes, solid by default; heading 0.15 (0.1-0.25) where the heading settles, the statement starting at the same height; nav '', the selector of a fixed nav that gets is-on-dark over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.js` and `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.html`. On a phone: The same opening with the keyhole starting at 64% of the width, 3:5, its neighbours running off the edges; hold 1.6. From 600px all four notes show two by two on the band; below 600px wide or under 540px tall they leave the stage and follow the picture in the page's flow, every one shown.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Light against dark, and the slow pacing.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Grey text on black fails contrast quickly: check it. No glow effects, no neon accent, no coloured light blobs. A dark page with a bright busy photograph is not this direction; choose low-key images. Dark does not mean empty: every screen still carries its content at a readable size, and running text is a light ink at 7:1 or more, not a dim grey.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
