## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`bottom-dock`):** A dock at the bottom: at the top of the first screen only the wordmark, at the left margin, which scrolls away with the hero. The links and the action live in a capsule fixed 20px above the bottom edge and centred, there from the first screen on, 56px tall, as wide as its content, filled with the ink and lettered in the ground colour (on a dark page the surface tone with ink letters), opaque, no border, no shadow: three or four section names at 15-16px, the current one at weight 600, and the action as its one filled item in the accent. On a phone the dock spans the width less the margins, 60px plus the safe area, each target 44px or taller, and the page keeps 96px free under its last line. It slides down out of view while a pinned scene or the replay holds the screen, and comes back after. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The links and the action live in a capsule in the ink fixed 20px above the bottom edge; at the top only the wordmark.
- **Footer (`two-halves`):** Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** Two halves meeting on the centre line: the closing line and button on the ink at the left, the links and contact on the surface tone at the right.
- **Cards (`rows`):** Rows with a surface: each item a full-width row on the surface tone, 80-104px tall, 8px apart, the dealt corner: the name at the h4 step on the left, one fact in the middle columns, the price or the link at the right. No line between them, no outline; hover steps the fill. For a menu, the plans, the rooms in short, a schedule. On a phone each row sets its parts on two lines. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Full-width filled rows 80-104px tall, 8px apart: the name at the left, one fact in the middle, the price or link at the right.
- **Buttons (`btn-tile-display-type-bar`, Display-type bar: a pattern of the study):** One block, about 690px wide and 150px tall at 1440 (half the content width), filled with the ink (letters in the ground colour), in the dealt corner, holding a single label of one or two short words at the display step (display face, one line) with 48px side padding. The whole block is the link. Hover: the accent floods up from the bottom in 400ms and the label swaps ink to match, a short label nudging 8px right. It closes a section, so it needs 160px of space above and below, and at most one short sentence near it, at the lead step or smaller. Its everyday form, for the first screen, the navigation and the cards, is the same block at button size: 52px tall, as wide as its label plus 28px each side, the dealt corner, the ink fill, a 16px label in the dealt button weight, the same flood on hover; the full block is used once, to close a section. Phone: the block runs the margin width, 120px tall, the label drops to the head step and may wrap to two lines. House rules for it: One label in the display face at the display step: no tracked capitals, no outline or rule. A box on a flat ground, never on a photograph. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** One solid ink block half the content width (about 690 by 150 at 1440) whose whole face is a one-line label at display size, the accent flooding up on hover; everywhere else the same block runs at 52px.
- **Corners:** Square corners on pictures, cards, panels and fields. (`--radius: 0`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Airy: 22-28vh between sections (200-250px at 1440, 112-140px on a phone), page margins 48-72px at 1440 and 24px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(7rem, 12vh + 7vw, 15.75rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4.5vw, 4.5rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts
with a stated brand constraint or needs an asset that does not exist, and say why in your report.

## 1. The idea
Something stays put while everything else changes. A title holds its place across the viewport and the images beneath it swap; the name is split to two opposite corners and the page passes between the halves; a single frame stays centred while its contents change. Every scroll step is a new cover of the same magazine. The idea suits subjects that are a series (a book of portraits, a line-up, a collection, a set of projects): the fixed element says what the series is, the changing element shows its members.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A sticky layer holds the fixed element for the height of two to three viewports: usually the title at 10–14vw across the middle or top of the screen, or two words pinned to the top-left and bottom-right corners. Under it, a stack of full-height panels scrolls by, each a single large photograph (portrait 4:5, centred, 40–50% of the viewport wide) with one line of caption at the same small size everywhere. The title sits above the images in the stacking order and uses `mix-blend-mode: difference` or a solid colour that reads on every panel. When the pinned run ends, the page releases into ordinary flowing sections with a narrow measure. Build with `position: sticky`; never hijack the scroll.

## 3. Type — Pathway Gothic One over Instrument Sans
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.414 scale from a 17px body; the hero's display step is about 7.3vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Pathway Gothic One only for the display and head steps; Instrument Sans for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Pathway+Gothic+One:wght@400&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Instrument+Sans:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Pathway Gothic One** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking 0.002em, leading 0.88
- Text: **Instrument Sans** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0625rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.277rem, 0.766rem + 0.63vw, 1.419rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.700rem, 1.020rem + 1.06vw, 2.125rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.125rem, 1.275rem + 1.67vw, 3.006rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.740rem, 1.644rem + 4.50vw, 6.525rem);
}
```
The pinned title is the only large type on the page. Captions and body share one small size (15–17px). Tight tracking and solid weight so the title reads over photographs.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

## 4. Colour — Bottle green, gold
```css
:root {
  --bg: #133621;
  --surface: #234d31;
  --ink: #f4d96c;
  --muted: #c0b26a;
  --accent: #f4d96c;
  --accent-ink: #133621;

```
Bottle green covers the page and warm gold sets all the type, with panels one step lighter than the ground. The button is solid gold with green text, and photographs are kept dark and warm so the pair is not disturbed.
One strong title colour against a pale or dark ground. Every panel stands on that ground or the surface tone; the photographs carry the other colours.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names). No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 5. Imagery
**There are no photographs of these products: each is shown by what is true of it, its own smaller product statement of the hard rule under the photograph total,** where the page lists them, in the gallery section (section 7). Each holds the slot for the client's product shot (`data-slot="product-photo"`), named in the report. **With an image tool in your kit,** the slot holds a render of this product as the brief describes it (its form, its colours, its parts; never another maker's product, and no prompt that names one), beside the statement's name and figures, which stay; the Image credits mark it as a render made for this page, not a photograph, and the report says so. Its photographs (the shopping in a crate, a child's helmet on a hook, the school street in the rain) show at least once who it is for (people from behind or far off, without the product). The page is never without them (a cargo-bike page with none read as undesigned): find them in the open collections (`node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "<its use, its load or its place>"`), sharp and of one light, each shown once and credited in the Image credits dialog. **The brief decides what the product is, never the photographs found:** its words and specifications stay as the brief gives them. A picture of what the name means (the bird the brand is named after) never stands in for the product. In your report, list the statement and every photograph, and tell the client that a product shoot is the first thing to add: its photographs go in `data-slot="product-photo"`, beside the statement's name and figures.

**What the pictures show for this brief:** the desk the writing is done at: a pencil on an open notebook, hands writing, paper and bindings close. **Never:** a typewriter or old letters; another product's screen; a page whose words can be read; another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

**Each product is shown, not boxed: as many pictures as products listed.** Every product the page sells has its own picture: the client's own where they exist; otherwise its product statement (`data-slot="product-photo"` on its name, price, two figures and colourways, as the hard rule below says), never another maker's product, all in one light, and the report lists each as a stand-in for the client's own. A section that lists six products shows six pictures, one beside each, never one picture for the lot; with fewer good pictures, show the products you can picture and name the others in one plain line, rather than a product without its picture. Each product stands in two columns, its picture at 55% of the width or more beside one grouped block of its name and price, on the page's ground; never a small picture centred in wide margins with the name at one edge and the price at the other (a guesthouse's four identical bands read as timid). A stand-in whose title or credit names another business (a real hotel's room, another maker's product) is never shown as this one, marked as a placeholder or not: take one that names none.

Fewer photographs, chosen better: no more of the subject and its world than the photograph total below allows (the rooms, dishes or products a page pictures are their own set, one photograph each beside its facts), each one modern, well lit and professional-looking (the quality of a good Unsplash or Pexels photograph), never none: a product page shows its use, its load and its place (below). **Never documentary or archive photographs:** no old Commons or museum pictures, no history photographs, postcards, old maps or engravings, no news-like or snapshot-looking shots; they made recent pages look serious, old and cheap, and the owner named them first. Order of sources: (1) the project's own pictures; (2) pictures you generate, if you have an image tool (the best way to show this exact subject); (3) the dealt photographs below and the rest of the library (`node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --photos <words>`): sharp and modern, free of credits or, from Wikimedia Commons, credited by author and licence as their line says; (4) only for what the library lacks (a detail of a room, the product's use and load; never a dish or a drink a menu names, which is the client's own photograph or type), photographs from the open collections (`node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "janakpuri street"`, then queries from your inventory (its place, its materials, its use and its details; never the client's own thing: the hard rule below)), only sharp modern photographs at 1800px or more, never from the museum sources, each looked at full size first and cropped or dropped if it shows another maker's name or logo, a shop or company sign, a readable house number or licence plate, words that would embarrass the client, or a child who could be recognised (references/imagery.md §2), never retouched or blurred instead (no blurred or pixelated face: take shots with faces turned away, out of frame or naturally out of focus, or frame the load and the place), never one whose title or credit names another business (a real hotel's room) shown as the client's own, and every one credited in the Image credits dialog (`--credits` prints it), never in the footer. **When the search fails** (the script retries, and searches Wikimedia Commons when Openverse fails or finds nothing), take the dealt photographs and the library; where none fits, the slot goes to type, or the client's own thing to what the hard rule below puts in its slot, never to a museum or archive source, and the report names the searches that failed; (5) type and space: never a drawing in place of a picture (inline SVG only for the logo and small icons, and a chart only inside a product fragment, `data-ui`; a drawn diagram, chart or illustration on the page fails, labels or not, so say a process in words: `drawn-illustration`). Logos of known products the page names (Gmail, Figma, Slack, Chrome, ChatGPT, Claude) are the real marks, never drawn: `node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs --logo gmail,figma` saves them from Simple Icons at a pinned version, and a brand missing there comes from its own press kit, unchanged; never an initial in a circle in its place. A picture that carries its own lettering (a title card, a sign, a label, a poster) is cropped so the lettering stays whole or leaves the frame entirely, at every width: a portfolio cut its own work titles to "Pelik" and "ZUIDKAI".

A series of photographs with the same crop ratio and the same framing (all portraits, all objects, all rooms), so that swapping them reads as one image changing. Five to eight in the pinned run.

**Pictures: fewer, better, larger (the inspector checks them).** No more pictures of the subject and its world than the photograph total below (the products the page pictures are their own set, as above), scenes included, each requested at least 1.5 times the size it is shown (twice for the hero), all with the treatment dealt in section 0. Before placing one, look at it: if it is soft, random, or could belong to any site, leave the slot out instead. Not the category's stock shot (smiling models in a studio, the product floating in mid-air on a bright colour) unless it is art-directed for this page; the client's own pictures come first, and without them its use, its load, its place and the details that belong to no one, never its own thing by another's (references/imagery.md §2). No thumbnails inside sentences, no pictures inside headlines, no small pictures filling a gap, no caption under a picture unless it says something the visitor needs. Each photograph once on the page, never again as a crop (a scene's stamp of the picture it has just shown excepted); a picture under or beside a line shows what the line names; a photograph of a place that is not the client's own is captioned with what and where it is, as its source names it, never with a place the source does not name. SVG drawings do not count as pictures. Every file the page asks for exists: a `src`, a `srcset` crop or a CSS background that is not there fails (`missing-image`). Words on a photograph sit on its calm part, of one tone, at 1440, 768 and 390 alike, since the crop moves as the screen narrows (`text-on-photo`), with no gradient, fade or milky fog laid over it. Video only as section 0 deals it.

**Search first: the library holds no photograph of the writing's use and load.** Search the open collections with `node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "janakpuri street"` (Openverse, then Wikimedia Commons when Openverse fails or finds nothing) and use the sharp, modern finds.

**The photograph total: at most 5 on the whole page, at least 3** (this direction takes 6, held to the 5 a product page with no photographs of its own can honestly show): the hero 1, the gallery the products' statements and 4 and the intro none. Each blueprint below takes its share, whatever number it names; a part left with none is built from its words, the colour and the dealt card style, never with a photograph shown again at another crop.

**Hard rule: the client's own thing is never shown by another's.** No other maker's product however cropped, no named hotel's or other house's room, no other artist's work, no other house captioned as the client's, whatever the credit says; and no picture shown as the client's carries a credit or file title that names another business (a hotel, a maker, a brand): a picture so credited shows only the world around, captioned with what and where it is, or is left out. Without the client's photographs, each product is shown by what is true of it, never by an empty frame: **its product statement**, in place of its picture wherever the page lists it: its name, its price and two of its true figures (what it holds, what it weighs, its size) as tabular figures, the price the largest, each with its unit and a label of what it counts; and its colourways as named swatches (a small filled square of each colour with its name, never a band or a card in the colour). The figures and the colourways are the brief's; where it gives none, the usual ones of a product of its kind, plausible and marked in the report as sample copy for the client to confirm. `data-slot="product-photo"` stays in the code on each statement's figures, where the client's own shot will stand, never visible as an empty or tinted box. No silhouette, no outline, no drawing. The photographs show only its use, its load and its place, with no product of its kind in them, however cropped, unbranded or out of focus.

## 6. Hero — Inset photo frame with text at the foot (`hero-inset-photo-frame-text-at-the-foot`, a pattern of the design-anatomy study)
Described as the study saw it on a great site: build this composition with this page's own words and pictures, in the dealt type, colour and corners, its buttons section 0's; a backdrop it names that this page cannot honestly have (a 3D scene, footage) is a photograph of the subject or the product's own interface at the same size and place, or the ground itself.
Ground as dealt. One photo frame fills the first screen inset by the page margin on all four sides (about 1344x756 at 1440x900), with the dealt corner, entirely inside the first screen, nothing of the next section showing. The lettering sits on the photo's calm lower part (failing that, a flat black scrim at 40-55% under the words only, no gradient). Inside the frame at its foot, on the photo's calm part, lettered in whichever of ground and ink is lighter: at the left the headline at the display step in two lines; at the right, on the same baseline, a one-sentence sub at the lead step over the dealt button. The upper part of the frame stays open photograph. The dealt navigation sits as dealt; the hero adds no rail, bar, chip or menu of its own. On scroll the photo drifts at 0.9 speed inside the frame (parallax inside the mask) while the frame stays still. It arrives with the dealt entrance. Colour roles: ground, ink and the photograph; the accent only on the dealt button. House rules for it: No rail, bar, chip or menu of its own; not a centred headline over a rounded card (the text sits at the foot, left and right); text on the photo's calm part, never a tint over the whole photo or a gradient (failing that, a flat black scrim at 40-55% under the words only); one accent, on the dealt button.

On a phone: The frame is inset by the 24px phone margin with the dealt corner and fills the screen; the headline at the display step on two lines at its foot, the sub and the dealt button full width beneath it.
Headline: The page's own idea, in this client's words, on two lines at most (12 words or fewer).
**Must show:** One photo frame inset by the page margin on all four sides with the headline at its bottom left and the sub and action at its bottom right, the upper two thirds open photograph: not a centred headline over a rounded card.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its picture shows what the headline names.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** gallery gallery, features grid, intro split. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. gallery — Staggered pairs in empty ground (gallery)
No heading. Photographs are placed one or two per screen so that more than half of every screen stays empty ground. Screen one: a 3:4 portrait in columns 1–4 against the left margin, and a near-square 5:4 image in columns 7–12 against the right margin whose top edge starts 30vh lower than the portrait's. Screen two: a 3:2 landscape in columns 6–11 and, 24vh lower, a small 4:5 image in columns 2–4. Repeat the two arrangements, mirrored on the second pass, for six to ten photographs. Under each image's left corner, a one-line caption at 13px in the soft ink, 10px below the image. Vertical gaps between pairs are 22–28vh. Every edge sits on a column line and offsets come from only two values. Square corners. If motion is used, images enter with a short `clip-path` wipe from the bottom edge.
On a phone: A single column in which widths and sides alternate: 70% against the left margin, 84% against the right, 56% left, then 100% full-bleed, with 64px between images. Captions sit under each image's left corner at 13px. Portrait crops are preferred so each image fills most of the screen height.

Adjusted for this page:
- It asks for 10 photographs: on this page it takes 4, the best (section 5 counts the page's photographs), and a plain line of names for the rest where it lists more.
- It lists the products, each shown by its own smaller product statement (section 5's hard rule) in place of its picture: its name, its price and two of its true figures as tabular figures with their units and labels, and its colourways as named swatches, with `data-slot="product-photo"` on each; any object or product photograph it names is left out, never another make.

### 2. features — One-size name columns (grid)
Pure text at one size. Three columns start on columns 1, 4 and 7 of the 12-column grid, leaving columns 10–12 empty on purpose. Each column opens with a group name in full ink and continues with a tight stack of six to ten capability names in the soft ink, all at 16–17px, regular weight, line-height 1.25, no bullets and no symbols; one blank line separates group name from stack. Hovering a name brings it to full ink and shows a one-line description in a fixed spot: the top of the empty columns 10–12, at the same size. The heading is no larger than 28px; two or three words, alone on column 1, 96px above the columns. Give the section 20vh of empty ground above and below, so the smallness reads as decided. Works on light or deep grounds.
On a phone: Columns stack with 40px between groups; text rises to 18px with line-height 1.4 for touch. Group names stay in full ink. Hover descriptions are dropped; each group ends instead with one sentence in the soft ink that sums it up. The heading is 24px, 48px above.

### 3. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
The hand-over scene `lib-0312` (section 9) opens this section; its direction says what of the section it carries, and the rest follows in this shape.
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

Adjusted for this page:
- It asks for a photograph, and the page's photograph total leaves none here (section 5): build it from its words, the colour and the dealt card style, with no picture.
- Its vw sizes assume a face of normal width: with this condensed display face, size the words to fill the measure the blueprint gives them instead of copying the vw value.

### 4. cta — carried by the footer's closing line (section 0)
The footer dealt in section 0 opens on the page's closing line and its button: that is this page's call to action. Build no section here; the section before it runs straight into the footer after the section gap.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
Two halves: the left half on the ink (or the accent field the palette names) holds the closing line at the h2 step and the button in the ground colour; the right half on the surface tone holds the links in two short columns, the contact and the legal line. Full width, 50-60svh, the halves meeting on the centre line; on a phone they stack. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Pinned story
One element holds its place with `position: sticky` (a title, a frame, a product fragment) while the items that belong to it scroll past or swap inside it. The page's ordinary scrolling does the work; nothing is hijacked. Hold it for about one screen per step and three screens at most, two held stages per page at most, with a line of text on every held screen. Recipe: 'Pinned story' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.
It holds the stage about 2 screens: one of this page's held stretches, counted with the scenes in section 9, and no section beside it pins.

### Entrance — Aperture (`aperture`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** The picture opens from a small window in its middle out past every edge of its frame, pulling back from a detail to the whole view. The type is in place from the first frame and never moves. As described; centre the window on the subject with four insets. Settled by 1.2 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="aperture">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Centre the window on the subject with `--window: <top> <right> <bottom> <left>` on the frame (default `34% 36%`); on an interface fragment also `--from-scale: 1.1`.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `aperture` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=aperture`.
- **Below the fold: Pictures open from a window.** Up to three large pictures (data-reveal="picture") open once from a centred window (inset 12% 14%) while the photograph eases back from 1.08, over 1,000 ms. Text never moves. No other block gets `data-reveal`: no fade-up on every block.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Scroll ink fill** (`mo-text-scrub-ink-fill`): A statement of 18-24 words at the sub step or smaller (36px at most, leading 1.2, three lines in a column of about 880px, left aligned on the page margin) rests in ink at 45% opacity, already readable (3:1 or better). As it crosses the viewport (block top at 80% to block bottom at 40%) each word goes to 100% ink in reading order, smoothed 0.15. It is not pinned: it scrolls with the page, so the fill finishes as the last line reaches about 45% of the screen height. The section holds the dealt gap above and below and nothing else. Colour roles: flat ground, ink, dim ink = ink at 45%. All words use the same fill so none is singled out. House rules for it: No accent word: the whole statement fills in one ink and the dim state stays readable. Designed out: the 36-56px paragraph (a paragraph statement stays at 36px or less on three lines) and the pinned variant with a picture. On a phone: 24px on four lines, same word by word mechanism; with reduced motion show full ink at once. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Late word slides in** (`mo-parallax-word-slides-into-line`): A hero headline of three to five words at the display step over two lines. The last word starts about 96px below its line, fully visible from the first frame, so the sentence already reads whole; across 60svh of scroll, in a hero held that long, it slides up (scrubbed, eased) into its line and closes the gap. Every word shares one face, colour and weight. The hero still fills the first screen alone. On a phone the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. House rules for it: The sliding word has exactly the same face, colour, weight and style as the rest (no accent word); it is a real word of the sentence; the hero still fills the first screen alone and reads whole in the first frame. On a phone: Display step on the phone; the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On the hero's headline it runs on scroll only, once the entrance has set the headline in place. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Rounded tile goes edge** (`mo-scale-rounded-tile-widen`): A picture tile (16:9 or 21:9, 1370px wide at 1440) sits inset by the page margin on each side with the dealt corner as the section arrives. Over the next 650-720px of scroll (while it is sticky, or as its top travels from 90 to 30 percent of the viewport) it widens to the full 1440px: clip-path inset(0 var(--margin) round var(--radius)) to inset(0 0 round 0), the height holding or growing from 80svh to 100svh. The corner eases to 0 so it ends edge to edge. Text for the section sits below the tile and is simply there (the dealt entrance decides whether it arrives); no caption sits on the picture. Phone: inset by the page margin with the dealt corner, widening to the full 390 over 60 percent of the scroll distance. House rules for it: A picture tile in the dealt corner, no outline, no caption box on the photograph; the pin, if any, is under two screens. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, a researched technique to build fresh. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 1 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall); the signature move (Pinned story) holds about 2 more. A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The example is not the content.** The direction a technique gives is written for another client: its business, its objects and its words are examples. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the example's content is unfinished.
**This page is short of pictures of its own subject, so its scene needs no photograph or one.** Fill it from the words and the product's own facts; a scene's one picture is one the page shows nowhere else (a cargo-bike page showed its one family photograph three times, the scene's among them). If even that cannot be filled, `--redraw scenes` deals another of the same kind before any that needs more.

### A full-screen veil hides the swap of the whole palette (`lib-0312`) — a technique to build fresh, the hand-over into the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. A flat full-screen sheet comes up to fully opaque at the exact moment a chapter ends, the page's ground, ink and chrome swap roles underneath it while nothing can be seen, and the sheet then thins away to leave the visitor standing in the new palette, so a change of world is hidden behind one beat instead of blended or wiped, and the colour never passes through an in-between.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** A bright full-screen flash from near-black is hard on the eyes and on photosensitive visitors, so it needs a reduced-motion path and must never loop; an accent sheet and a content-break placement soften it. It depends on two grounds that contrast. Anyone who jumps by keyboard or an anchor link lands past the veil, so the ground must be tied to scroll position, not to time. The line is only seen by people who stop, so it must be dispensable. Sections with their own opaque backgrounds do not follow the swap. Fails if the brief wants a gradual colour ramp: the hand-off is a cut, so slow scrolling never produces an in-between colour. With fewer than four chapters the swap does not read as a pattern. Built in WebGL it is heavy and wants a laptop; the custom-property version is light and works on phones.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** Make the veil the page's single accent colour, not white: a flat sheet that rises from the bottom edge like a tide line (clip-path inset from 100 percent at the top to 0, scrubbed over about 150 px of scroll), covering the fixed header as well. While it is fully opaque, swap ground and ink instead of adding a second ground: an ink-on-paper page for the first half becomes paper-on-ink after it, and the header flips with it. Pick the swap point at a content break (between the studio's case studies and its contact section) rather than after a hero. The sheet then clears over about 400 px, fastest in the middle, and if the visitor stops on it one short plain line about the next section appears in the middle of the sheet. Reverse on scroll up. The chrome re-skins on the same beat (the header ink, the button fill), and the swap is used once or twice per page at the edges that mean something, never on every section. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 1 screen of scroll: `hold: 1` in `Stage.scene`, its section 2 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** the same move at phone size (`phone: 'same'`). Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 1, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals another scene or technique that needs no photograph or one.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Repetition with change. The fixed title makes each new photograph feel like an event, so the photographs must be good and consistent.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
If the title is not legible over every panel, the idea fails: test each one. Keep the pinned run under six viewports or it feels like a trap. On a phone the title shrinks to 16–18vw and stays pinned at the top; panels become full-width. A pin longer than three screens, or one where the screens under it are nearly empty, read in blind tests as a stuck page. Every screen under the pin needs its own content, and nothing may pass over the pinned words.
