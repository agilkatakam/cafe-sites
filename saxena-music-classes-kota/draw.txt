## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`chapter-band`):** A band that names where you are: fixed at the top, 56px tall, a solid band of the page ground on every screen. The wordmark at the left margin at the h4 step; at the right margin a Menu text button beside the one action (40px tall); dead centre the name of the section in view at 16px in the ink: the section's one- or two-word name (its link in Menu), never a sentence or a number. When the next section takes the screen the old name slides up out of a 24px mask and the new one slides up in (280ms, ease-out); over the hero the centre is empty. Menu opens the section names full-screen on the ink, with the action and the email. On a phone: a 56px band with the wordmark, Menu and the action; the live name is dropped. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as the links of the Menu panel, and the one action. **Must show:** The centre of the fixed 56px band shows the name of the section in view and swaps it by sliding the old name up out of a mask.
- **Footer (`picture-and-facts`):** A split footer: a photograph of the subject on the left six columns, from the left margin, 60svh tall; on the right six columns, on the surface tone, the closing line at the h3 step, the button, the contact, the links and the legal line, 32px between the groups. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** A 60svh photograph on the left six columns beside the closing line, the button, the contact and the links on the surface tone.

Nothing framed inside it. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** One large filled panel 8-10 columns wide holding one whole group, beside or under its heading.

## 1. The idea
People come to a service page with questions: what is it, is it for me, what does it cost, how do I start. Answer them in that order, plainly, and the page feels trustworthy. The idea is the one thing this service does that others do not, stated in the first line and proven in the rest.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The first screen states what the service is and for whom, with the action (book, apply, call) and one picture of the real place or people at work. Then: who it is for, as a short list; how it works, as three or four plain steps; the prices or terms, as a table; answers to real questions, as an accordion that opens one at a time; how to reach them, with the address, hours and a map or directions. The action stays reachable on every screen.

## 3. Type — Prata over Chillax
A serif display over a sans. A 1.333 scale from a 17px body; the hero's display step is about 5.8vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Prata only for the display and head steps; Chillax for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Prata:wght@400&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=chillax@400,600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Prata** — weight 400, sentence case, tracking -0.028em, leading 0.99
- Text: **Chillax** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0625rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.277rem, 0.766rem + 0.63vw, 1.419rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.510rem, 0.906rem + 0.94vw, 1.887rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.887rem, 1.132rem + 1.40vw, 2.519rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.192rem, 1.315rem + 3.60vw, 5.219rem);
}
```
A friendly, readable sans or a warm serif for headings; text at a comfortable size.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

## 6. Hero — The answer first
The headline says what the service is and for whom; one sentence; the action. Beside or under it one real picture of the place or the people at work. Beside it, the headline is 5vw or more (72px at 1440); a headline that cannot be that large goes above the picture, or over it full-bleed. Under both, three or four facts a visitor would otherwise search for (price from, where, when, how long a stay or a session lasts, never a travel time the brief does not give), each a short line at the lead step that says the whole fact ("From 39 € a night, breakfast included"), never a band of big figures over one-word labels. The facts are true and complete.

On a phone: Headline, sentence, action, picture, then the facts as a short list.
Headline: What it is and for whom, three to eight words; at most about 28 characters when it stands beside the picture.
**Must show:** A headline that says what the service is and for whom, one real picture beside or under it, and three or four complete facts as short lines under both.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**A split hero takes a large headline:** beside a picture that holds half the screen, the `h1` is 72px (5vw) or more at 1440, on two lines at most, so about 26-30 characters. If the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead; never a small headline in a 50/50 split, which reads as a shop template.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (400), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro the scene, features grid, showcase the scene. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — the scene `lib-0105` (section 9)

### 2. features — One-size name columns (grid)
Pure text at one size. Three columns start on columns 1, 4 and 7 of the 12-column grid, leaving columns 10–12 empty on purpose. Each column opens with a group name in full ink and continues with a tight stack of six to ten capability names in the soft ink, all at 16–17px, regular weight, line-height 1.25, no bullets and no symbols; one blank line separates group name from stack. Hovering a name brings it to full ink and shows a one-line description in a fixed spot: the top of the empty columns 10–12, at the same size. The heading is no larger than 28px; two or three words, alone on column 1, 96px above the columns. Give the section 20vh of empty ground above and below, so the smallness reads as decided. Works on light or deep grounds.
On a phone: Columns stack with 40px between groups; text rises to 18px with line-height 1.4 for touch. Group names stay in full ink. Hover descriptions are dropped; each group ends instead with one sentence in the soft ink that sums it up. The heading is 24px, 48px above.

## 8. Motion — Pushed line
One line of very large type, the name or the claim, is pushed sideways by the scroll rather than by a clock: it travels from 12% right to 12% left of its place while it crosses the screen, linear and locked to the scrollbar, and reads whole at the middle of its run. One per page, never across running text, and the words must never be cropped at the moment the line sits in the middle of the screen. Build with animation-timeline: view() on the line and a translateX keyframe; static where scroll timelines are unsupported and under reduced motion.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Set type (`set-type`), dealt
This is the masked line rise with the clip-path shutter: once every page's default, now one entrance of seven and rarely dealt. Build it as base.css has it.
- **What the visitor sees:** The headline's lines rise out of their own baselines one after another while the picture opens downward like a blind; the sentence and the action rise 12 px after them. Settled by 1.2 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="set-type">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `set-type` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=set-type`.
- **Below the fold: Headings by line.** Chapter headings (data-lines, five at most) rise line by line from their masks once, using the "Lines that rise on scroll" script in motion.md. Nothing else enters, and a stray data-reveal does nothing.

### Small moves — two, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Alternate side slide** (`mo-scrub-alternate-side-slide`): Three or four rows, each full width inside the page margins (about 1296px at 1440): one big photograph tile (560x560, the dealt corner) and a caption block (a title at the head step and one paragraph of about 20 words at 20px, 44 characters wide) filling the other half, so no row is empty at the left. Odd rows put the tile on the left, even rows on the right. As a row crosses from 90% to 50% of the viewport height the tile slides 180px into its place from its outer side and the caption 180px into its place from the opposite side, scrubbed, both fully visible throughout; at rest both are in place. Every row sits on the same ground, with no second fill. The dealt section space between rows. Phone: tile above caption, each moves 40px from alternating sides. House rules for it: The tile is a photograph, never a digit or a headline in a card (no 01/02/03 numbering); no outlined tiles; no row left empty on one side; scroll-linked, nothing starts hidden. On native scroll in the section it fits, without a pin; still for reduced motion.
- **Mask roll swap** (`mo-text-mask-roll-swap`): When a state changes (a slide, a tab, a disc, a list item) the text that describes it, a title, a price, a date or a one-to-three-word value at the lead, sub or head step, rolls out and the new text rolls in. The old text moves up 100 percent out of a mask that is exactly one line tall plus 0.1em at the bottom so descenders survive, in 300-420ms on cubic-bezier 0.77, 0, 0.18, 1; the new text enters from below 60ms later, words staggered 30ms. Labels beside it slide 12-23px. If the new title wraps to two lines the container animates its height in the same 300ms. The roll follows the direction of navigation (next goes up, previous goes down). It plays only after the visitor's own action, never on load or on scroll. Phone: the same, 260ms. House rules for it: The mask only clips the text itself, no outlined box; whole words roll, never single letters; it runs only after the visitor's own action, so it is not an entrance. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, one tested and ready to fill, and one researched technique to build fresh. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 3 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Type sets like wet ink (`lib-0105`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. Display type exists first as swollen, merged ink blobs and sharpens into clean letters line by line as it reaches its place on screen, because a blur is pushed through a hard alpha threshold that relaxes as the blur clears, so words seem to set like wet ink rather than fade in.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Thin, light or serif faces lose their hairlines in the threshold and settle looking bolder than set. Over photographs or busy grounds the blobs read as dirt. One SVG filter per line is costly: more than four or five moving at once stutters on phones and in some browsers, so attach the filter only while a line moves and remove it at rest. Fonts must load before the lines are split. Reduced motion needs a plain cut to crisp type. Used on every heading of a page it becomes a tic.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a mineral-water brand's manifesto, set four short lines of a heavy round grotesk in one ink on a flat chalk ground, one line per screen of native scroll. Each line condenses out of chunky blobs as it rises from the lower third to the centre, and stays crisp once set, with the blur at 8px at most, so the reader sets the pace line by line; the last line stays crisp with the one button under it. Nothing else on the page melts: every other heading cuts or slides plainly, no accent colour in the lines, no paragraph or drawings around them. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 1 screen of scroll (the research ran it without a pin; on the stage it holds one screen): `hold: 1` in `Stage.scene`, its section 2 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** the same move at phone size (`phone: 'same'`). Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 1, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

### Thumbnail becomes the stage (`thumbnail-becomes-the-stage`) — carries the showcase section
A statement of one or two lines at display size sits over a row of large thumbnails on the layout's columns, whose middle one is a keyhole onto a full-screen chapter; from the first step of the scroll the keyhole grows over its neighbours as they slide in under it, rises and opens to full bleed, and the statement passes through its edge, ink outside and paper inside, sliding from centred to flush left to become the chapter's heading word for word. It opens a product, a place or a film chapter from one strong muted loop or one strong picture. Hold: 2 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle, as the door from the intro into the showcase or the work; the opened picture carries the chapter's feature notes on a solid band at its foot, and below 600px they follow it in the page's flow.
Content: One chapter picture or muted loop of 8-15 s (16:9, 1920x1080 or more, 8MB at most, with a poster; for software, a screen recording of the product), its subject in the centre: the keyhole opens on about the central half of its width and three quarters of its height on desktop (that crop is the thumbnail); its upper left calm and dark for the white heading; a 4:5 cut for phones. One or two satellite stills a side of neighbouring chapters in the same grade, 960px wide or more with the subject in the centre (the row crops them close to square on desktop, 3:4 on tablets, 3:5 on phones). No text burned into the pictures. A statement of up to two lines of 20 characters or fewer that names the chapter and becomes its heading word for word; an optional note under the heading; three or four feature items of a 2-3 word label and 16 words or fewer.
Options (data-options): hold 2 (1.4-2.6); phoneHold 1.6 (1.4-2.6); phone 'same' | 'rest'; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis (0-0.8); thumb, the layout's columns by default (0.12-0.5; a share under the columns is lifted to them, a larger one runs the neighbours off the edges); radius, the page's --radius or 0 (0-16 px), always 0 at full bleed; shade 0.3 (0-0.6) flat scrim over the picture under the heading; band 1 (0-1) the band under the notes, solid by default; heading 0.15 (0.1-0.25) where the heading settles, the statement starting at the same height; nav '', the selector of a fixed nav that gets is-on-dark over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.js` and `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.html`. On a phone: The same opening with the keyhole starting at 64% of the width, 3:5, its neighbours running off the edges; hold 1.6. From 600px all four notes show two by two on the band; below 600px wide or under 540px tall they leave the stage and follow the picture in the page's flow, every one shown.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
