## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`bottom-dock`):** A dock at the bottom: at the top of the first screen only the wordmark, at the left margin, which scrolls away with the hero. The links and the action live in a capsule fixed 20px above the bottom edge and centred, there from the first screen on, 56px tall, as wide as its content, filled with the ink and lettered in the ground colour (on a dark page the surface tone with ink letters), opaque, no border, no shadow: three or four section names at 15-16px, the current one at weight 600, and the action as its one filled item in the accent. On a phone the dock spans the width less the margins, 60px plus the safe area, each target 44px or taller, and the page keeps 96px free under its last line. It slides down out of view while a pinned scene or the replay holds the screen, and comes back after. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The links and the action live in a capsule in the ink fixed 20px above the bottom edge; at the top only the wordmark.
- **Footer (`three-cards`):** Three filled cards in a row on the ground, on the surface tone with the dealt corner, whatever the page's card style: the studio's or company's city and what it takes on, the contact (the email with Copy, a phone only if given), and the news or the social links; each as tall as what it holds, its heading at the h3 step over three or four lines, never stretched hollow to a set height; the © line, the legal links and Image credits under them. It counts as a row of cards: one section above it in cards at most, never two (`same-structure` counts the footer). The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** Three filled cards in a row on the surface tone, each as tall as its heading at the h3 step and the three or four lines under it.
- **Cards (`card-thin-mat-portrait-pair`, Thin-mat portrait pair: a pattern of the study):** Two cards side by side, each 360x540 at 1440 with a 32px gap (a 752px pair), in the surface tone with the dealt corner as a thin mat: 10px on the top and sides round a portrait picture at 0.83 (340x410), then a 16px padded block holding a 16px medium title (two lines) and a 14px meta line in muted ink. The pair sits left of a text column (the section heading and a short paragraph) so the far side is full. Scroll: the cards start tilted -3 and +3 degrees and settle to straight across the section's crossing (a scrubbed tilt: they are fully visible throughout, straight at rest and with reduced motion). Hover: the card lifts 8px. Phone: the pair stays two across at 46% width each, title 16px, meta dropped, with the text above. House rules for it: Text 14px or more; modern lit portraits, never documentary; the text column fills the far side; the tilt settles straight at rest and with reduced motion. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Two thin-matted 360x540 portrait cards tilted -3 and +3 degrees that straighten as the section crosses the screen, set left of the text column.
- **Buttons (`circle-arrow`):** A label with a round arrow: the label at 18-20px in the ink, weight 500, then 12px on a 52-56px circle filled with the accent holding an arrow in the accent's ink; label and circle are one link, 56px tall; hover turns the arrow 45° and slides the label 4px. The second action is the label alone, without the circle. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A label followed by a 52-56px circle in the accent holding an arrow that turns 45 degrees on hover; label and circle are one link.
- **Corners:** A 10px radius on cards, panels and pictures alike. (`--radius: 10px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Open: 30-36vh between sections (270-320px at 1440, 128-160px on a phone), page margins 40-64px at 1440 and 24px on a phone; one section to a screen at most. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(8rem, 14vh + 10vw, 20rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4vw, 4rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** One photograph that moves slowly as the page scrolls (a scale or drift of a few percent), once.

This is the direction for this build. Adopt it. Do not blend it with the layout you would have made anyway,
and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts

On the first screen one more order holds: **the hero's geometry and its display size > the type steps** (the `h1` at `--step-hero` where section 3 prints it; every other size there keeps its step). ## 1. The idea
Take one shape from the subject and let it run the whole system. A drilling tool's octagonal cross-section becomes the buttons, the image masks and the loader. A two-account money app becomes two circles that recur as icon, inline dot and section colour. A café's striped awning becomes every band on the page. Look at the product, the logo, the object or the word in the headline and find the simplest geometry in it (a circle, an arc, a chamfer, a stripe, a notch, a ring). Then use only that shape, at three or four very different scales, everywhere a designer would normally reach for a generic rounded rectangle.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: the motif appears once at enormous scale as a flat filled shape, cropped by the viewport (a circle 120vh across entering from the right, a chamfered block filling the lower half), with a short headline at 6–8vw and one supporting sentence set on the remaining ground, left-aligned on a shared margin. Through the page the motif returns as: the mask for every photograph (via `clip-path` or `border-radius` made from the same geometry), the shape of the primary button, the bullet beside list items at text size, and one full-width band that separates the two halves of the page. Sections use simple two-column or single-column layouts so the shape is the only thing with character.

## 3. Type — Bitter over Alegreya Sans
A slab. A 1.25 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5.8vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Bitter only for the display and head steps; Alegreya Sans for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Bitter:wght@500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Alegreya+Sans:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Bitter** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.011em, leading 0.98
- Text: **Alegreya Sans** — weight 400 for running text, 700 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.125rem, 0.675rem + 0.56vw, 1.250rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.340rem, 0.804rem + 0.84vw, 1.675rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.675rem, 1.005rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.192rem, 1.315rem + 3.60vw, 5.219rem);
  --step-hero: clamp(2.192rem, min(6.39vw, 34svh), 5.750rem);
}
```
`--step-hero` is the hero headline's own size (section 6): its blueprint sets it at about 92px at 1440, where `--step-display` stops at 73px. The first screen's `h1` takes it and nothing else does: in the dealt display face and weight, two lines at most, every word whole between the margins (where the longest line would not fit, the size comes down until it does).
A clean geometric or grotesk face that echoes the shape (round bowls for circles, squared terminals for chamfers). Medium weight; headings 6–8vw in the hero and 3–4vw after.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-hero`, the one size beside them.
**Type note (`type-transitional-tight-ladder-over-neo`, Tight transitional serif ladder): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** A transitional serif at 400 on a close heading ladder over a neo-grotesque at 500 for text. Top head 84px (5.8vw at 1440, lh 0.9, -0.02em, at most two lines), section heads 63px and 58px, card heads 42px and small heads 21px (lh 1.0-1.2). Text 16px (lh 1.45, max 52 characters) and, where the page states one, a stat figure at 47px in the sans, set in the line that says what it counts. Ratio about 5x from top head to text. Because the steps sit close, the hierarchy is carried by position and by filled cards in the dealt card style that group the content. Colour: the dealt ground and ink; the accent only on the action fill. Motion: arrives with the dealt entrance; card hover as the dealt cards have it. House rules for it: The figure is a real stat set in its own line, never 01/02/03 numbering or a stats band. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

## 4. Colour — White, rich green
```css
:root {
  --bg: #ffffff;
  --surface: #e9e9e9;
  --ink: #1a1817;
  --muted: #5d5c5a;
  --accent: #2a8741;
  --accent-ink: #ffffff;
}
```
White ground, dark ink, one rich green accent. The accent is for the primary action, links and at most one other thing on a screen; it is never a ground for text blocks, never a second headline colour and never spread across cards. Two grounds, no field: the page ground and --surface, a clear step from it; sections are told apart by space, size and pictures. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product, never from added tints, badges or chips.
The motif carries the accent colour at large scale, flat. Everything else is ground and ink. A second motif colour is allowed only if the subject has a real pair (two accounts, two modes).
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names). No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 6. Hero — Giant cropped shape
A plain ground with one shape at enormous scale: a photograph of the subject (its material, its object, its place) cut with `clip-path` to the simplest geometry found in the subject (a circle, a ring segment, a chamfered block, an arch), never a drawn or filled shape. It is about 110vh tall on columns 7–12, its right side on the right page margin and its centre at 55% of the height, so the hero's top and bottom crop it. The headline sits on the bare ground on columns 1–6, vertically centred, on the left margin: two lines at clamp(64px, 6.4vw, 100px), heavy weight, tracking -0.03em. Beneath it comes one supporting sentence at 19px, then the single action as the dealt button. The dealt navigation runs across the top.

On a phone: The shape moves to the bottom: the full content width between the page margins, its top at 56svh, cut only by the hero's bottom edge. The headline sits above it at 11.5vw on two lines on the page margin, starting 15% down, with the sentence at 16px and the button beneath. The navigation folds behind a Menu word.
Headline: Two lines of two or three words each, up to about 12 characters per line at 6.4vw.
**Must show:** A photograph of the subject cut to one simple shape about 110vh tall on columns 7-12, cropped by the hero's top and bottom, beside the headline on the bare ground.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-hero` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (500), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro split, features grid, showcase the scene, cta split. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — Title, note, facts row (split)
The hand-over scene `letter-bar-floods-the-ground` (section 9) opens this section; its "Where" line says what of the section it carries, and the rest follows in this shape.
First row: a heading of two to four words in a light weight at clamp(44px, 5vw, 76px) sits on the left margin in columns 1–6; a three-line paragraph at 16px, 260px wide, sits in columns 10–12 with its last line on the heading's baseline. Columns 7–9 stay empty. 120px lower, a second row sets three or four facts (a date, a time, a place, a price) as large light text at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px), spread across the 12 columns with `justify-content: space-between`. Each fact has a one-line explanation at 14px directly under it in the soft ink, left-aligned to the fact. Facts are separated only by the space between them, so the row reads like the line of times on a printed programme. A photograph may start under the right half of the facts row, 5 columns wide at 4:3. Padding 140px top, 120px bottom.
On a phone: Heading at 11vw on the left margin; the note follows directly under it at 16px, full width. The facts become a two-column grid with a 32px row gap, each fact at 26px with its explanation beneath. Any photograph goes last, full width.

### 2. features — Specimens on one ground (grid)
Each capability is represented by one object photographed alone and shown on a tile of the page's surface. Columns 1–3 hold the heading (two to four words at 40–52px), top-aligned, with a 30ch sentence beneath and nothing else down the rest of that column. Columns 4–12 hold a grid of five or six tiles in two rows with 10px gaps: tiles are 4:5 except one, which is double width at 8:5 and placed second in the first row. Every tile takes that one fill, the surface one step off the ground; the objects carry the colour, never the tiles. Each photograph is centred at about 70% of its tile's height, with mix-blend-mode: multiply so a white studio background disappears. Under each tile, outside it and aligned to its left edge: the capability name at 20px medium and one fact at 16px in the soft ink. No text on the tiles and no hover lift.
On a phone: The heading and its sentence sit on top at full width. Tiles become a two-column grid with 8px gaps; the double tile spans both columns at 8:5 and comes first. Names shrink to 17px and facts to 15px, still under each tile.

### 3. showcase — the scene `lib-0109` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the showcase) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 4. cta — The object returns (split)
The thing shown in the hero comes back once, from another angle, to close the page. On one flat field 90svh tall, the object's photograph occupies columns 1–8, scaled to about 130% of that area and anchored so the section's bottom edge crops it while its left side keeps the page margin (`overflow: hidden` on the section; on light fields `mix-blend-mode: multiply` removes a white studio background). The text sits small in columns 10–12, vertically centred: a closing line of three to five words at 32–40px, one sentence at 17px, the price or key fact on its own line at 22px, and one rectangular button. Nothing overlaps the object's focal area. Where the page has no photograph of an object, the closing line takes the field alone at the display step. The closing line is the only heading.
On a phone: The text block comes first: closing line at 28px, sentence, fact, full-width button. The object follows at the full content width, centred, cut only by the section's bottom edge, filling about 55svh on the same flat field.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
Three filled cards in a row on the ground, on the surface tone with the dealt corner, whatever the page's card style: the studio's or company's city and what it takes on, the contact (the email with Copy, a phone only if given), and the news or the social links; each as tall as what it holds, its heading at the h3 step over three or four lines, never stretched hollow to a set height; the © line, the legal links and Image credits under them. It counts as a row of cards: one section above it in cards at most, never two (`same-structure` counts the footer). Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Already there (`already-there`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Nothing arrives. The first frame is the finished first screen. The only motion is inside the media: a clip that plays, a product that responds to the pointer, a picture that fades in only if it arrived late. The photograph is there. If none of it had painted by the first frame, it waits on --picture-ground and fades in over 320 ms. A dealt clip plays from frame one, with that frame as its poster. Settled by the first frame.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="already-there">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `already-there` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=already-there`.
- **Below the fold: Nothing.** Nothing enters below the fold, and no element on the page gets data-reveal. Section content is simply there, and motion comes from the moves the sheet deals, any moving picture and feedback under the hand. Lazy pictures get the same arrival courtesy as the hero: they wait on their ground colour and fade in when they land. That is loading, not a reveal.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Scroll ink fill** (`mo-text-scrub-ink-fill`): A statement of 18-24 words at the sub step or smaller (36px at most, leading 1.2, three lines in a column of about 880px, left aligned on the page margin) rests in ink at 45% opacity, already readable (3:1 or better). As it crosses the viewport (block top at 80% to block bottom at 40%) each word goes to 100% ink in reading order, smoothed 0.15. It is not pinned: it scrolls with the page, so the fill finishes as the last line reaches about 45% of the screen height. The section holds the dealt gap above and below and nothing else. Colour roles: flat ground, ink, dim ink = ink at 45%. All words use the same fill so none is singled out. House rules for it: No accent word: the whole statement fills in one ink and the dim state stays readable. Designed out: the 36-56px paragraph (a paragraph statement stays at 36px or less on three lines) and the pinned variant with a picture. On a phone: 24px on four lines, same word by word mechanism; with reduced motion show full ink at once. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Late word slides in** (`mo-parallax-word-slides-into-line`): A hero headline of three to five words at the display step over two lines. The last word starts about 96px below its line, fully visible from the first frame, so the sentence already reads whole; across 60svh of scroll, in a hero held that long, it slides up (scrubbed, eased) into its line and closes the gap. Every word shares one face, colour and weight. The hero still fills the first screen alone. On a phone the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. House rules for it: The sliding word has exactly the same face, colour, weight and style as the rest (no accent word); it is a real word of the sentence; the hero still fills the first screen alone and reads whole in the first frame. On a phone: Display step on the phone; the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On the hero's headline it runs on scroll only, once the entrance has set the headline in place. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Rounded tile goes edge** (`mo-scale-rounded-tile-widen`): A picture tile (16:9 or 21:9, 1370px wide at 1440) sits inset by the page margin on each side with the dealt corner as the section arrives. Over the next 650-720px of scroll (while it is sticky, or as its top travels from 90 to 30 percent of the viewport) it widens to the full 1440px: clip-path inset(0 var(--margin) round var(--radius)) to inset(0 0 round 0), the height holding or growing from 80svh to 100svh. The corner eases to 0 so it ends edge to edge. Text for the section sits below the tile and is simply there (the dealt entrance decides whether it arrives); no caption sits on the picture. Phone: inset by the page margin with the dealt corner, widening to the full 390 over 60 percent of the scroll distance. House rules for it: A picture tile in the dealt corner, no outline, no caption box on the photograph; the pin, if any, is under two screens. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, one tested and ready to fill, and one researched technique to build fresh. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 3.1 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Letter bar floods the ground (`letter-bar-floods-the-ground`) — the hand-over into the intro section
One short statement holds on a single line in the page's display face with one straight letter (I, l, i, 1 or a T's stem) on the dead centre of the screen; from the first frame the camera zooms on that letter: the whole line scales up round its stem, the letter in its own proportions and the words growing more slowly as they leave past the screen's edges. Once the letter is wider than a third of the screen and taller than it, only its bar is left, a column of ink that keeps widening; the next section's opening statement arrives inside it at about half-way while the outgoing words' edges are still in frame, the column reaches full bleed by 80% and the statement grows to its resting size as the camera lands, so the next ground arrives through the inside of a letter with no seam and no hold of plain colour. A hand-over for pages with no pictures, in any sector. Hold: 1.1 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: Between two sections, as the hand-over from the page's ground to its ink (the intro to the offer, the about to the prices, the work to the contact). The section before ends on the page's ground; the section after continues on the ink straight under the statement the flood ends on, with little top padding. Once per page, with a quiet section before it.
Content: No pictures. A statement of 2-4 words, 12-22 characters, on one line, set at display size (96px or more on a desktop, nearly the full width on a phone), with a straight-stemmed letter near its middle: I, l, i, 1 or a T, never O, S, A or any letter with a bowl or a counter. Long words that push the pivot far from the middle shrink the line (its longer half round the stem sets the size). The next section's opening statement, 4-8 words in two lines at most at every width, and an optional sentence of 12-24 words under it, both in the ground's colour on the ink. One ground and one ink, 7:1 or better both ways; no accent, no second colour on any word.
Options (data-options): hold 1.1 (1-1.5); phoneHold 1 (0.8-1.2); pivot, the letter's index in the statement (spaces counted) or the letter itself (a span.lbf-pivot in the markup wins; else the straight letter nearest the middle); third 0.3 (0.2-0.4) share of the hold by which the stem is a third of the width; half 0.5 (0.4-0.6) share by which it is half the width (the next statement arrives about here); flood 0.8 (0.6-0.8) share by which the column fills the screen; grow 0.5 (0.3-0.8) the words' scale as a power of the letter's; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-flooded', which it gets while the ink covers it; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --lbf-size, --lbf-gutter, --lbf-next-size, --lbf-next-measure, --lbf-pad.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.js` and `letter-bar-floods-the-ground.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.html`. On a phone: The same zoom with phone numbers: the line spans nearly the whole width round its stem (a 12px gutter); the letter stays a letter, ends in view, until it is taller than the screen (about 0.55, near 30 times its size, repainted sharp rather than stretched from a small layer); the stem is a third of the width by 0.3 with the outgoing words' edges in frame; full bleed by 0.8; hold 1. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

### A hand dealt one card per beat (`lib-0109`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the showcase section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. Cards are dealt one at a time onto a pile, each arriving turned (tilted in the plane, or swung in from edge-on) and settling at its own small angle on top of the last, either fanned along a huge arc or levelled almost flat, while a title holds beside or beneath them, so a short set of items is laid down like a hand of cards being played.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** The fan needs about 940 px of width for four cards; phones need a vertical fan or a plain stack. More than five cards spread past a laptop screen. Stepping relative to the last step leaves cards missing after a reload mid-pin or a hard fling; set every card from the current step. Body text on covered cards is unreadable, so only the top card is really read. A colour per card breaks the house palette, overlapping cards alternate ground and ink rather than take an edge line or a shadow, and big 1-2-3-4 numerals on the cards are banned numbering.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a type foundry's four weights: tall paper strips hang from the top edge of the screen like pendants on an arc whose centre sits far above the viewport, and each beat swings a new strip in from the left in a hard 150 ms snap with no spring, the set re-centring as it lands. Strips alternate ground and ink, told apart by colour alone with no edge line or shadow, and each carries its weight's name set in that weight, large enough to read on the covered strips. Four strips at about half a screen each, and the section releases on the full set. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 2 screens of scroll: `hold: 2` in `Stage.scene`, its section 3 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** its own version, as the direction above says (`phone: { hold, build }` in `Stage.scene`), or `phone: 'rest'` with a finished composition that reads whole; never the desktop move squeezed. Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 2, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
One shape at many sizes. The giant cropped shape gives the hero mass and colour without a single image.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
One motif means one. A circle here, a hexagon there and a blob behind is the generated look this replaces. The motif must come from the subject and you must be able to say from where in one clause.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
