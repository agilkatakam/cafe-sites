## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`corners`):** No bar, four corners: the wordmark top-left and the action top-right, each on a small solid tab of the ground (10-14px padding, the dealt corner), fixed on every screen; on the first screen three or four links stand beside the wordmark and scroll away with the hero. On a phone the links go behind a Menu button beside the action. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** No bar: the wordmark top-left and the action top-right on small solid tabs, fixed on every screen.
- **Footer (`foot-centred-logo-and-link-row-over-columns`, Centred logo and link row over columns: a pattern of the study):** A filled panel on the surface tone, inset by the page margin, in the dealt corner, as tall as its content, about 700px at 1440, with 120px top padding. A centred logo lockup 56px high; 56px below it a centred row of up to seven primary links in the strong weight at the lead step on a 160px pitch (the site's other pages where it has them; on a one-page site its own sections and the contact; never a dead link), hover underlining in the ink. 96px under that, three left-aligned link columns on a 300px pitch starting at x 160 (titles at the lead step in the strong weight, body-size links), and at the far right a Back to top text link. The last row sits 80px below: the legal line at the left, the social text links and an Image credits link at the right, body size. Ink is the dealt ink on the surface tone. Phone: Lockup and primary links in a 2-up grid; columns stack. House rules for it: Designed out: the soft-tint variant with 50px top corners rising into view (a second radius and its own entrance), the 56px round back-to-top disc (a text link), the brand colour as the panel's ground (the surface tone). A rule and a row of membership marks would break the hairline and logo-wall bans; both are left out. The panel is one card, not boxes in boxes. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** A filled panel inset from the page margins in the dealt corner, with a centred logo and a centred row of bold links over three link columns, so the footer reads as one rounded panel and not a full-width bar.
- **Cards (`expanding`):** Cards that open, every one full at rest: a row of three or four filled cards on the surface tone, 520-560px tall, each holding its own picture at full colour from its top edge (never dimmed, washed or greyed) and its words on the fill under it. At rest the first stands open across about half the row: its picture over the top 60%, its name at the h3 step and one sentence under it; each of the others is a narrow card about a sixth of the row wide, its picture a tall strip over the top 60% and its name at the h4 step with its one fact under it. Hovering or focusing a narrow card opens it and narrows the open one (grid-template-columns, 400ms); no still ever shows an empty or ghosted card. On a phone they stack, each at its natural height with its picture and its words; everything a visitor needs reads in every card at rest and in the plain fallback. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** A row of filled cards, each with its own picture at full colour and its name at rest: the first open across about half the row with a sentence, the others narrow strips of picture over their name and one fact.
- **Buttons (`square-block`):** Square blocks: no radius, 56px tall, 28-32px padding, the text face at 17px weight 600, filled with the ink; hover swaps the fill to the accent in 160ms. The second action is the label alone in the ink, underlined on hover. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** Square 56px blocks in the ink with no radius that swap to the accent on hover.
- **Corners:** A large 24px radius on cards, panels and pictures, as consumer apps do. (`--radius: 24px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(6rem, 9vh + 5vw, 11.25rem); --margin: clamp(1.25rem, 3vw, 3rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** One working tool the visitor would really use, built from the subject's content (a price or size calculator, a booking with real dates, a filter over the real list). Not a toy, not a demo of the page's own design. It works on a phone with a thumb and has a plain fallback.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

## 1. The idea
Show the thing, bigger than seems reasonable. One photographed object fills the first screen, scaled until the viewport crops it, with the text small and tucked beside or behind it. The crop is the idea: a record whose edge leaves the top-right corner, a bottle whose shoulder runs off the bottom, a chair seen so close you read its grain. Being that close reads as confidence in the object. Everything else on the page serves it: details as tight close-ups, facts as short lines, one way to buy.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the object occupies 55–75% of the viewport and breaks at least one edge. It sits on a flat field (ground colour or one bold colour), never in a box. The headline is short and either very large and partly hidden behind the object (10–13vw, the object overlapping two or three letters) or small (28–36px) in the opposite corner with a one-line description, and small only when the object stands in the middle of a wide field with the words well clear of it: beside an object that holds one half of a split, the headline is 5vw or more at 1440 (72px). No navigation bar beyond a wordmark and one action. Following sections: a row of three or four close-up details at different crop ratios (not equal tiles), a single wide photograph of the object in use, a plain list of facts set large (materials, size, price) aligned to one edge, then the purchase action. The object returns once at the end from a different angle.

## 3. Type — Onest, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.25 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 7.6vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Onest does every job, told apart by size and weight (500 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Onest:wght@400;500;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Onest** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.041em, leading 0.93
- Text: **Onest** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.125rem, 0.675rem + 0.56vw, 1.250rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.340rem, 0.804rem + 0.84vw, 1.675rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.675rem, 1.005rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.872rem, 1.723rem + 4.71vw, 6.838rem);
}
```
Either very large and structural or very small and precise; pick one for the headline and hold it. Facts are set in the text face at 22–28px, not in a table.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-black-grotesk-heads-regular-body`, Black grotesque heads, regular body): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one grotesque with 400 and 900 cuts (the true black only; the heaviest loaded weight where the dealt face stops lower). Heads in black (900), mixed case: 100px for the hero and 72px (5vw) for section heads at 1440, leading 0.9-1.0, tracking -0.02em, two lines at most. Body 400 at 16-18px, leading 1.45; buttons keep the dealt button's label. Ratio about 6.3x. The contrast is between black and regular, with nothing in between: no 700 or 800 steps. Keep heads to three or four words. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card fill. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; links change colour on hover. House rules for it: Clean after the pass: no own button size or weight, entrance as dealt, accent off cards. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

```css
:root {
  --bg: #0f0f0f;
  --surface: #212121;
  --ink: #ffffff;
  --muted: #8f8f8f;
  --accent: #dafa9c;
  --accent-ink: #0f0f0f;

```
A studied palette with its other fields left out: black ground, white ink, one lime accent for the primary action and links. Two grounds and one band: the page ground and --surface, and the ink as ground, one section at most. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product.
A flat field chosen to make the object's own colour sing (its complement, or a tone darker than its shadow side). One field for the whole page, or a second for the facts section.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 6. Hero — View with its next steps in the corner

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro the scene, features grid, showcase object, cta statement. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Focus pull (`focus`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** The first screen comes into focus like a lens finding it. The picture starts soft and a touch large and sharpens over a second; as it does, the headline comes into focus from half strength. Nothing travels, and the sentence and the action are sharp from the first frame. On a phone the picture only settles in scale, with no blur; the headline still comes into focus. Settled by 1.0 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="focus">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). A photo treatment's filter (black-and-white, a tint) goes on the frame, never on the img or video: the focus animates the picture's own filter.
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `focus` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=focus`.
- **Below the fold: Headings sharpen.** Chapter headings (data-reveal="heading", at most one per section) clear from 4 px of blur at 45% strength in 700 ms, once, with no travel. Body copy, pictures, cards and lists never move. No other block gets `data-reveal`: no fade-up on every block.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, a researched technique to build fresh. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 2 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The example is not the content.** The direction a technique gives is written for another client: its business, its objects and its words are examples. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the example's content is unfinished.

### Statements pull into focus under the scroll (`lib-0236`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. On a held stage short statements arrive by focus rather than by movement: each word or line comes in oversized, soft and faint and sharpens into place under one scrubbed value a little behind the reader, then leaves the same way or is pushed out while the next statement, or a paragraph drifting up through it, takes over, so a run of statements plays as a relay of focus pulls.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Animated blur on dozens of split letters is costly on low-end phones, so split by word and drop the blur on touch. A fast scroll swaps statements before they are read unless each rests sharp for a stretch. Text over a busy picture or video loses contrast mid-pull, so keep the ground flat. A paragraph crossing the statement hurts legibility for a moment and needs short text. Screen readers get split letters unless the sentence sits whole in an aria-label. More than two screens of hold feel stuck if nothing else happens on the stage.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a research lab's three findings: three statements of five to eight words in a heavy grotesque, ink on a flat paper ground, left-aligned at one height on a stage held for two screens. Each word, not each letter, comes in from 125 percent scale and a 6 px blur to sharp over its own slice of the scroll, the blur gone in the first third of its travel; once a statement is sharp, its one-sentence explanation in the body face rises up through and past it at 1.4 times the scroll speed, and the statement then leaves by focus in reverse while the next arrives. No accent word in a statement, no picture behind, and on touch the blur is dropped for a plain opacity rise. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 2 screens of scroll: `hold: 2` in `Stage.scene`, its section 3 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** its own version, as the direction above says (`phone: { hold, build }` in `Stage.scene`), or `phone: 'rest'` with a finished composition that reads whole; never the desktop move squeezed. Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 2, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A small centred product shot with space all round is a catalogue thumbnail, not this direction. If the image has a busy background it cannot sit on a field; crop tighter or change image. Keep text off the object's focal area.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
