## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`bottom-dock`):** A dock at the bottom: at the top of the first screen only the wordmark, at the left margin, which scrolls away with the hero. The links and the action live in a capsule fixed 20px above the bottom edge and centred, there from the first screen on, 56px tall, as wide as its content, filled with the ink and lettered in the ground colour (on a dark page the surface tone with ink letters), opaque, no border, no shadow: three or four section names at 15-16px, the current one at weight 600, and the action as its one filled item in the accent. On a phone the dock spans the width less the margins, 60px plus the safe area, each target 44px or taller, and the page keeps 96px free under its last line. It slides down out of view while a pinned scene or the replay holds the screen, and comes back after. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The links and the action live in a capsule in the ink fixed 20px above the bottom edge; at the top only the wordmark.
- **Footer (`foot-centred-logo-and-link-row-over-columns`, Centred logo and link row over columns: a pattern of the study):** A filled panel on the surface tone, inset by the page margin, in the dealt corner, as tall as its content, about 700px at 1440, with 120px top padding. A centred logo lockup 56px high; 56px below it a centred row of up to seven primary links in the strong weight at the lead step on a 160px pitch (the site's other pages where it has them; on a one-page site its own sections and the contact; never a dead link), hover underlining in the ink. 96px under that, three left-aligned link columns on a 300px pitch starting at x 160 (titles at the lead step in the strong weight, body-size links), and at the far right a Back to top text link. The last row sits 80px below: the legal line at the left, the social text links and an Image credits link at the right, body size. Ink is the dealt ink on the surface tone. Phone: Lockup and primary links in a 2-up grid; columns stack. House rules for it: Designed out: the soft-tint variant with 50px top corners rising into view (a second radius and its own entrance), the 56px round back-to-top disc (a text link), the brand colour as the panel's ground (the surface tone). A rule and a row of membership marks would break the hairline and logo-wall bans; both are left out. The panel is one card, not boxes in boxes. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** A filled panel inset from the page margins in the dealt corner, with a centred logo and a centred row of bold links over three link columns, so the footer reads as one rounded panel and not a full-width bar.
- **Cards (`overlapping`):** Overlapping cards: a row of three or four filled cards with the dealt corner, in turn the surface tone and the ink (an ink card lettered in the ground colour), so every edge reads with no border, no shadow and no third tone. Each overlaps the one before by 24-32px and sits 24px lower, so the row steps down from left to right; its padding on the covered side is the overlap plus 24px or more, so no word sits under the card above. Hover or focus brings one to the front with a 6px rise. On a phone they stack with no overlap. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** A row of three or four cards, in turn the surface tone and the ink, each overlapping the one before by 24-32px and sitting 24px lower.
- **Buttons (`btn-rounded-chunky-display`, Chunky display block: a pattern of the study):** A heavy block: 10px radius, 64px tall at 1440, 24-28px side padding, the label in the dealt display face at the lead step in sentence case, one to three words, with an optional leading plus at the same size, filled with the accent with the accent's ink as letters. No border. Hover (200ms) brightens the fill 10% and turns a leading plus 90 degrees; press scales to 0.97. It is the loud action on a bold, game-like or food page. Colour roles: ground = page, accent = fill, accent's ink = letters. Phone: Block 60px tall with the label at the lead step, full column width; hover becomes press. House rules for it: Sentence case in the dealt display face at the lead step (the pattern's capitals are not kept). The pattern's same-colour 2px border is invisible and dropped; no outline variant, no shadow. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A heavy 64px block with 10px corners, the label in the display face at the lead step and a leading plus that turns 90 degrees on hover.
- **Corners:** A 16px radius on cards and panels; pictures at 16px or square inside a card. (`--radius: 16px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Open: 30-36vh between sections (270-320px at 1440, 128-160px on a phone), page margins 40-64px at 1440 and 24px on a phone; one section to a screen at most. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(8rem, 14vh + 10vw, 20rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4vw, 4rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** One moving picture of the subject: a muted loop with a poster frame and a pause control, used once, large. Only if an honest clip of this subject exists.

and do not redraw because it is unfamiliar: unfamiliar is the point. Redraw a slot only when it conflicts

## 1. The idea
Precision as the personality: the product or place described the way an engineer would, exactly. Dimensions, materials, tolerances, numbers and names, set beautifully. The page wins trust by being specific where everyone else is vague; find the numbers only this subject has.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
The first screen sets the object (or the place, or the product's output) large on a plain ground, with its name and a one-line description. Further down the page alternates between large views of the subject and well-set facts: a specification table with real rows, a numbered build or process only if order matters, a comparison of models as a clean table. A grid of details (close-up pictures with a short label naming what they show) can appear once.

## 3. Type — Pilcrow Rounded over Asap
A rounded sans. A 1.5 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5.8vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Pilcrow Rounded only for the display and head steps; Asap for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=pilcrow-rounded@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Asap:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Pilcrow Rounded** — weight 700, sentence case, tracking -0.011em, leading 1.01
- Text: **Asap** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.198rem, 0.719rem + 0.59vw, 1.331rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.800rem, 1.080rem + 1.13vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.250rem, 1.350rem + 1.88vw, 3.375rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.700rem, 1.620rem + 3.60vw, 5.219rem);
}
```
A precise sans; tabular figures for every number; units set properly (12 mm, 1.4 kg).
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

## 4. Colour — Newsprint and brass
```css
:root {
  --bg: #e8e8e3;
  --surface: #d3d3cd;
  --ink: #181715;
  --muted: #5b5a54;
  --accent: #bfb21a;
  --accent-ink: #181715;
  --field-1: #181715;  --field-1-ink: #e8e8e3;
}
```
Warm paper grey with near-black type holds the upper page, then whole sections flip to near-black with paper-grey type; no section is tinted. Brass yellow marks one small detail per screen, such as the button or a single word. The accent reads under 3:1 on this ground, so, whatever this note says before, it is a fill with its own text colour (the button, one block), never the colour of a word, a link or a thin mark: those take the ink.
Very restrained: white, grey or black ground, ink, and one accent used like a signal.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

**There are no photographs of these products: each is shown by what is true of it, its own smaller product statement of the hard rule under the photograph total,** where the page lists them, in the gallery section (section 7). **What the pictures show for this brief:** its use, its load and its place, with no product of its kind in them. **Never:** another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

**Each product is shown, not boxed: as many pictures as products listed.** Every product the page sells has its own picture: the client's own where they exist; otherwise its product statement (`data-slot="product-photo"` on its name, price, two figures and colourways, as the hard rule below says), never another maker's product, all in one light, and the report lists each as a stand-in for the client's own. **Hard rule: the client's own thing is never shown by another's.** No other maker's product however cropped, no named hotel's or other house's room, no other artist's work, no other house captioned as the client's, whatever the credit says; and no picture shown as the client's carries a credit or file title that names another business (a hotel, a maker, a brand): a picture so credited shows only the world around, captioned with what and where it is, or is left out. Without the client's photographs, each product is shown by what is true of it, never by an empty frame: **its product statement**, in place of its picture wherever the page lists it: its name, its price and two of its true figures (what it holds, what it weighs, its size) as tabular figures, the price the largest, each with its unit and a label of what it counts; and its colourways as named swatches (a small filled square of each colour with its name, never a band or a card in the colour). The figures and the colourways are the brief's; where it gives none, the usual ones of a product of its kind, plausible and marked in the report as sample copy for the client to confirm. `data-slot="product-photo"` stays in the code on each statement's figures, where the client's own shot will stand, never visible as an empty or tinted box. No silhouette, no outline, no drawing. The photographs show only its use, its load and its place, with no product of its kind in them, however cropped, unbranded or out of focus.

## 6. Hero — Words beside the product
The content width is split about 5 to 7. The headline, one sentence and the action sit in the narrower part, vertically centred or aligned to the top of the picture; the headline is large, 5vw or more (72px at 1440) on two lines at most, since a small headline beside a half-screen picture reads as a shop template. The wider part holds the product, the object or the real place, large, from the split to the right margin at the full height of the first screen. When the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead, with the words on its calm side. Nothing else on the first screen.

On a phone: Words first, then the picture at full width.
Headline: What it is, two to five words, at most about 26 characters.
**Must show:** The content width split about 5 to 7: a headline of 72px or more beside the product, the object or the place at the full height of the screen.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**A split hero takes a large headline:** beside a picture that holds half the screen, the `h1` is 72px (5vw) or more at 1440, on two lines at most, so about 26-30 characters. If the words cannot be that large in their part, the picture goes full-bleed behind them instead; never a small headline in a 50/50 split, which reads as a shop template.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (700), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** gallery gallery, features the scene, intro column, cta statement. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. gallery — Dense masonry sheet (gallery)
A three-line statement at 22px, 30ch wide, sits at the 66% column with 64px of space below it; it is the only large text. Then a dense masonry of about twenty tiles in six columns with 4px gaps and 12px outer margins, built with CSS `columns: 6; column-gap: 4px` and `break-inside: avoid` on each figure. Tile sizes range from 90px squares to one feature tile of 280 by 160px that sits above the first two columns, outside the column flow. Images keep their natural ratios, so column bottoms are ragged. Every tile has one caption line at 12px directly beneath, 6px below the image and 14px above the next tile, in the form name, comma, type. Mix photographs, posters, details and textures. No hover chrome; the sheet reads at a glance and fills the full width.
On a phone: Three columns with 4px gaps and 10px margins; the feature tile spans the full width at the top. Captions stay at 12px but shorten to the name only. The statement sits above at 19px, full width and left-aligned.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets text at 12px: use your small step (13px or more) for a caption or a label, and your body step for running, list or table text.
- It asks for 20 photographs: on this page it takes 2, the best (section 5 counts the page's photographs), and a plain line of names for the rest where it lists more.
- It lists the products, each shown by its own smaller product statement (section 5's hard rule) in place of its picture: its name, its price and two of its true figures as tabular figures with their units and labels, and its colourways as named swatches, with `data-slot="product-photo"` on each; any object or product photograph it names is left out, never another make.

### 2. features — the scene `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the features) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 3. intro — Narrow column, empty field (column)
A single text column 300–340px wide, its left edge at 15% of the viewport width, starting 64px below the section top. It holds four or five short paragraphs at 15–16px with leading 1.5 and one blank line between them; the first paragraph is in medium weight and full ink, the rest in regular weight and the soft ink. No heading, no image, no button; one underlined text link may end the last paragraph. The remaining three-quarters of the 1440 screen is untouched ground, and the section is only as tall as the text plus 160px of padding below. The column's left edge must match a line used elsewhere on the page (the wordmark's left edge or a later column). Where the page has a large object or a fixed photograph, this column is the small text beside it. Holds 90–150 words comfortably.
On a phone: The column becomes 78% of the width, left-aligned at the 20px margin, so a strip of empty ground remains on the right as a deliberate margin. Text stays at 15–16px with the first paragraph in medium weight. Padding is 72px above and 96px below.

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets running, list or table text at 15px: use your body step (16px or more, 15px at the least on a phone). The inspector fails it under 14px and warns under 16px (`small-body`).
- It keeps most of the width as untouched ground beside its content: no section is huddled on one side (`lopsided`). Set its words across the grid at a size that fills it, or put the section's picture or figure on the other side.
- It asks for a photograph, and the page's photograph total leaves none here (section 5): build it from its words, the colour and the dealt card style, with no picture.
- It holds up to 150 words: a section carries 60 at most outside a list or a table (section 7). Cut it to two or three sentences.

### 4. cta — Question at both edges (statement)
For a closing choice between two answers: book or ask, this date or that, the tasting or the tour. A flat field exactly 100svh tall in the darkest tone. The question is a two-line heading at clamp(110px, 11vw, 168px), leading 1.0, centred, about 54% of the width: line one with its cap line 32px under the field's top edge, line two with its baseline 24px above the bottom edge. The band of about 560px between them is empty except for one row at its optical centre: two solid rectangular buttons, each about 200×56px with its label at 18px in medium weight, their inner edges 120px either side of the centre axis, and between them, exactly on the axis, the word 'or' at 18px in the soft ink. 16px under the row, centred, one line at 16px in the soft ink says what happens after either choice. The type above and below closes the empty band, so it reads as held breath rather than a gap.
On a phone: The question drops to 18vw, still two centred lines, line one 32px from the top and line two 24px above the bottom. The buttons stack at the centre at 72% of the width, 12px apart, with 'or' between them and the line of explanation under the second.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
A filled panel on the surface tone, inset by the page margin, in the dealt corner, as tall as its content, about 700px at 1440, with 120px top padding. A centred logo lockup 56px high; 56px below it a centred row of up to seven primary links in the strong weight at the lead step on a 160px pitch (the site's other pages where it has them; on a one-page site its own sections and the contact; never a dead link), hover underlining in the ink. 96px under that, three left-aligned link columns on a 300px pitch starting at x 160 (titles at the lead step in the strong weight, body-size links), and at the far right a Back to top text link. The last row sits 80px below: the legal line at the left, the social text links and an Image credits link at the right, body size. Ink is the dealt ink on the surface tone. Phone: Lockup and primary links in a 2-up grid; columns stack. House rules for it: Designed out: the soft-tint variant with 50px top corners rising into view (a second radius and its own entrance), the 56px round back-to-top disc (a text link), the brand colour as the panel's ground (the surface tone). A rule and a row of membership marks would break the hairline and logo-wall bans; both are left out. The panel is one card, not boxes in boxes. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Edge-to-edge marquee
One line of very large type travels sideways across the full width at a slow constant speed, as a band between two sections or as the closing statement. Linear timing, duplicated content for a seamless loop, paused on hover and under reduced motion. One marquee per page, carrying real words, never a list of buzzwords. Its words are new information (the services, the formats, the clients the page can name), never the hero's line again.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Set type (`set-type`), dealt
This is the masked line rise with the clip-path shutter: once every page's default, now one entrance of seven and rarely dealt. Build it as base.css has it.
- **What the visitor sees:** The headline's lines rise out of their own baselines one after another while the picture opens downward like a blind; the sentence and the action rise 12 px after them. Settled by 1.2 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="set-type">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `set-type` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=set-type`.
- **Below the fold: Headings by line.** Chapter headings (data-lines, five at most) rise line by line from their masks once, using the "Lines that rise on scroll" script in motion.md. Nothing else enters, and a stray data-reveal does nothing.

### Small moves — one, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build this once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Brackets close in** (`mo-text-bracket-close-in`): A single word or two short words set large as the section's statement (display step, display face, ink, centred), flanked by two bracket glyphs from the same face at 1.2x the word's height. Scrubbed by scroll (from 85% to 50% of the viewport height) the brackets start 300px further out on each side and slide inward until they sit 0.2em from the word; the word itself is always fully visible. After landing everything holds still; a paragraph (lead step, 44 characters) sits 56px under it. Ground as dealt, accent none. Phone: word at the head step, brackets travel 120px. House rules for it: Never a small bracketed label above a heading (an eyebrow): the brackets are typographic marks around the large statement word itself; the page's own face, never a monospace; brackets stay ink, not a styled accent; scroll-linked, so the dealt entrance stands. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, tested and ready to fill. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 2 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Thumbnail becomes the stage (`thumbnail-becomes-the-stage`) — carries the features section
A statement of one or two lines at display size sits over a row of large thumbnails on the layout's columns, whose middle one is a keyhole onto a full-screen chapter; from the first step of the scroll the keyhole grows over its neighbours as they slide in under it, rises and opens to full bleed, and the statement passes through its edge, ink outside and paper inside, sliding from centred to flush left to become the chapter's heading word for word. It opens a product, a place or a film chapter from one strong muted loop or one strong picture. Hold: 2 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle, as the door from the intro into the showcase or the work; the opened picture carries the chapter's feature notes on a solid band at its foot, and below 600px they follow it in the page's flow.
Content: One chapter picture or muted loop of 8-15 s (16:9, 1920x1080 or more, 8MB at most, with a poster; for software, a screen recording of the product), its subject in the centre: the keyhole opens on about the central half of its width and three quarters of its height on desktop (that crop is the thumbnail); its upper left calm and dark for the white heading; a 4:5 cut for phones. One or two satellite stills a side of neighbouring chapters in the same grade, 960px wide or more with the subject in the centre (the row crops them close to square on desktop, 3:4 on tablets, 3:5 on phones). No text burned into the pictures. A statement of up to two lines of 20 characters or fewer that names the chapter and becomes its heading word for word; an optional note under the heading; three or four feature items of a 2-3 word label and 16 words or fewer.
Options (data-options): hold 2 (1.4-2.6); phoneHold 1.6 (1.4-2.6); phone 'same' | 'rest'; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis (0-0.8); thumb, the layout's columns by default (0.12-0.5; a share under the columns is lifted to them, a larger one runs the neighbours off the edges); radius, the page's --radius or 0 (0-16 px), always 0 at full bleed; shade 0.3 (0-0.6) flat scrim over the picture under the heading; band 1 (0-1) the band under the notes, solid by default; heading 0.15 (0.1-0.25) where the heading settles, the statement starting at the same height; nav '', the selector of a fixed nav that gets is-on-dark over the picture.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.js` and `thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/thumbnail-becomes-the-stage.html`. On a phone: The same opening with the keyhole starting at 64% of the width, 3:5, its neighbours running off the edges; hold 1.6. From 600px all four notes show two by two on the band; below 600px wide or under 540px tall they leave the stage and follow the picture in the page's flow, every one shown.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
