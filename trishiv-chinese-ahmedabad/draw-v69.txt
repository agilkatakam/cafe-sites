## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`bottom-dock`):** A dock at the bottom: at the top of the first screen only the wordmark, at the left margin, which scrolls away with the hero. The links and the action live in a capsule fixed 20px above the bottom edge and centred, there from the first screen on, 56px tall, as wide as its content, filled with the ink and lettered in the ground colour (on a dark page the surface tone with ink letters), opaque, no border, no shadow: three or four section names at 15-16px, the current one at weight 600, and the action as its one filled item in the accent. On a phone the dock spans the width less the margins, 60px plus the safe area, each target 44px or taller, and the page keeps 96px free under its last line. It slides down out of view while a pinned scene or the replay holds the screen, and comes back after. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as links and the one action. **Must show:** The links and the action live in a capsule in the ink fixed 20px above the bottom edge; at the top only the wordmark.
- **Footer (`big-link-list`):** Large links on the ink: the page's sections and Contact stacked as links at the h2 step in the display face on the left two thirds (on hover each moves 8px and takes the accent); on the right third the email, the city with a Directions link (the full visit block stands in the section above) and the social links at body size; the legal line and Image credits at the foot. About 70svh. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). **Must show:** The sections and Contact stacked as links at the h2 step in the display face on the ink, the contact at body size in the right third.
- **Cards (`card-photo-text-top-bottom-row`, Photo, text top and bottom: a pattern of the study):** Three photo cards of one height, 464px at 1440, in mixed widths (portrait 400, landscape 480, portrait 400) on 16px gaps, together filling the container (1312) with all three whole, none cut by the screen edge; the dealt corner on each. Choose pictures with a quiet top and bottom: the words sit on those calm, dark parts; where a picture has none, a flat black scrim at 40-55% covers only each text block (never a gradient, never a wash over the whole picture). Inside, a 20px medium title sits at the top-left, 28px in, and a 20px paragraph of at most 14 words (two to three lines) at the bottom-left at the same inset, both in the light ink. Colour roles: ground = the page ground, text on the pictures = the light ink, accent unused. On hover the picture scales 1.03. Pictures are rendered, product or abstract; never documentary. No arrows, no track, no sideways scrolling on desktop. House rules for it: Words sit on the pictures' calm parts or on a flat scrim under the text only, never a gradient or a wash over the picture; all three cards whole inside the margins and nothing scrolls sideways on desktop. On a phone: Cards stack one per row at full width, 400 tall; the text keeps the same top and bottom positions. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** Three photo cards of one 464px height in unequal widths (400, 480, 400), each with a 20px title at the top-left and a short paragraph at the bottom-left, lettered straight onto the picture.
- **Buttons (`wipe-fill`):** A fill that wipes in: fully round, 52px tall, 28px padding, the text face at 16px weight 500; at rest the surface tone with an ink label, on hover the accent wipes in from the left (a scaleX layer behind the label, 300ms) and the label turns the accent's ink. The second action is a text link. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** A fully round button in the surface tone that the accent wipes across from the left on hover.
- **Corners:** A 4px radius on cards, panels and fields; pictures square or at the same 4px. (`--radius: 4px`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Airy: 22-28vh between sections (200-250px at 1440, 112-140px on a phone), page margins 48-72px at 1440 and 24px on a phone. In CSS: `--section-gap: clamp(7rem, 12vh + 7vw, 15.75rem); --margin: clamp(1.5rem, 4.5vw, 4.5rem);`, the gap set as `padding-block: calc(var(--section-gap) / 2)` on every section after the hero and as `padding-bottom` of the same half under the hero's last content, so it is the same on one ground or across two (references/layout.md §2). **The first screen is the hero alone:** `min-height: 100svh` at every width with the navigation inside it, and nothing of the next section (its heading, text, picture or ground) in view at 1440 by 900 or 390 by 844 (`hero-not-alone`). The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).
- **Interactive piece:** No interactive widget. The page is read: its content, pictures and motion carry it. Controls are the navigation, the links, the action and any real form.
- **Video:** No video. Motion comes from the page itself.

## 1. The idea
For a place, the photograph is the argument. One full-bleed image of where you would be standing fills the first screen, and the design steps back: the name sits whole on the bottom margin like a film title, or small in a corner, and nothing else interrupts the view. The page that follows keeps that restraint: few words, large pictures, long pauses. The designer's work here is editing: choosing the one frame that makes someone want to be there, and then not covering it.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: a single photograph at 100vw by 100svh, focal point kept clear of text. The name is set either very large (14–20vw) whole on the bottom margin, no letter cut by any edge, or small (20–28px) top-left with a one-line description bottom-left. Navigation is two to four words in the top corners in the same small size. If text sits on the image, a flat dark scrim under the text area only. Below, sections alternate between a short centred or left-set paragraph in a narrow column (max 52ch) with wide empty margins, and photographs shown large: one full-bleed, then a pair at different sizes, then one portrait offset to the right. Practical information (hours, address, rates, booking) is gathered once near the end in a plain two-column block.

## 3. Type — Panchang over Funnel Sans
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.333 scale from a 17px body; the hero's display step is about 4.6vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Panchang only for the display and head steps; Funnel Sans for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://api.fontshare.com/v2/css?f[]=panchang@700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Funnel+Sans:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Panchang** — weight 700, sentence case, tracking -0.015em, leading 0.98
- Text: **Funnel Sans** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0625rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.277rem, 0.766rem + 0.63vw, 1.419rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.510rem, 0.906rem + 0.94vw, 1.887rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.887rem, 1.132rem + 1.40vw, 2.519rem);
  --step-display: clamp(2.015rem, 1.209rem + 2.88vw, 4.175rem);
  --step-hero: clamp(2.015rem, min(17.50vw, 56svh), 15.750rem);
}
```
`--step-hero` is the hero headline's own size (section 6): its blueprint sets it at about 252px at 1440, where `--step-display` stops at 61px. The first screen's `h1` takes it and nothing else does: in the dealt display face and weight, two lines at most, every word whole between the margins (where the longest line would not fit, the size comes down until it does).
A face with some grace for the name; small, calm, well-spaced text everywhere else. Sentence case. Body 17–19px with leading around 1.55.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-hero`, the one size beside them.

## 4. Colour — Brown and olive
```css
:root {
  --bg: #472913;
  --surface: #603c24;
  --ink: #e4e0cf;
  --muted: #c3af93;
  --accent: #525e1b;
  --accent-ink: #e4e0cf;
  --field-1: #525e1b;  --field-1-ink: #e4e0cf;
}
```
A studied palette with its other fields left out: brown ground, sand ink, one olive accent for the primary action. Two grounds and one band: the page ground and --surface, and the olive accent, one section at most. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product. The accent reads under 3:1 on this ground, so, whatever this note says before, it is a fill with its own text colour (the button, one block), never the colour of a word, a link or a thin mark: those take the ink.
Neutral paper or deep neutral ground chosen to flatter the photographs; text ink drawn from the darkest tone in the imagery. Colour lives in the pictures.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 5. Imagery
**The project has no photographs of its own yet.** Plan the set before building: sharp pictures of one family, as many as the photograph total below allows (one light, one treatment) that show the subject's world, each shown once, the scenes' pictures counted among them (a gallery or a wall of twelve is cut to six); a slot with no good picture left goes to type, never to the same picture at another crop. They show the world around the client and never pass for its people, its team or its customers. Where no honest picture exists, leave the slot to type and space; never a drawn illustration in its place (references/imagery.md §8). List every picture and its source in your report.

**What the pictures show for this brief:** the subject's world: its place, its materials and the details that belong to no one. **Never:** another trade's tools or goods; a skyline, a bridge or a landmark the brief does not name. The look-and-fix drops a picture that does not show the client's own world, however good it is.

**The menu stands in type: a dish or drink the page names (a flat white at 3.80) is never shown by a stock picture of someone else's.** The client has no photographs of its food and drink yet, so each item is its name, a short line and its price in the page's type (a list, or the card style section 0 deals where the items are read as a group), with no photograph beside or under it. The photographs dealt below (the cup, the pour, the beans, the street) only set the mood, away from the menu: never captioned with an item's name or price, and never a plated dish passed off as one of the menu's (a café's page showed stock drinks as its own menu). The report names the menu as the first thing to photograph.

Fewer photographs, chosen better: no more of the subject and its world than the photograph total below allows (the rooms, dishes or products a page pictures are their own set, one photograph each beside its facts), each one modern, well lit and professional-looking (the quality of a good Unsplash or Pexels photograph), or none at all, with the product, the type, the colour and the interface carrying the page. **Never documentary or archive photographs:** no old Commons or museum pictures, no history photographs, postcards, old maps or engravings, no news-like or snapshot-looking shots; they made recent pages look serious, old and cheap, and the owner named them first. Order of sources: (1) the project's own pictures; (2) pictures you generate, if you have an image tool (the best way to show this exact subject); (3) the dealt photographs below and the rest of the library (`node <skill>/scripts/draw.mjs --photos <words>`): sharp and modern, free of credits or, from Wikimedia Commons, credited by author and licence as their line says; (4) only for what the library lacks (a detail of a room, the product's use and load; never a dish or a drink a menu names, which is the client's own photograph or type), photographs from the open collections (`node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs "fresh herbs close up"`, then "market vegetables close up", then queries from your inventory (its place, its materials, its use and its details; never the client's own thing: the hard rule below)), only sharp modern photographs at 1800px or more, never from the museum sources, each looked at full size first and cropped or dropped if it shows another maker's name or logo, a shop or company sign, a readable house number or licence plate, words that would embarrass the client, or a child who could be recognised (references/imagery.md §2), never retouched or blurred instead (no blurred or pixelated face: take shots with faces turned away, out of frame or naturally out of focus, or frame the load and the place), never one whose title or credit names another business (a real hotel's room) shown as the client's own, and every one credited in the Image credits dialog (`--credits` prints it), never in the footer. **When the search fails** (the script retries, and searches Wikimedia Commons when Openverse fails or finds nothing), take the dealt photographs and the library; a drawn diagram, chart or illustration on the page fails, labels or not, so say a process in words: `drawn-illustration`). Logos of known products the page names (Gmail, Figma, Slack, Chrome, ChatGPT, Claude) are the real marks, never drawn: `node /tmp/taste-skill/skills/taste-skill/scripts/assets.mjs --logo gmail,figma` saves them from Simple Icons at a pinned version, and a brand missing there comes from its own press kit, unchanged; never an initial in a circle in its place. The dealt set is the palette. Choose four to six photographs with the same light. The hero image must have a calm area for the name. Full-bleed or large with square corners; never thumbnails, never a carousel of ten.

**Pictures: fewer, better, larger (the inspector checks them).** No more pictures of the subject and its world than the photograph total below, scenes included, each requested at least 1.5 times the size it is shown (twice for the hero), all with the treatment dealt in section 0. Before placing one, look at it: if it is soft, random, or could belong to any site, leave the slot out instead. Not the category's stock shot (a flat lay of ingredients, a chef plating with tweezers) unless it is art-directed for this page; the client's own pictures come first, and without them its use, its load, its place and the details that belong to no one, never its own thing by another's (references/imagery.md §2). No thumbnails inside sentences, no pictures inside headlines, no small pictures filling a gap, no caption under a picture unless it says something the visitor needs. Each photograph once on the page, never again as a crop (a scene's stamp of the picture it has just shown excepted); a picture under or beside a line shows what the line names; a photograph of a place that is not the client's own is captioned with what and where it is, as its source names it, never with a place the source does not name. SVG drawings do not count as pictures. Every file the page asks for exists: a `src`, a `srcset` crop or a CSS background that is not there fails (`missing-image`). Words on a photograph sit on its calm part, of one tone, at 1440, 768 and 390 alike, since the crop moves as the screen narrows (`text-on-photo`), with no gradient, fade or milky fog laid over it. Video only as section 0 deals it.

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the

**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). **The visit: this is a place people go to, so the page says how to get there** (in the contact section): the street, a real street in the right district of the town the brief names, with no house number (the brief gives none), or for a remote place or a brief that names only a region, the village it stands in (a real one in that place, chosen and marked as sample copy when the brief names none) and the nearest town with the road from it; the district or area; the nearest stop and its line (read from a map in this session or one you are sure of, otherwise the district's main station), or for a remote place the drive from that town with its time, and from the airport or city most visitors come from (from the road distance on a map, or at about 60 km an hour on country roads when no map can be read, rounded up; never a time that makes the place nearer than it is: a Westfjords house once claimed forty minutes from Reykjavik); the opening hours; a "Directions" link to a maps service searching that street, village or road and town; and one contact (the email on the brand's own domain, on `.example` where the name is coined, since the brief gives no phone). ### Each arrival pushes the deck back a rung (`lib-0194`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the list section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. Each new item slides up into the front slot of a deck seen in depth while every item already there steps one rung back, smaller and higher, and the oldest fades out, so a list is laid down like records dropped into a crate and one whole item always rests in front.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Back items show only a top strip, so items that look alike (similar covers, same ground) turn the rungs to mush; separate them by alternating ground and ink or by a word set at the top edge, never by a colour per item. Short or landscape windows squeeze the stack into the top half when the front item is tall. Fewer than about five items shows the end too soon; more than eight makes a long hold. An endless wrapping deck hides the footer and fights find, keyboard paging and screen readers, so release after one pass. An entrance that opens from a slit is the overused clip-path shutter, and a backdrop blur on the receding items is glass; use scale and fade only. Sticky stacking breaks under any ancestor with overflow hidden. Phones need a shallower depth and the whole front item in view.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a design studio's six services, pin one section. Each service is a full-width flat poster card carrying one huge word at its top edge and two lines of copy under it, the cards alternating the page's ground and ink so neighbouring rungs separate without a third colour. The next card slides up flat from below the fold into the front slot while every card already in the deck steps one rung back and up, shrinking to about 0.84 and then 0.72, toward a vanishing point at the upper right so the deck recedes on a diagonal; only three rungs show and the fourth fades as it goes, so the word on each back card's top strip still names it. About a third of a screen of scroll per card, with a soft settle so one card always rests in front. When the deck rests, one large sentence-case line under it cuts (no fade) to the front card's summary, and the single accent marks only the front card's link. After the sixth card the pin lets go into ordinary scroll: no loop, no counter. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 2 screens of scroll: `hold: 2` in `Stage.scene`, its section 3 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** its own version, as the direction above says (`phone: { hold, build }` in `Stage.scene`), or `phone: 'rest'` with a finished composition that reads whole; never the desktop move squeezed. Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 2, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

### A still closing line in a field of words (`closing-line-in-a-field-of-words`) — carries the contact section
The closing section holds one line still at the lower left of a calm ground, at display size in two lines with the page's one action after its last word, while a stream of single short words (the places, dishes, materials or services the page is about) comes out of a vanishing point above it: each word appears at a readable size, grows quickly in one flat ink as it travels out along one of six to eight fixed lanes, and fades to nothing while it is still whole on screen, so no word is ever cut by the edge and at most six are drawn at once. The last word grows out of the same point while a last wave is still passing; it stops at its full size over the closing line and stays as the section's heading. Each lane is a wedge round the vanishing point and every word grows about that point, so no word ever touches another, the line, the action or the heading, and nothing is searched. Opacity and scale only: no blur, no 3D layers; the field is hidden from screen readers. For a page with no pictures, or none to spare for its close. Hold: 1 screen. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: The end of the page: the call to action or the contact, straight after a quiet content section, before a short visit section and the footer. Once per page, never right after another scene, and not on a software page (its big moment is the product replay). The section's heading is the last word, so write the closing around it.
Content: No pictures. 16-30 real single words of the subject (its dishes, places, materials or services; never client or partner names), 9 letters or fewer, in an aria-hidden list with the best first (a desktop flies about the first 25, a phone about the first 14); phrases do not fit. One last word of 4-9 letters that can carry the close as a heading (a time, a place, a verb: 'Tonight', 'Aboard', 'Visit'). One closing line of 9-12 words in two clauses of about 22 characters each (44 in all), a <br> between them, two lines at display size on every screen, a phone included (a statement, so it reads in a breath and a short line is never held alone over an empty stage; 'Off the boats at dawn,<br> on the table by seven.'). Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
The photographs, at full size, uninterrupted. Empty paper around short text makes the next image land harder.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A weak hero photograph cannot be fixed by typography; pick again. Do not stack a headline, a paragraph and two buttons on the image. Do not use a booking bar that covers the bottom of the view; one small action is enough.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
