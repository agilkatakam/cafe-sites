## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.

Every section after the hero keeps its content in columns 3-12, so the margins stay equal and nothing runs under the list: map each blueprint's columns 1-12 onto columns 3-12. ## 1. The idea
A page with the directness of a street poster: flat saturated colour, the heaviest type you can find, short blunt statements, and photographs cut out and stuck on. It is for subjects with a voice. Write the page as things a person would actually say out loud, and let the design say them at full volume. The craft is in the control beneath the noise: a strict grid, two or three colours, and exact alignment keep it from becoming a mess.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: a statement of three to six words in ultra-heavy type at 11–15vw, filling the width, lines stacked tight (leading 0.85), flush left with 20–28px margins, on a flat saturated ground. One cut-out or hard-cropped photograph overlaps the type from the right or bottom, tilted 3–6 degrees. A single chunky button in the contrasting flat colour. Sections are solid panels on the page's grounds in turn (the ground, the surface tone, and once the band section 4 deals in the ink or the accent): one is a row of short statements each on its own panel of unequal width; one is a single photograph bleeding off two edges with a caption block set over a solid colour bar; one is a large plain list. Panels meet edge to edge. Corners are square or use one large radius (28–40px) consistently.

## 3. Type — Zilla Slab, a slab
A slab. A 1.2 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Zilla Slab does every job, told apart by size and weight (700 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Zilla+Slab:wght@400;600;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Zilla Slab** — weight 700, sentence case, tracking -0.012em, leading 1.03
- Text: **Zilla Slab** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8313rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.080rem, 0.648rem + 0.53vw, 1.200rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.315rem, 0.789rem + 0.82vw, 1.644rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.644rem, 0.986rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(1.890rem, 1.134rem + 3.10vw, 4.500rem);
  --step-hero: clamp(1.890rem, min(9.79vw, 34svh), 8.813rem);
}
```
`--step-hero` is the hero headline's own size (section 6): its blueprint sets it at about 141px at 1440, where `--step-display` stops at 63px. The first screen's `h1` takes it and nothing else does: in the dealt display face and weight, two lines at most, every word whole between the margins (where the longest line would not fit, the size comes down until it does).
The heaviest weight available, set tight, mostly lowercase or mostly uppercase (choose one). Body text is a plain grotesk at 18–20px.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-hero`, the one size beside them.
**Type note (`type-serif-display-and-body-sans-labels`, One serif for display and reading): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one transitional serif sets the display and the reading text; a neo-grotesque appears only in small interface text. Display at 5vw in mixed case (72px at 1440, lh 1.0, -0.03em, one or two lines of at most 20 characters), h2 54px, and a large reading body at 30px (lh 1.2, max 44 characters, at most three lines a paragraph). Captions and fine print in the sans at 14px sentence case 500; buttons and navigation are the dealt ones. Ratio about 2.4x. No italic on words: italics only for a whole pull quote or an attribution line. Colour: the dealt ground and ink; the accent only on the action fill. Motion: arrives with the dealt entrance. House rules for it: No italic words; captions are 14px sentence case. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (700), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets words on a photograph: put them on its calm part, of one tone, and look again at 768 and 390, where the crop moves; the inspector reads the pixels under the letters at every width (`text-on-photo`).
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Zilla Slab, text Zilla Slab) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.
- It names colours (grey): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro the scene, features list, showcase single-picture. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. intro — the scene `lib-0105` (section 9)

### 2. features — Capabilities at poster scale (list)
No images. The heading, two to four words, is the first line of the list itself, set at the same size as the items but in the soft ink, so no separate title sits above. Five to eight capability names follow, one per line, at clamp(40px, 5.2vw, 84px), medium to heavy weight, leading 1.0, tight tracking, flush left on the page margin across columns 1–8 of 12. Each name's one-sentence description is set at 16–18px in columns 9–12, its first baseline aligned with the name's baseline (CSS grid, align-items: first baseline). Each description sits directly under its name at 16px, indented one margin width, with 28px before the next name. The soft-ink heading line stays as the first row. All rows at full ink.

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Set type (`set-type`), dealt
This is the masked line rise with the clip-path shutter: once every page's default, now one entrance of seven and rarely dealt. Build it as base.css has it.
- **What the visitor sees:** The headline's lines rise out of their own baselines one after another while the picture opens downward like a blind; the sentence and the action rise 12 px after them. Settled by 1.2 s.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="set-type">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child). Wrap each headline line: `<span class="line"><span data-line style="--i:0">…</span></span>`, three at most, with breaks that fit on one line at every width down to 360 px (a smaller display step on phones if they must).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `set-type` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=set-type`.
- **Below the fold: Headings by line.** Chapter headings (data-lines, five at most) rise line by line from their masks once, using the "Lines that rise on scroll" script in motion.md. Nothing else enters, and a stray data-reveal does nothing.

### Small moves — one, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build this once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Char blur scrub** (`mo-text-char-blur-scrub`): A short headline of at most 10 words at the head step on two lines (or a statement of 14 words at most at 36px or less on three lines), ink on the ground, split into characters. It is scroll-linked (0.6 smoothing) over 400-720px of scroll as the block travels from 85 to 40 percent of the viewport: each character comes into focus from blur 4-12px to blur 0, staggered left to right and line by line so the focus runs like a wave of three to five letters. The text is in full ink and in place from the first frame (nothing fades or moves in; arrival is the dealt entrance's): only the focus changes. On the way out the same values reverse once the block passes 20 percent from the top. It is fully crisp before it has to be read. One type step, no colour change per character. Phone: blur peaks at 8px, splitting limited to 80 characters to protect the frame rate; the parent keeps an aria-label with the whole text. House rules for it: No accent word, no coloured letters; letters never move or fade in, only their focus changes, and the real words are present whole; the headline is two lines at most or the statement 36px or less on three lines. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moment: one scroll scene, a researched technique to build fresh. It is part of the composition, so build it in step 6 with the section it carries, not as a motion afterthought. It runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** This scene holds about 1 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The example is not the content.** The direction a technique gives is written for another client: its business, its objects and its words are examples. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the example's content is unfinished.

### Type sets like wet ink (`lib-0105`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. Display type exists first as swollen, merged ink blobs and sharpens into clean letters line by line as it reaches its place on screen, because a blur is pushed through a hard alpha threshold that relaxes as the blur clears, so words seem to set like wet ink rather than fade in.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Thin, light or serif faces lose their hairlines in the threshold and settle looking bolder than set. Over photographs or busy grounds the blobs read as dirt. One SVG filter per line is costly: more than four or five moving at once stutters on phones and in some browsers, so attach the filter only while a line moves and remove it at rest. Fonts must load before the lines are split. Reduced motion needs a plain cut to crisp type. Used on every heading of a page it becomes a tic.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a mineral-water brand's manifesto, set four short lines of a heavy round grotesk in one ink on a flat chalk ground, one line per screen of native scroll. Each line condenses out of chunky blobs as it rises from the lower third to the centre, and stays crisp once set, with the blur at 8px at most, so the reader sets the pace line by line; the last line stays crisp with the one button under it. Nothing else on the page melts: every other heading cuts or slides plainly, no accent colour in the lines, no paragraph or drawings around them. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 1 screen of scroll (the research ran it without a pin; on the stage it holds one screen): `hold: 1` in `Stage.scene`, its section 2 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** the same move at phone size (`phone: 'same'`). Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 1, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).
