Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A part marked as a pattern is described as the study saw it: its description is your spec, built with this page's own content, in the dealt type, colour and corners (where it names a colour, a face, a radius or a size the dealt system lacks, the dealt system wins), within the rules printed after it. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.

## 1. The idea
The name is the headline. Set it so large that it becomes architecture: as wide as the margins allow, or stacked to fill the screen, every letter whole. Nothing else on the first screen competes; the description of what this is drops to a single small sentence. The idea comes from treating the name's letters as material. Ask what the subject can do to its own name: a photograph of the work stands behind it or opens under it, the name repeats down the page like a reel, the letters are split to opposite corners and the page happens between them (never a picture inside the letters or between the words). In the study this was the single most common device among sites scoring 7.5 or more.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
First screen: the wordmark spans the width between the page margins (font-size 16–24vw, set with a clamp so it never wraps), either whole on the bottom margin with no letter cut by any edge, or dead centre with everything else pushed to the corners. One line of supporting text at 16–20px sits in a corner on a shared margin; navigation is three or four words in the opposite corner. After the hero the page is deliberately small and calm: a narrow text column or a simple two-column grid, so the contrast with the monument holds. The footer is the dealt one, at text sizes: the name does not return there at full width. The name keeps the margins section 0 deals like everything else, every letter whole. Two type sizes do nearly all the work: the monument and a small text size, with one mid size for section headings.

## 3. Type — Funnel Display over Figtree
A display face over a plain sans. A 1.414 scale from a 18px body; the hero's display step is about 9vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Funnel Display only for the display and head steps; Figtree for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Funnel+Display:wght@500&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Figtree:wght@400;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Funnel Display** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.037em, leading 0.89
- Text: **Figtree** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.1250rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.350rem, 0.810rem + 0.67vw, 1.500rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.800rem, 1.080rem + 1.13vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-head: clamp(2.250rem, 1.350rem + 1.77vw, 3.181rem);
  --step-display: clamp(3.402rem, 2.041rem + 5.58vw, 8.100rem);
}
```
One family. The display cut must survive enormous sizes: tight tracking (-0.04 to -0.07em), leading 0.8–0.9, optical kerning on. Section headings are modest (28–40px) on purpose.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.

A single rosy grey covers the page from top to bottom with brown-black text and grey secondary lines; pictures keep the treatment section 0 deals, greyscale only where it deals black-and-white. Green appears only on the current-page link and on one button in the footer.
Two values carry the page (ground and ink). The accent, if used at all, appears on one thing only: the single action.
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).

Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

## 6. Hero — Colour-block collage, crossing headline (`hero-colour-block-collage-crossing-headline`, a pattern of the design-anatomy study)

It is drawn once per ink (the extra copies hidden from screen readers, each clipped to its blocks), so letters change colour exactly where a block edge cuts them. The dealt button sits in the bottom-left block under the headline. No picture, no discs, no gradient. The pointer shifts the block boundaries by up to 4% (smooth); on scroll the blocks hold and the next section starts on the ground. Colour roles: the ground, the surface tone and the ink field only, plus their inks; the accent is the button. House rules for it: Three flat tones of the palette (ground, surface, ink field), no fourth colour, no blended discs, no gradient. One real h1; the clipped copies are decoration.

On a phone: The blocks re-cut into three full-width horizontal bands (ground, surface tone, ink field); the headline at the display step crosses the band edges at the bottom with the same clipped copies; the dealt button sits in the last band above the bottom.
Headline: The page's own idea, in this client's words, on two lines at most (12 words or fewer).
**Must show:** Three hard flat blocks (62% by 55% top-left, 62% by 45% bottom-left, 38% full height right) fill the first screen and the two-line headline crosses their edges, its letters switching ink exactly at the edge of the ink-field block.
**Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (500), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** list statement, gallery gallery, about the scene, cta statement. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 1. list — One item per poster line (statement)
The hand-over scene `seam-pocket-gathers-the-words` (section 9) opens this section; its "Where" line says what of the section it carries, and the rest follows in this shape.
A short list set as a poster. Four to seven items (services, headline acts, courses, opening days) stack one per line at clamp(64px, 10vw, 150px) in the display weight, all capitals or all lowercase, leading 0.92, flush left at a 20px margin on a flat field. Its one detail (a price, a date, a time range) sits at the right margin on the same baseline at 18–20px with tabular figures, so the page has two alignments only: giant words left, small facts right. Items too long for one line at this size are shortened, not shrunk. Between items there is only the tight leading. Hovering an item swaps its ink for the accent. There is no heading: the first item starts 80px below the section top and the last ends 80px above the bottom. The list reuses the hero title's exact type size, so it reads as another poster.
On a phone: Items at 15vw, one per line, flush left with 16px margins. Each detail moves beneath its item at 15px, left-aligned, with 6px above and 22px below. If any single word is wider than the screen at 15vw, drop the whole list to 12vw rather than breaking a word.

Adjusted for this page:
- It names a kind of typeface: use the dealt faces (display Funnel Display, text Figtree) and their settings; capitals only where the type system sets them.

### 2. gallery — Photographs gathered round a heading (gallery)
A band about one screen tall on the plain page ground, used as the page's one deliberate rest. At its visual centre sits the heading, two lines at clamp(32px, 2.6vw, 40px) in a light weight, centred, with one sentence at 17px under it and a text link beneath that. Around it six to eight small photographs are scattered irregularly across the middle columns (x 320–1120; the outer 320px on each side stay empty, equally), each 160–200px wide at about 9:10, square corners, at different heights with 60–200px of air between them. Two of them come within 40px of the heading's block, which their drift never reaches, so the cluster reads as gathered round it rather than laid on a grid. No captions and no frames. Each photograph drifts at its own rate as the band passes (4–12% of its own height on a scroll-linked timeline), so the cluster loosens and tightens; under reduced motion it stays put. The pictures come from one place or one day and share one treatment.
On a phone: Five photographs at 30% of the width sit offset left and right around the centred heading, two above it and three below, none cut by an edge. The heading drops to 7vw on two lines.

Adjusted for this page:
- It asks for 8 photographs: on this page it takes 4, the best (section 5 counts the page's photographs), and a plain line of names for the rest where it lists more.

### 3. about — the scene `letter-physics` (section 9)
This section is the scene: what it says (the about) is set in the scene as section 9 says, with no second shape around it and no blueprint here.

### 4. cta — The idea again, inverted (statement)
The close flips the page's colours: ink becomes the ground and the ground becomes the ink, across a full-bleed field at least 90svh tall. The page's opening idea returns as a headline of three to five words at 9–11vw on two lines, leading 0.9, flush left, positioned in the upper two-thirds; it should echo the hero's wording, not repeat it. Along the bottom, on one shared baseline 40px above the edge: one supporting sentence at 20px in columns 1–5 and one rectangular button in the accent colour, right-aligned in columns 10–12, about 56px tall. Nothing else: no image, no second button, no logo. The field's top edge is a hard horizontal line where it meets the section above. If the footer follows on the same inverted field, leave 160px before it so the close still reads as its own screen.
On a phone: The same inverted field at 80svh. Headline at 15vw on two lines at the top. The sentence sits lower at 17px, and the button spans the full width at the bottom, 56px tall, with 20px margins. Nothing else is added.

### 5. footer — as dealt in section 0
Large links on the ink: the page's sections and Contact stacked as links at the h2 step in the display face on the left two thirds (on hover each moves 8px and takes the accent); on the right third the email, the city and the social links at body size; the legal line and Image credits at the foot. About 70svh. Build it as dealt, every part and size of it, never a line of credits or a © row in its place (`footer-not-as-dealt`).

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Already there (`already-there`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Nothing arrives. The first frame is the finished first screen. The only motion is inside the media: a clip that plays, a product that responds to the pointer, a picture that fades in only if it arrived late. Nothing on the first screen moves. Settled by the first frame.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="already-there">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"` and `data-e="action"` (no picture on this first screen).
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `already-there` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=already-there`.
- **Below the fold: Nothing.** Nothing enters below the fold, and no element on the page gets data-reveal. Section content is simply there, and motion comes from the moves the sheet deals, any moving picture and feedback under the hand. Lazy pictures get the same arrival courtesy as the hero: they wait on their ground colour and fade in when they land. That is loading, not a reveal.

### Small moves — three, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build each of these once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Mask roll swap** (`mo-text-mask-roll-swap`): When a state changes (a slide, a tab, a disc, a list item) the text that describes it, a title, a price, a date or a one-to-three-word value at the lead, sub or head step, rolls out and the new text rolls in. The old text moves up 100 percent out of a mask that is exactly one line tall plus 0.1em at the bottom so descenders survive, in 300-420ms on cubic-bezier 0.77, 0, 0.18, 1; the new text enters from below 60ms later, words staggered 30ms. Labels beside it slide 12-23px. If the new title wraps to two lines the container animates its height in the same 300ms. The roll follows the direction of navigation (next goes up, previous goes down). It plays only after the visitor's own action, never on load or on scroll. Phone: the same, 260ms. House rules for it: The mask only clips the text itself, no outlined box; whole words roll, never single letters; it runs only after the visitor's own action, so it is not an entrance. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.
- **Late word slides in** (`mo-parallax-word-slides-into-line`): A hero headline of three to five words at the display step over two lines. The last word starts about 96px below its line, fully visible from the first frame, so the sentence already reads whole; across 60svh of scroll, in a hero held that long, it slides up (scrubbed, eased) into its line and closes the gap. Every word shares one face, colour and weight. The hero still fills the first screen alone. On a phone the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. House rules for it: The sliding word has exactly the same face, colour, weight and style as the rest (no accent word); it is a real word of the sentence; the hero still fills the first screen alone and reads whole in the first frame. On a phone: Display step on the phone; the word starts 64px below and runs over 50svh. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On the hero's headline it runs on scroll only, once the entrance has set the headline in place. On one or two large pictures or groups, never on every one; still for reduced motion.
- **Rounded tile goes edge** (`mo-scale-rounded-tile-widen`): A picture tile (16:9 or 21:9, 1370px wide at 1440) sits inset by the page margin on each side with the dealt corner as the section arrives. Over the next 650-720px of scroll (while it is sticky, or as its top travels from 90 to 30 percent of the viewport) it widens to the full 1440px: clip-path inset(0 var(--margin) round var(--radius)) to inset(0 0 round 0), the height holding or growing from 80svh to 100svh. The corner eases to 0 so it ends edge to edge. Text for the section sits below the tile and is simply there (the dealt entrance decides whether it arrives); no caption sits on the picture. Phone: inset by the page margin with the dealt corner, widening to the full 390 over 60 percent of the scroll distance. House rules for it: A picture tile in the dealt corner, no outline, no caption box on the photograph; the pin, if any, is under two screens. Nothing of it pins: where it names a hold or a sticky layer, it runs over the same plain scroll. On one picture or group, once on the page, on native scroll; still for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, tested and ready to fill. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 2.8 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Section seam bends into a cup that gathers the scattered words (`seam-pocket-gathers-the-words`) — the hand-over into the list section
The stage holds a section's heading and sentence across the top, with eight to twelve short words (what the section was about, one or two words each) lying scattered under them as small plain type, each at its own tilt, over a band of the next section's dark ground with a low swell in its middle. The scroll carries the words one by one, in staggered windows, to their places in two short columns; as each row is about to be filled, the middle of the seam rises in a wide, shallow, round-shouldered cup (at least half the stage, smootherstep sides, no corner anywhere) just far enough to take it, and each word straightens, grows to display size (48px or more on desktop) and turns to the next ground's light ink exactly where it crosses the edge. The edge's feet lift through the hold, so by the end the dark ground holds most of the screen and the gathered words rest in it as the next section's opening; the stage lets go onto that section on the same ground. The first frame already moves. Fully scrubbed: scrolling back scatters the words exactly as they were. No pictures, no physics, flat colour, no box round the words. Hold: 1.4 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: Between two sections, on the section whose subject is a set of short names (what a farm grows, what a studio makes, the services, the rooms, the subjects of a course), handing over to the next section, which starts on the inverted ground (the page's ink) with no margin: an order or booking section, the offer, the contact. Once per page; a quiet section before it.
Content: A section heading of 2-5 words (two lines at most at every width) and one sentence of 30 words at most. Eight to twelve real names that sum the section up, one or two words each, 14 characters at most (longer labels make the columns too wide for the cup), in reading order: the first half reads down the left column, the rest down the right; the first six to eight are the ones a phone keeps. Nothing else in the scene: no pictures, no pills, no boxes, no icons. The next section continues on --spg-next (the ink by default).
Options (data-options): hold 1.4 (1-1.5); phoneHold 1.2 (1-1.5); maxWords 12 (8-12) words used on desktop and tablet; phoneCount 7 (6-8) the first items kept on a phone, the rest hidden there in the finished composition too; columns 2 (1-2) on desktop and tablet, a phone takes one; at 0.5 (0.3-0.7) where the cup stands, its middle as a share of the width (a phone keeps it centred); feet 0.6 (0-0.85) how far the edge's feet rise by the end, as a share of the way to the cup's final top, phoneFeet 0.72; scale 0.6 (0.45-0.85) a scattered word's size as a share of its landed size, phoneScale 0.64; tilt 9 (0-14) the largest tilt of a scattered word; flight 0.2 (0.15-0.3, 0.17 at most on a phone) share of the hold each word takes to its slot; seed 3 the scatter's seed (the script tries the next seeds until the whole flight is clear); scrub 0.5, true with Lenis or on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. CSS tokens on the section: --spg-next, --spg-next-ink, --spg-title-size, --spg-lede-size, --spg-word-size, --spg-word-lh, --spg-col-gap, --spg-gutter, --spg-foot, --spg-at, and for the finished composition --spg-rise, --spg-a, --spg-sw.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/seam-pocket-gathers-the-words.js` and `seam-pocket-gathers-the-words.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/seam-pocket-gathers-the-words.html`. On a phone: The same scene with phone numbers: the first seven words (phoneCount) in one centred column at about 37px, the heading block across the width with the words scattered under it, the cup in the middle and never moving sideways (its curved sides run out past the screen, so they stay soft), flights of 0.17 and a 1.2-screen hold. Screens under 600px tall, reduced motion, no script and a failed CDN get the finished composition: heading and sentence, then the next ground with the cup risen at its top and the words resting in it in the light ink.

### Letter physics (`letter-physics`) — carries the about section
A bold claim stands readable on a dark stage, breathes in, then bursts apart in true 3D, every letter flying off on its own path with spin and a little gravity and fading before the screen's edge can cut it, while a short answer comes out of depth from its centre, whole at every frame, and lands in paper on the dark at the claim's own size, as the picture behind comes up like a room's lights switched on. It opens a chapter with a claim of 12-36 characters and needs no picture. Hold: 1.4 screens. Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: In the middle, as the opening of a chapter (the intro, the about, the work, the features): the claim is its heading and the answer its first sentence. Not for the first screen, where the answer would arrive only halfway through the scroll.
Content: One claim of 12-36 characters (40 at most), fitted to two lines; one answer of about the claim's length (16-36 characters, six words or fewer, never longer) that lands at the claim's size in two lines (a longer answer pulls the claim down with it, or set match false and keep the older smaller answer); two optional side labels of 1-3 words in sentence case. Optional: one dark photograph of a lit building or room, 2400x1600 or more, dark above its middle where the answer lands and its subject below, with a crop for phones. Two colours: a near-black ground and the paper of the claim and the answer. On a 390px phone a claim over 30 characters sets below 40px: keep it short.
Options (data-options): hold 1.4 (1.4-2.4); phoneHold 1.2 (1.2-2.2); phone 'same' | 'rest'; scrub 0.8, true with Lenis; exit 'burst' (3D) | 'detonate' (flat, wider, fast spin) | 'crumble' (the letters drop away in place, the middle first); spread 0.36 (0.3-0.45) burst radius; depth 520 (380-540) px the centre letters come nearest; gravity 0.22 (0.12-0.3); light 'none' (no disc: the answer comes out of depth over 0.22-0.80 and lands at the claim's size, then the picture comes up to full over 0.80-0.86; deal only this) | 'white' | 'warm' | any CSS colour (the older feathered disc, which reads as a glow); answer 'whole' (one paragraph out of depth, whole at every frame) | 'letters' (the older letter-by-letter flight); match true (the answer at the claim's size, in two lines) | false (the older --lp-b-size, up to 64px); disc 1.1 and phoneDisc 1.15 (light only); payoff 'land' | 'pass' (only when the next section continues the sentence); intro false (true gathers the letters on load when the scene opens the page); particles 'auto' | 'chars' | 'words'; seed 0.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/letter-physics.js` and `letter-physics.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/letter-physics.html`. On a phone: The same burst with phone numbers: the claim at 13.5vw on up to two lines, the labels hidden, words as the particles for a long claim or a slow device, the answer at the claim's size in two lines; hold 1.2.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Scale. The size of the name against the smallness of everything else is the entire visual event, so protect both ends: do not shrink the name to make room, and do not let body text creep above 20px.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
A long name set small is just a logo. If the name has more than about 12 characters, stack it on two lines, every letter whole; never drop below 12vw. Do not add a second big headline under the name. Do not decorate the letters.

## Always

no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
