## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`menu-button`):** Minimal: the wordmark at the left margin, and at the right margin a Menu text button beside the one action, both 44-48px tall, at every width. Menu opens a full-screen panel on the ink: the section names as links at the h2 step stacked on the left, the action, the email and the address or hours on the right, Close where Menu was; it slides down in 320ms. The 72px bar's ground scrolls away with the hero while the wordmark and the two buttons never leave: they stay fixed in their corners, each on a solid tab of the ground. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as the links of the Menu panel, and the one action. **Must show:** At every width only the wordmark and a Menu button beside the action; Menu opens a full-screen panel on the ink with the section names at the h2 step.
- **Footer (`statement-and-row`):** A closing statement: one line of five to eight words (the action as a statement) at the display step held to 6vw at most, two lines at most, with the button 32px under it, on the ground; then 96px of space and one band of the surface tone, 120-160px tall, with the links on the left, the contact in the middle, the © line and Image credits on the right. As tall as its content, about 60svh, so the statement, its button and the band share one screen. The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). Never the name or the email at display size, never a credit wall (the credits sit behind one small "Image credits" link that opens a dialog), never a list the page already shows as content (the menu, the rooms). Every link goes to this page, the client's own pages or the client's own accounts, never to another company's product (a footer that sent six of ten links to other companies' apps read as their advert). Its closing line is 6vw at most (`min(var(--step-display), 6vw)`; `giant-footer` fails 7vw), two lines at most, on one screen with its button and links (`lone-heading`); the footer is as tall as its content, and nothing in it pins, holds or reveals word by word on scroll (`held-dead-screen`, `line-holds`). **Must show:** One line at the display step (6vw at most) with its button on the ground, then 96px of space and one band of the surface tone with the links, contact and legal line, all on one screen.
- **Cards (`picture-top`):** Picture-led cards: the picture runs to the card's top and side edges at 4:3, the words under it on the card's surface tone with 24-28px padding: the name at the h4 step, one line of facts, the price or the link. Three to a row on desktop (two for rooms), 24px gaps; the corner on the card, the picture clipped by it; hover scales the picture 3% inside the card. On a phone one to a row. Cards group what belongs together and give the page its hierarchy: use them in two or three sections (the things a visitor compares or chooses between, the facts that belong together) and let the rest stand on the ground. One card style on the page. Filled, never outlined; no shadow at rest; no icon on top; never a card inside a card (a product window on a card is one), never a card laid half or more over a photograph, never more than one product window on the first screen, never a page built only of cards (`framed-elements`, `boxed-list`, `boxed-page`); the hero headline never in a card. **Must show:** The picture runs to the card's top and side edges at 4:3, the words under it on the card's fill.
- **Buttons (`big-block`):** Big blocks: square, 72px tall, 280px wide or more, the text face at the h4 step, weight 600, filled with the accent, the label at the left; hover darkens the fill. For the one action of a confident page. The second action is a text link at the same size. One button family on the whole page: this button, and at most one quieter sibling for the second action (the label alone with this button's arrow where it has one, or the same shape in the surface tone). The family is its shape, fill, type and hover, and every button on the page takes them: the navigation's action, the hero, the sections, the cards, the forms and the footer. It keeps the size written here in the hero, the sections and the footer; in the navigation, on a card and in a form it takes the height that part gives it (40-48px where it names none). Where a hero, section, card, footer or navigation above or below names a button of its own (a pill, a play disc, a 64px block, a split button), build this one in its place (`button-family` fails three unrelated shapes). The primary action takes it on the first screen (a real button, 44px or taller). Sentence case, no tracked capitals, no gradient, no shadow, no outline as the only shape. Where the brand has its own button, the brand's wins (say so in the report). One action once a screen, the bar's included (`duplicate-action` fails two buttons, or a button and a link, to one place on one screen): the bar's action and the hero's button go to different places, or the bar's action comes in once the hero's button has scrolled away; a fixed bar's action steps aside while a section's or the footer's button to its place or under its label is on the screen; each card of a group sends to its own place (a plan's sign-up with that plan chosen). **Must show:** Square blocks in the accent, 72px tall and 280px wide or more in the hero and the sections, the label at the h4 step at the left.
- **Corners:** Square corners on pictures, cards, panels and fields. (`--radius: 0`; buttons as dealt above).
- **Space:** Standard: 16-20vh between sections (about 144-180px at 1440 by 900, 96-120px on a phone), page margins 32-48px at 1440 and 20px on a phone. The space between sections counts from the last content of one to the first content of the next, and a change of ground never shortens it (`section-air`). **Nothing runs into one screen edge:** whatever is not full-bleed keeps the page margin on both sides at rest; a product window that ran into the right edge while the left margin held failed a notes page (`edge-run`).
- **Pictures:** Pictures in natural colour, as shot, with matching light; no filters, no tints, no grain. Never greyed: no grayscale filter, and no stand-in whose file is black-and-white (the client's own pictures as they are).

## 1. The idea
Present what is on offer the way a field guide presents birds: each item alone on the same plain field, same scale, same angle, named plainly. A bakery's pastries become specimens and the specimens become the menu. A furniture maker's chairs each stand alone in the same pale frame. The format flatters ordinary objects because it treats each as worth looking at, and it replaces both the feature grid and the product-card grid with something that has a point of view.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
Hero: a left-aligned statement of what this is (5–7vw, upper-left) beside or above a tight cluster of four to seven specimen tiles of different sizes arranged as a loose mosaic that ends on the right margin. Each tile is a square or 4:5 rectangle in one fill, the page's surface one step off the ground, with one photograph centred on it, no border, 8–12px gaps. The main section is the catalogue itself: a grid of tiles in which each item has its name and one fact set beneath the tile in the text face, aligned left to the tile edge. Every tile takes that same fill: the items carry the colour, never the tiles. One tile in every six or seven is double size. Supporting sections (about, how to order) are plain text on the ground colour, kept short.

## 3. Type — Be Vietnam Pro, one sans family
One sans family. A 1.25 scale from a 16px body; the hero's display step is about 5vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Be Vietnam Pro does every job, told apart by size and weight (500 for display, 400 and 600 in text). Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.
```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Be+Vietnam+Pro:wght@400;500;600&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Be Vietnam Pro** — weight 500, sentence case, tracking -0.033em, leading 1
- Text: **Be Vietnam Pro** — weight 400 for running text, 600 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css
:root {
  --step-small: 0.8125rem;
  --step-body: 1.0000rem;
  --step-lead: clamp(1.125rem, 0.675rem + 0.56vw, 1.250rem);
  --step-sub: clamp(1.340rem, 0.804rem + 0.84vw, 1.675rem);
  --step-head: clamp(1.675rem, 1.005rem + 1.25vw, 2.250rem);
  --step-display: clamp(1.890rem, 0.600rem + 4.60vw, 5.500rem);
}
```
`--step-display` is raised above for this page's split hero (section 6): it reaches 76px at 1440, where the type system's own stops at 63px. The other steps stand.
Friendly and plain. Item names at 18–22px in medium weight, facts beneath in the soft ink. One big statement in the hero; no other display type.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-black-grotesk-heads-regular-body`, Black grotesque heads, regular body): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** Faces: one grotesque with 400 and 900 cuts (the true black only; the heaviest loaded weight where the dealt face stops lower). Heads in black (900), mixed case: 100px for the hero and 72px (5vw) for section heads at 1440, leading 0.9-1.0, tracking -0.02em, two lines at most. Body 400 at 16-18px, leading 1.45; buttons keep the dealt button's label. Ratio about 6.3x. The contrast is between black and regular, with nothing in between: no 700 or 800 steps. Keep heads to three or four words. Colour: the dealt ground and ink at full contrast; the accent on the primary action and links only, never a card fill. Motion: headlines arrive with the dealt entrance; links change colour on hover. House rules for it: Clean after the pass: no own button size or weight, entrance as dealt, accent off cards. Take from it how the steps relate (how far they jump, where the big jump falls, which steps carry the page), and its leading, tracking and case where the type system above allows them. The faces, weights and six steps dealt above stand: map its sizes onto the steps, nothing under 13px, running text 16px or more, and no face it names in place of the dealt ones.

Stone ground (#e0e0e0), dark ink, and no accent hue: the action is set in the ink, solid. Colour comes only from the pictures. Two grounds and one band: the page ground and --surface, a clear step from it, and the ink as ground for one section at most. Every other colour on the page comes from photographs or the product, never from added tints, badges or chips.
The items are the colour. The ground stays quiet and every tile takes one fill, the surface a step off the ground; the accent goes on the action only. Tiles in three or four colours read as a wall of boxes and spread the palette (the inspector fails it, tile-wall).
When the client's name names a colour, a material or a light (white, black, ember, slate, linen, fog, in any language), the page's ground follows it; redraw this palette if it contradicts that (SKILL.md step 1).
**Two grounds, no more:** every section stands on `--bg`; cards, panels and form fields take `--surface`, or the ink where section 0 deals ink cards or an ink bar; the footer takes the ground section 0 gives it (`--surface`, the ink or `--bg`, or the band its design names); `--field-1` grounds one section at most, as a band. No other ground or panel colour on the page: no tint between them, no colour per section or per card, no new tone for an overlap, and no card or panel filled with the accent (a pattern above that fills one takes `--surface` or the ink; the accent marks the action). Where the note or the direction above names more grounds, these tokens win (`surface-count`).

### Type sets like wet ink (`lib-0105`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. Display type exists first as swollen, merged ink blobs and sharpens into clean letters line by line as it reaches its place on screen, because a blur is pushed through a hard alpha threshold that relaxes as the blur clears, so words seem to set like wet ink rather than fade in.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** Thin, light or serif faces lose their hairlines in the threshold and settle looking bolder than set. Over photographs or busy grounds the blobs read as dirt. One SVG filter per line is costly: more than four or five moving at once stutters on phones and in some browsers, so attach the filter only while a line moves and remove it at rest. Fonts must load before the lines are split. Reduced motion needs a plain cut to crisp type. Used on every heading of a page it becomes a tic.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a mineral-water brand's manifesto, set four short lines of a heavy round grotesk in one ink on a flat chalk ground, one line per screen of native scroll. Each line condenses out of chunky blobs as it rises from the lower third to the centre, and stays crisp once set, with the blur at 8px at most, so the reader sets the pace line by line; the last line stays crisp with the one button under it. Nothing else on the page melts: every other heading cuts or slides plainly, no accent colour in the lines, no paragraph or drawings around them. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 1 screen of scroll (the research ran it without a pin; on the stage it holds one screen): `hold: 1` in `Stage.scene`, its section 2 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** the same move at phone size (`phone: 'same'`). Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 1, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Rhythm of like objects on one ground and the pleasure of comparing like with like.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
It needs at least six items that can be shown as objects; with three it is a feature row. Keep labels outside the tiles. No shadows under the objects, no hover lift.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen
(100svh), one h1 there that says what this is, 16vh or more between sections, equal outer margins and nothing run into one edge, no
section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
in it; the footer a designed section, never one line. Cards with a fill where things belong together, in the dealt style; never a card
in a card, never a page of nothing but cards. No picture inside a headline or a sentence, no headline in a card, no centred stack of
headline, line, two actions and photograph card, no giant name or email anywhere, no credit wall, no drag or progress bars or range
sliders, no switch that changes nothing, no bento grid, no wall of tiles in several fills, no heading alone on its screen, no drawn
shapes, diagrams or charts outside a product fragment, no documentary or archive photographs, one accent hue. Few words: the first
screen 30, a section 60, the page about 500; running, list and table text 16px or more at 4.5:1. One email form a page, no stats band
of one-word labels, key content in the open, no two sections in a row with the same split, each photograph once, in colour, never blurred.
