## 0. The frame (dealt; these replace whatever a blueprint below says about them)
Pages built by models came out alike in exactly these parts: three button shapes, one plain bar, a one-line footer, text floating with nothing grouping it. Each part below is one of about fifteen tested designs or one of the patterns of the design-anatomy study (great sites read part by part), dealt away from the last pages built; build it as written, at the sizes given. Its **Must show** line is what the built part is checked against: a part that does not show it is not built as dealt. A pattern's id (in code type, here and below) is only its handle for redraws: where the id names something its description leaves out (capitals, an outline, glass, mono, a nested card), the description wins; and where a description makes its content arrive (on load, or as it comes into view: it rises, fades, slides or wipes in), section 8's entrance decides instead, on the first screen and below the fold.
- **Navigation (`chapter-band`):** A band that names where you are: fixed at the top, 56px tall, a solid band of the page ground on every screen. The wordmark at the left margin at the h4 step; at the right margin a Menu text button beside the one action (40px tall); dead centre the name of the section in view at 16px in the ink: the section's one- or two-word name (its link in Menu), never a sentence or a number. When the next section takes the screen the old name slides up out of a 24px mask and the new one slides up in (280ms, ease-out); over the hero the centre is empty. Menu opens the section names full-screen on the ink, with the action and the email. On a phone: a 56px band with the wordmark, Menu and the action; the live name is dropped. Nothing stands above it (no announcement, information or language strip: `announcement-bar` fails it), and every link is one or two words, never a sentence (`nav-sentence` fails a sentence in the bar and any link or button over three words). It sits on the page's margins, on an opaque ground wherever it is fixed. It never hides on the way down and comes back on the way up, and never scrolls away leaving nothing in its place: where the first screen's bar scrolls away, what this design keeps fixed takes over at once (`nav-not-as-dealt` fails a bar that hides). The real sections as the links of the Menu panel, and the one action. **Must show:** The centre of the fixed 56px band shows the name of the section in view and swaps it by sliding the old name up out of a mask.

The footer is a designed section, never a single line: it stands on its own ground (the surface tone or the ink; the page ground where its cards carry the surface tone; the palette's band in the accent only where the design here names it), at least 40% of a screen tall at 1440, its links at body size. **Build it as dealt,** every part and size written here: never cut down to a line of credits or a © row (a portfolio's dealt three cards were built as one credit line); the inspector compares the built footer with this one (`footer-not-as-dealt`). ## 1. The idea
A calm page where the pictures are placed like prints on a gallery wall: each at its own size and position, with text set small and light between them. Nothing lines up in the obvious way, yet everything sits on a grid you can feel. The unevenness is what makes it read as art-directed by a person: a small portrait far left, a wide landscape ending on the right margin, a square set lower than both. It lets photographs lead without ever becoming a slideshow.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
If section 0 deals an interactive piece, decide now what it is, from the subject (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A 12-column grid with wide margins (5–7vw). The hero is a single line of large light type (6–9vw) across the top third, and below it one photograph placed off-centre (columns 6–12, 16:10), its top edge overlapping the baseline zone of the headline by a few pixels of deliberate tension. Each following section places two or three photographs with different spans and vertical offsets: for example a 3:4 portrait in columns 2–5 starting 120px lower than a 16:10 landscape in columns 7–12; then a single small 1:1 in columns 9–11 beside a paragraph in columns 2–6. Paragraphs are short (max 40ch) and sit in the gaps. No two consecutive sections use the same arrangement. Vertical spacing is large and irregular on purpose (120–260px).

## 3. Type — Cabinet Grotesk over Charis SIL
A sans display over a serif text. A 1.333 scale from a 17px body; the hero's display step is about 4.4vw at 1440. Six steps and no others: small, body, lead, sub, head, display. Cabinet Grotesk only for the display and head steps; Charis SIL for everything a visitor reads at length, buttons and navigation included. Load only the weights listed; a weight the face lacks is faked by the browser and the inspector fails it.

```html
<link href="https://fonts.googleapis.com/css2?family=Charis+SIL:wght@400;700&display=swap" rel="stylesheet">
```
- Display: **Cabinet Grotesk** — weight 800, sentence case, tracking -0.039em, leading 0.98
- Text: **Charis SIL** — weight 400 for running text, 700 for emphasis and buttons, leading 1.5, 60-72 characters a line
```css

```
A light or regular weight at large size for the few headings; small, quiet text. Serif or a slender sans. Everything left-aligned to grid lines.
The direction decides sizes and how many of them; the type system decides how this face is set. Where the two give different tracking or leading, use the values of the type system: they are tuned to this face.
These six steps are the whole scale. Map every size a blueprint names onto the nearest step; the hero headline takes `--step-display`, whatever size the hero blueprint gives.
**Type note (`type-tight-sans-serif-figures`, Tight sans with serif figures): how a site of the study set type of this kind.** A neo-grotesque at 500 for all words, display at 11.7vw, over a transitional serif used only for figures. The display is 168px at 1440 (-0.04em), one line of a word without descenders so a leading of 0.7 does not collide; at two lines, leading 0.85. Steps at 54px, 30px, 24px and 18px (lh 1.1-1.4); body 16-18px. Figures in the serif at 72px (lh 1.0): real numbers the page states, such as a price, a year or a quantity, set in the line that says what they count, never in a band of figures. Ratio about 9x from the display to the 18px step. Colour: the dealt ground and ink; the accent only on the action fill, and the figures take the ink, not the accent. **Within the house rules and the adjustments under it, this geometry is binding:** what is large, where the words sit and how the picture is cut, in the dealt type and colour; where section 2 describes the first screen otherwise, this is it. **It fills the first screen alone** (`min-height: 100svh`, the navigation inside it): nothing of the next section shows under it at 1440 by 900 or on a phone, and a part of this blueprint that would run below the fold (a peek of the next section, a row continuing under the fold) is left out. **Its headline stands whole on the first screen:** the `h1` at `--step-display` (section 3), its last line, descenders included, clear of the bottom edge by the page margin at 1440 by 900 and 390 by 844, at rest and through the entrance (`hero-cut`); a name the fold cuts reads as cut off. At most 30 words in it. Its largest type carries the page's own idea (the Headline above says how) or, for a name whose meaning the page is built on (Klee is clover), the name; never a bare category line with nothing of this client in it. **The headline never repeats the logo:** when the name is the headline, the bar shows no second wordmark on the first screen (the mark alone, or the wordmark arriving once the hero has scrolled away); otherwise the headline says something new and the name stays in the bar. One action appears once on this screen, the bar's included: never two buttons to the same place (two Directions buttons on one first screen; "Start free" in the bar and again under the headline). Never the template stack of a centred headline and a line under it over a photograph card with round corners or a row of two or three cards, with or without actions (`template-hero`). Its picture shows what the headline names.
**Two lines read as one name:** a headline or a name set on two lines keeps them tight, line-height 0.9-1.0 (section 3's own where it is tighter) with no margin or gap between them, in the weight section 3 deals for the display step (800), never a lighter cut for the size: a thin two-line name with a gap between its lines read as a bug (references/type.md §3).

Adjusted for this page:
- It sets running, list or table text at 15px: use your body step (16px or more, 15px at the least on a phone). The inspector fails it under 14px and warns under 16px (`small-body`).
- It names colours (black): map them onto this palette's roles (ground `--bg`, panels `--surface` and the fields, text `--ink`/`--muted`, the accent `--accent` on the primary action only).

## 7. Sections, in order
**Words: cut hard.** The first screen 30 words at most, its headline 12 at most; a section 60 words at most outside a list or a table; the whole page about 500 (the inspector warns over each and fails past one and a half times: `wordy-hero`, `hero-words`, `wordy-section`, `wordy-page`). Fewer, shorter sections: drop any that carries no claim, and merge two that say one thing. Award sites carry about 27 words a screen. Say each thing once, as a fact: one heading per idea, never a heading that restates the one above it or a label repeated over every step (`restated-heading`, `repeated-label`).
Each is one example of a shape for this section: keep what is large and what sits beside what, and let the real content decide the
details (sizes from the six steps, colours from section 4, corners from section 0). Re-order or drop a section when the argument of the
page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a page of cards. A section a scene carries (section 9) is that scene.
**The run of sections:** never two in a row with the same picture and text arrangement (picture left, text right twice running is one layout), and at least one that breaks the split grid: a full-bleed picture, a headline across the width, an 8/4 asymmetry (three splits in a row warn, `repeated-split`). The key content stands in the open (specs, loads, rooms, the menu); tabs and accordions only for secondary detail such as the questions. No stats band of big figures over one-word labels: a figure stands in the line that says what it counts (`stats-band` warns). One email form on the page (`duplicate-form` warns). **Cards where things belong together:** in two or three sections the group a visitor compares or reads as one (the plans, the rooms, the visit details, the features) stands in the card style section 0 deals, and no text floats loose where it belongs to a group; never a card inside a card or a window, never outlined boxes in a row, never a page of nothing but cards (`framed-elements`). A control drawn in a product fragment works or reads as static (`dead-control` warns), and every photograph that is not the client's own has its line in the Image credits (`uncredited-picture` warns).

**Structures, in order:** intro the scene, features list, showcase split, cta statement. Build each section in the structure its heading names: a list stays rows, a grid stays a grid of cells, tiles or columns, a split stays two sides; never three on one structure, nor three sections round the same thing (three lists, three rows of cards, three single pictures), and never two alike in a row (`same-structure` warns at three and fails at four).

### 2. features — Rows with margin photographs (list)
Everything hangs from the left margin. First the heading: a statement of up to five words at clamp(40px, 5vw, 80px) on the margin, with the first row 96px under it. Then four or five benefit rows, each starting on the same margin: a title at clamp(28px, 3vw, 48px) and under it a paragraph of 34ch at 17px. Rows are separated by 96–120px of space, not lines. On a saturated field, set all type in one pale ink and leave the photographs ungraded.
On a phone: Everything stays on the left margin. The statement sits at 9vw with the first row 56px under it. Each row stacks its title at 26px, the paragraph, then its photograph at alternating widths: 100%, 70% flush right, 55% flush left. 64px between rows.

- It sets a sentence large over several lines: jurors read that as a long headline. Either cut it to a two-line headline at display size, or keep it a statement at 36px or less on three lines (24px, four lines on a phone).

### 3. showcase — Square plate, spare text (split)
One flat field split 50/50, at least 88svh tall. Left half: a single square photograph filling the half's width minus 40px margins, vertically centred, square corners. On a phone: The square photograph comes first at full width with 16px margins. The tabs sit under it as one row at 17px. Then the title at 28px, the description, and the two action cells stacked, each full width and 60px tall.

Adjusted for this page:
- It fades text: anything a visitor reads, inactive items of a list included, is `--ink` or `--muted` at full strength (contrast 4.5:1 or more). The active item is told apart by `--ink` against `--muted`, weight or the accent, not by fading the rest.
- It hides content behind tabs or an accordion: on a one-page site the key content (specs, loads, rooms, plans, the menu) stands in the open, all of it readable without a click; tabs or an accordion only for secondary detail such as the questions. Set every panel of it in the open, one after another, or take another shape (`--alternatives showcase`).

## 8. Motion — Slow crossfade
The principal photograph quietly changes: three or four images of the same framing crossfade over about 1.6 seconds, holding five to six seconds each, or the fixed image changes when its matching section arrives. It should feel like light shifting in a room. Pause when the tab is hidden; under reduced motion show only the first image.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): the entrance below, the small moves below and feedback on every control; video only as section 0 deals it. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

### Entrance — Already there (`already-there`), dealt
It replaces the old default (headline lines rising out of masks, a clip-path shutter on the picture): build this one and neither of those.
- **What the visitor sees:** Nothing arrives. The first frame is the finished first screen. The only motion is inside the media: a clip that plays, a product that responds to the pointer, a picture that fades in only if it arrived late. The photograph is there. If none of it had painted by the first frame, it waits on --picture-ground and fades in over 320 ms. A dealt clip plays from frame one, with that frame as its poster. Settled by the first frame.
- **Markup:** `<body data-entrance="already-there">`; the first screen is `[data-hero]`, its parts `data-e="headline"`, `data-e="lead"`, `data-e="action"` and `data-e="picture"` (on the element that already crops the picture, with `--picture-ground` sampled from the picture; the img, video, picture or `[data-ui]` is its first child).
- **The hero picture:** paste the snippet from motion.md, "The hero picture", in a `<script>` right after the hero, so a photograph that has not arrived waits on its ground instead of the entrance playing over an empty frame.
- **CSS:** already in `<skill>/assets/base.css` (the `already-there` block under "the entrance"); write none of your own. It needs `<script>document.documentElement.classList.add('js')</script>` in the head and base.js at the end of the body. To watch it run: `<skill>/assets/entrances/demo.html?e=already-there`.
- **Below the fold: Nothing.** Nothing enters below the fold, and no element on the page gets data-reveal. Section content is simply there, and motion comes from the moves the sheet deals, any moving picture and feedback under the hand. Lazy pictures get the same arrival courtesy as the hero: they wait on their ground colour and fade in when they land. That is loading, not a reveal.

### Small moves — one, dealt from the design-anatomy study
A good page has more than one kind of motion. Beyond the signature move and the entrance, build this once, where its line says, on native scroll or under the hand, with transform and opacity, and a reduced-motion fallback that keeps the content where it rests. None makes anything enter below the fold (the entrance's rule stands), none pins, and none replaces feedback on the controls. Each description is the spec: set it in this page's own content, type and colour (its fills take `--surface`, the ink or the accent, never tints of their own: section 4). A move written as rows, tiles or a list of its own goes on a section of this page already built that way, in its structure and with its pictures; where there is none, leave the move out and say so in the report.
- **Mask roll swap** (`mo-text-mask-roll-swap`): When a state changes (a slide, a tab, a disc, a list item) the text that describes it, a title, a price, a date or a one-to-three-word value at the lead, sub or head step, rolls out and the new text rolls in. The old text moves up 100 percent out of a mask that is exactly one line tall plus 0.1em at the bottom so descenders survive, in 300-420ms on cubic-bezier 0.77, 0, 0.18, 1; the new text enters from below 60ms later, words staggered 30ms. Labels beside it slide 12-23px. If the new title wraps to two lines the container animates its height in the same 300ms. The roll follows the direction of navigation (next goes up, previous goes down). It plays only after the visitor's own action, never on load or on scroll. Phone: the same, 260ms. House rules for it: The mask only clips the text itself, no outlined box; whole words roll, never single letters; it runs only after the visitor's own action, so it is not an entrance. On one paragraph or heading of the page, never on every one, and never the hero's headline (the entrance has it); the words read whole at rest and for reduced motion.

## 9. Scroll scenes
The page's big moments: 2 scroll scenes, one tested and ready to fill, and one researched technique to build fresh. They are part of the composition, so build them in step 6 with the section they carry, not as a motion afterthought. Each runs on the shared stage (assets/scenes/stage.js and stage.css, with GSAP and Lenis from the pinned CDN lines in stage.js) and ships its own finished composition for reduced motion and for phones that rest. How to put one on a page, fill it and check it: references/scenes.md. Check them with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` and look at every sheet.
**The length budget.** Together these scenes hold about 3 screens of scroll (a scene's section is its hold plus one screen tall). A scene replaces the content of the section it carries: it is that section, not extra screens in front of it, and it never adds screens of nothing. The whole page still runs about 12-16 screens at 1440 (the inspector's header counts them), so build the other sections to their content and keep each hold to the beats it has.
**The demo is not the content.** Each scene's demo page is a working example for a made-up client: its town, its photographs, its map, its object and its words are placeholders. Every picture, word and drawn thing in your scene comes from this subject (your inventory, SKILL.md step 1): a row of work is this client's own work; the words a scene gathers are what this client really makes or grows; a quote is the client's own words. A scene that still shows the demo's content, or the stand-in a script draws when it is given nothing, is unfinished.

### Scroll trades weight between headline and text (`lib-0192`) — a technique to build fresh, carries the intro section
Not a tested scene: a technique from the research library (`<skill>/library/techniques.json`), dealt so pages stop reusing the same few big moments. It has no files and no demo: you build it, and it is checked like any scene. On a held stage the scroll drives a variable font's weight axis, so one block of type thins from its heaviest cut to a hairline while another thickens the opposite way, and the moment where the two cross becomes the turn of the statement, so the type itself is the thing that changes rather than anything around it.
- **Avoid, first of all, what breaks it:** A static font, or a variable font with a narrow range (200 to 700 reads timid), cannot do it. Thinner letters are narrower and heavier ones wider, so long words or narrow phones re-wrap mid-sweep and the block jumps; keep short lines that fit at the heaviest weight, left-aligned or in a fixed box. Rewriting the weight of long copy on every scroll tick forces relayout that stutters on weak phones. Weight names, if shown, stay sentence case at normal size. Two screens with one statement frustrate visitors who want to move on. Reduced motion jumps to a middle weight.
- **Every frame of the hold is a finished, readable composition,** not only the last: never one strip, card or word alone on an empty screen while the rest waits; never a fan, stack or overlap that hides the texts a visitor needs (a covered card shows only what still reads, or nothing); every text beside its own title, never a screen away from it; every word whole and in its place or clearly on its way to it, never stranded with wide gaps between words; no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns letters turned or off their line at a settled step, `tilted-letters`, and a picture or filled box cut to a sliver by the screen edge mid-hold, `edge-sliver`). **A hold that steps** (statements or pictures in turn): where its steps carry pictures, each step its own picture, never one photograph held under every step; where it needs none (see Needs below), the statements stand on the ground alone, with no photograph added; statements as short headlines at display size (40px or more on desktop, two lines at most, marked up as h2 or h3: the 36px statement rule is for paragraphs), never 30px lines beside a picture; no frame between two steps near empty; and without the pin (reduced motion, tablet, phone) no picture repeats: with one picture, show it once and set the steps beside it (`repeated-picture`). Check the 10% frames of the scenes.mjs sheets one by one: a frame you would not ship as a still is unfinished.
- **The direction to take:** For a bicycle-frame builder: a flat bone ground, one ink, one accent. Two left-aligned lines in a variable grotesque, 'Built heavy.' over 'Ridden light.', at about 9vw on a held stage for about one and a half screens. The scroll drives the first line from weight 100 up to 900 and the second from 900 down to 100, so each line only becomes its own word at the end; at the crossing, both at 500, ground and ink swap in one cut, with no accent in the lines. No film behind, no list of weight names, no number readout, nothing else on the stage. That was written for another client: keep the principle and the manner, and take the subject, the pictures and the words from this page.
- **Hold:** 2 screens of scroll: `hold: 2` in `Stage.scene`, its section 3 screens tall, each beat about half a screen and something new on screen in every one.
- **Needs:** no photographs.
- **On a phone:** its own version, as the direction above says (`phone: { hold, build }` in `Stage.scene`), or `phone: 'rest'` with a finished composition that reads whole; never the desktop move squeezed. Below 600px a list laid over a picture drops into the flow under it, and nothing a visitor reads is clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`). Check it at phone width like the desktop.
- **Build it on the shared stage** as references/scenes.md §8 shows: a file of your own that registers `Scenes['<a short id>'] = (root, opts = {}) => Stage.scene(root, { hold: opts.hold ?? 2, build(tl, { q, phone }) { … } })`, the markup `<section class="scene" data-scene="<that id>">` holding one `.scene-stage`, and its CSS written as the finished composition first, the live layout under `.scene.is-live`. Build it with transforms, clip-path and CSS 3D on the stage's pinned GSAP; where the direction mentions WebGL or a 3D library, take its CSS 3D route. Never copy any site: no layout, picture, word or colour from wherever this was seen; set it in this page's own type, colour, pictures and words. Every rule of references/scenes.md §7 holds (native scroll, readable at rest, no drawn shapes, tracks or particles, no dead frames). Check it with `node <skill>/scripts/scenes.mjs <page>` like any scene and look at every sheet.
- **If it cannot be built honestly** within the usual three look-and-fix rounds, take `--redraw scenes` and say why in your report; it counts toward the same limit of two scene redraws, and the redraw deals tested scenes.

### Letter bar floods the ground (`letter-bar-floods-the-ground`) — the hand-over into the cta section
One short statement holds on a single line in the page's display face with one straight letter (I, l, i, 1 or a T's stem) on the dead centre of the screen; from the first frame the camera zooms on that letter: the whole line scales up round its stem, the letter in its own proportions and the words growing more slowly as they leave past the screen's edges. Once the letter is wider than a third of the screen and taller than it, only its bar is left, a column of ink that keeps widening; the next section's opening statement arrives inside it at about half-way while the outgoing words' edges are still in frame, the column reaches full bleed by 80% and the statement grows to its resting size as the camera lands, so the next ground arrives through the inside of a letter with no seam and no hold of plain colour. A hand-over for pages with no pictures, in any sector. Hold: 1 screen (set `data-options` hold to 1, or the lowest value the scene allows; its default is 1.1). Every frame of the hold must read as a finished layout: no word split into flying letters, no title sliced by a mask, nothing that looks broken halfway (scenes.mjs warns `tilted-letters` and `edge-sliver` at a settled step); its finished composition is no list drawn as boxes (`boxed-list`) and no display heading alone on its screen (`lone-heading`); on a phone a heading set in letters is whole wherever the scroll stops (`letters-mid-reveal`), and below 600px a list laid over the picture drops into the flow under it, never clipped by the stage (`clipped-text`).
Where: Between two sections, as the hand-over from the page's ground to its ink (the intro to the offer, the about to the prices, the work to the contact). The section before ends on the page's ground; the section after continues on the ink straight under the statement the flood ends on, with little top padding. Once per page, with a quiet section before it.
Content: No pictures. A statement of 2-4 words, 12-22 characters, on one line, set at display size (96px or more on a desktop, nearly the full width on a phone), with a straight-stemmed letter near its middle: I, l, i, 1 or a T, never O, S, A or any letter with a bowl or a counter. Long words that push the pivot far from the middle shrink the line (its longer half round the stem sets the size). The next section's opening statement, 4-8 words in two lines at most at every width, and an optional sentence of 12-24 words under it, both in the ground's colour on the ink. One ground and one ink, 7:1 or better both ways; no accent, no second colour on any word.
Options (data-options): hold 1.1 (1-1.5); phoneHold 1 (0.8-1.2); pivot, the letter's index in the statement (spaces counted) or the letter itself (a span.lbf-pivot in the markup wins; else the straight letter nearest the middle); third 0.3 (0.2-0.4) share of the hold by which the stem is a third of the width; half 0.5 (0.4-0.6) share by which it is half the width (the next statement arrives about here); flood 0.8 (0.6-0.8) share by which the column fills the screen; grow 0.5 (0.3-0.8) the words' scale as a power of the letter's; nav '', the selector of a fixed header, and navClass 'is-flooded', which it gets while the ink covers it; scrub 0.5, true with Lenis and on touch; phone 'same' | 'rest'. Tokens: --lbf-size, --lbf-gutter, --lbf-next-size, --lbf-next-measure, --lbf-pad.
Files: `<skill>/assets/scenes/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.js` and `letter-bar-floods-the-ground.css`; a working example (its content is a placeholder): `<skill>/assets/scenes/demo/letter-bar-floods-the-ground.html`. On a phone: The same zoom with phone numbers: the line spans nearly the whole width round its stem (a 12px gutter); the letter stays a letter, ends in view, until it is taller than the screen (about 0.55, near 30 times its size, repainted sharp rather than stretched from a small layer); the stem is a third of the width by 0.3 with the outgoing words' edges in frame; full bleed by 0.8; hold 1. Screens under 480px tall get the finished composition.

Leave a quiet section between two scenes so each one lands. A scene whose content you cannot fill honestly is redrawn (`--redraw scenes`), never faked; scenes are redrawn twice at most, and after that you take one that fits by id or build without one (`--redraw scenes=none`): one honest scene, or none, beats a fifth redraw. A page with scenes is long: keep the navigation, or at least the one action, in reach on every screen (references/layout.md §7).

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Placement. The differing sizes and offsets create movement on a still page.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Uneven is not random: every image edge lands on a column line, and offsets come from a small set of values. On a phone, keep the unevenness with alternating widths (100%, 72% left, 60% right) rather than stacking everything full width.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules or outlined boxes, no gradients,
no shadows at rest, no glass, no cream-and-terracotta, no violet, lavender or periwinkle. The frame: the hero alone on the first screen

section huddled on one side, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section. No bar above the navigation and no sentence
